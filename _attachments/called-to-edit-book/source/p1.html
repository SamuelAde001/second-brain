Start here

There are millions of video editors. Why you?

This book answers that question. It is everything I wish someone had told me when I started, from learning the skill to getting clients who pay you, in the order you will need it.

This is not an editing tutorial. The buttons are free on YouTube; I learned 80 to 90% of what I know about editing there. What YouTube hardly teaches is the career around the skill: what to learn first, how to get seen, how to get clients and how to get paid. That is this book.

Editing is cutting out what is not needed from the raw idea and presenting it in a way the viewer can get it.
My definition of video editing

Video editing is broad. There are movie editors, podcast editors, short-form editors and motion graphics animators. I can't teach you every kind of editing, but I can show you the pathway, and the pathway works whatever kind you do, wherever you live.

@@OP|1|My story|From a soldier with a hobby to an editor paid by clients abroad.|People watch what you do. People watch what you post.@@

I went from zero to being paid by clients abroad, working from Abuja, Nigeria. I didn't get there by talent. I got there by real work, by being seen, and by a lot of mistakes.

{MK}It started as a hobby

In 2020, during COVID, I was still a soldier. I learned editing for myself, for my Minecraft YouTube channel. I had a laptop and wanted free software, so I found DaVinci Resolve. If I had known CapCut then, I might have started there, but I'm glad I started with Resolve. Later I edited for my church.

A few years later I asked myself: what can I do better than just being a soldier? I looked at different skills and picked editing, because I already knew a little.

{MK}The best move I made: working for free

When my mentor opened a YouTube channel, I offered to edit his videos for free. I only knew how to cut. I had spent a whole month's salary on a laptop that was decent but laggy. It was enough.

At first I wasn't paid. Then he gave me NGN 25,000 a month (Nigerian naira) for data and expenses, for about 4 videos a month. I did it for two years, because it was making me better.

{MK}Putting my work out there

My mentor told me to put my work out there. I opened a new Twitter account, posted my work and networked with editors on Twitter and LinkedIn. Seeing their work showed me I had to step up. I locked in for six months, mastered Resolve and learned the business side.

I paid USD 60 out of my salary to join a community that taught the business of editing, not just the skill. That is where I learned that the career around the skill is what makes you succeed, and the psychology behind every edit choice. I asked editors questions, slid into DMs, got rejected and kept learning.

{MK}The first dollar

My first client came to me by DM. He had been watching my posts. He paid USD 20 per short-form video, my first dollar payment. I didn't even know how to turn dollars into naira. The same client went to USD 30, then about USD 60 a video. I worked days in the military and edited at night.

{MK}Going full time

Then I quit the military to edit full time, one of the riskiest things I have ever done. I had some savings and a place to stay in Abuja. My first month paid USD 600, my biggest payout then. The second paid about USD 1,000.

I took on three clients at once and nearly burnt out. That taught me that more clients does not mean you are better. I went after better clients instead. An agency paid me USD 250 per video.

I kept posting. Route Rise reached out to me because they had been watching my work. They are the biggest company I have worked with so far. Through them I have edited for a YouTube channel with 1 million subscribers and a billion-dollar company.

{MK}What it taught me

I'm about four to five years in now, with lots of mistakes and trial and error behind me. I wish I had known two years earlier what I know now. Having worked with the big players, I know that not everything matters. This book is the things that do.

{MKG}Remember

The skill gets you in. How you present it is why you get hired. There are millions of video editors around the globe. The question is: why should they hire you?

  • Video editing is not just a skill. It is a way of passing information and communicating.
  • A lot of people put effects in a video without understanding what purpose they serve.

{MK}Who pays editors

There are three kinds of creators, and they don't pay the same.

The people who pay you are creators, businesses and agencies. An agency is a middleman: one agency may have 20 to 30 clients and employ many editors.

Agencies
Good for beginners
Always have work, and give you clients you could never reach yourself. Agencies really helped my career. But they pay less, because they take a cut, and they give the worst feedback and many revisions, because they are trying to perfect the video so their client will love it.
Creators, businesses and brands, directly
The best
The best pay and the best working relationship. You have to find them and earn their trust, which is what this book is about.

{MK}"But the market is full"

No, it isn't. Some creators have three, four, five video editors. The problem is that most of us are not good enough yet, and we don't position ourselves so the people who need us can find us.

The biggest beginner mistake is learning a lot of skill and never positioning yourself: not using social media to get clients, putting random things in a portfolio and applying for jobs. Applying has its place, but only after you have a strong social media presence and portfolio. Before that, social media is where you build. My own biggest mistake was not putting myself out there.

{MK}AI is not taking your place

Yes, AI can edit. But most people don't even want to spend time asking AI to edit their videos. Your leverage is the skill plus AI in your workflow, so you deliver faster and more easily. The company I work with demands that its professional editors use AI. Clients need editors who are up to date, and creativity is still needed. If you don't know how to use AI in your workflow, you will fall behind. In this age, much of what you learn today can be useless tomorrow, which is why knowing what to learn matters more than ever.

{MKG}Remember

A diamond is as valuable as it is visible. You can't be a video editor without being visible, because video editing is literally a visible skill.
