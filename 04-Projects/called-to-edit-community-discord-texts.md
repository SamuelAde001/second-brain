# Called to Edit Community: Discord texts

Part of [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-community-launch|community launch]] · [[03-Areas/called-to-edit/called-to-edit|Called to Edit]].

What the bot posts in the server. **This note is the source:** edit a section here (Obsidian is fine), then run `python 00-System/scripts/community_admin.py post-texts` and the bot edits its message in place.

How sections work: `## #channel` (or `## #channel 2`) is one pinned message in that channel · `## #forum guidelines` is a forum's guidelines · `## #forum post: Title` is the forum's pinned post. `#name` in the text becomes a channel link, `{SITE}` the site address. Keep each message under 2,000 characters (Discord's limit).

Rewritten 2026-10-22 after Samuel: *"the 60 days challenge is just one of the many activities that would hold in the community"*; the community is *"a place where editors can grow with others, be kept accountable, share resources, share opportunities, share client works, archive of useful tutorials, editing hacks, etc, we would have weekly live calls"*. Rules: his approved list (2026-10-22). Wording: the Brand manager's draft. The morning nuggets are his own words from the [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-teachings|webinar teachings]]; `deploy.sh` builds the bot's list from them.

## #start-here 2

**The rules**

**1. Be kind to one another.** Give honest feedback, and give it with respect.
**2. No fights, no abuse, no insults.** Disagree with the idea, never attack the person.
**3. No scams.** No investment, forex or crypto offers, no fake jobs, no asking anyone for money. If something looks off, tell Samuel.
**4. No selling or self-promotion.** Don't pitch your services, courses or pages to members, here or in their DMs. Jobs for others go in #opportunities.
**5. What's shared here stays here.** Don't share call recordings, the workbook or other members' work outside the community.
**6. English**, so everyone can follow.

The bot removes messages that break these rules. Breaking them on purpose means removal from the community.

## #accountability guidelines

Make one post that's yours: call it "<your name>'s journey", or name your goal. Share your goals and what you did, as often as you like, in that same post. Pick a tag: 60-Day Challenge, Weekly goals or Daily log. Every day you post in your own post counts toward your streak 🔥 (type /streak). Go at your own pace, and cheer each other on.

## #accountability post: 📌 The 60-Day Challenge (start here)

**The 60-Day Challenge** 🗓️
Your first 60 days toward your first client: one task a day, from the workbook attached below.

**When:** the group starts on Sunday, 1 November. Join any time and start your own day 1.
**How:** tap **New Post**, call it "<your name>'s 60 days", pick the tag **60-Day Challenge**, and post your Day 1. Then post what you did each day in that same post.

**The four phases**
1. Set your direction (days 1–7)
2. Learn on real videos (days 8–21)
3. Real people, real work (days 22–35)
4. Go get your first client (days 36–60)

Each morning the bot adds 🔥 to yesterday's posts. Sunday evening, the leaderboard goes up in #wins.

## Morning nuggets

- A diamond is as valuable as it is visible.
- If you are targeting everyone, you have no one.
- Video editing is not a skill, it's a service.
- Business is exchange of value for money, literally.
- Not everyone is your client.
- You don't choose what to learn. What you learn is chosen for you.
- You need to niche into something first before you even pick your skill.
- Focus on where your power can handle.
- Don't learn what you are not going to be able to pitch out to your client.
- Don't just say I'm a video editor.
- Don't learn without implementation.
- You can't learn video editing without actually being in the videos, practicing it.
- Let the tutorials become your entertainment.
- There's a YouTube tutorial for everything you want to do. I promise you.
- YouTube is a mine of raw videos.
- When you can finish a full video from beginning to the end, you're already good enough to charge.
- As soon as possible, transition from practicing to real-world projects.
- My number one way to learn is working on real projects that have real consequences and are for real people.
- Don't tell the person you're learning. Tell them you're building your portfolio.
- Whatever you're paying for a subscription is your capital.
- CapCut is enough to get you paid work.
- Don't spend your video editing money on yourself yet.
- Don't wait till you have all the money to buy the high-end laptops.
- Every cut, every animation that you do needs to have a reason for it to be in the video.
- Whenever the client is saying something and you can show that thing, show it.
- A real thing always beats a fake thing.
- A client will prefer an editor that can do videos that produce more results than a video editor that is doing flashy stuff and the video is not performing.
- In any niche, business guys pay more.
- You have to always make sure you are specific.
- A short form editor can earn more than a long form editor if he knows how to package his stuff well.
- You can't succeed in this industry if you are hidden.
- Showing your work is your leverage.
- Pick one platform and focus on it.
- People trust who they can see.
- I only had 700 followers on my Twitter, but that 700 followers got me the best clients I needed.
- What the client wants to see is your videos, not a flashy website.
- Your practice work is your work. I repeat, your practice work is your work.
- It's their footage, but the edit is yours.
- Always show your work as much as possible.
- You can't rely on only inbound and you can't rely on only outbound.
- Clients are tired of editors that know how to do flashy edits, but they are not reliable.
- Those 200 views, one of them could be your client.
- I can't give you my money if I don't trust you.
- There are a lot of clients looking for people. You are invisible. That's why they can't find you.
- Warm outreach converts the fastest.
- You need to have a list. This is the most important thing for every video editor.
- By the time I get to their DM, I am not really a stranger.
- You need to learn to give out value.
- The more you do research around that person, the better responses you will get.
- People want to hear real human beings.
- As soon as possible, get that client into a video call.
- Don't just give people your price.
- Follow up till they say no.
- Getting clients is a lot of hard work. That's the truth.
- Don't be afraid to get no's. Don't be afraid to get rejections. It's part of the journey.
- Stop collecting single projects.
- One of the most important assets that you have is real testimonials and real results.
- Some of us have worked with people, but we've not told them to give us a testimonial.
- The real people that will give you opportunities are people that know you.
- You can't do video editing alone.
- Social media is meant to be social.
- Video editing is not just a skill. It is a way of passing information and communicating.
- Editing is literally cutting out what is not needed from the raw idea and presenting it in a way that the consumer can get it.
- If they come to your social media page and it is empty, they won't even reply to you.
