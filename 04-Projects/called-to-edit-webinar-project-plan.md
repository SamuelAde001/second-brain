## 5. The webinar: structure and content

**Promise.** "This is not a video editing tutorial." It is the pathway to becoming a professional video editor who earns from editing, on any device (phone or laptop, CapCut or Resolve or anything else). It is built from what helped Samuel himself.

**For:** Nigerian editors, and anyone abroad who wants in: those who want to start but have no plan, and those already learning who don't know how to get clients, position themselves or get paid.

**Format.** Google Meet, recorded locally in OBS as the master copy. Samuel solo. A chat prompt every ten minutes or so, one poll ("phone or laptop?"), and he names people who answer.

**Time budget [Proposed, Samuel to adjust].** His content list is bigger than 2½ hours, so this is a planning budget for staffing, not a script. Q&A can run past 9:30pm for those who stay.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 6:30 to 7:00pm | Doors open: music, welcome slide, chat prompt "Where are you joining from and what do you edit on?" |
| 7:00 to 7:10 | **Opening** |
| 7:10 to 7:45 | **Part 1: Start** (learn, tools and money, niche) |
| 7:45 to 8:05 | **Part 2: Establish yourself** |
| 8:05 to 8:10 | Break and chat |
| 8:10 to 8:40 | **Part 3: Get clients** (the core) |
| 8:40 to 8:55 | **Part 4: Pricing, getting paid, working with clients** |
| 8:55 to 9:05 | **Part 5: Network and grow your income** |
| 9:05 to 9:20 | **Your first 30 days, then the Academy offer** |
| 9:20 to 9:30+ | **Q&A** |

**What each block covers (from Samuel's own topic list, after his 4 October cuts)**

- **Opening:** his story, from the military to editing for a foreign company from Abuja. What tonight is and isn't. The mindset: treat it as a business from day one.
- **Part 1, Start:** what to learn first and how to learn (recreate videos you like). Working for free or low pay as a way to learn. How long until you can charge. Editing tools, pros and cons (phone and CapCut against a laptop). How much money you need to start, and the computer each niche needs. The decisions an editor makes and what should drive them. The niches (talking heads, short-form, YouTube, ads, weddings and events, music videos, corporate), their pros and cons, how to choose.
- **Part 2, Establish yourself:** the profile page and a new Twitter account. Which platform suits which editor (Instagram, YouTube, Twitter, LinkedIn, TikTok). A personal brand as an editor. Portfolio: where to build it, and what to do with no clients yet. Presenting yourself professionally. Showing your work.
- **Part 3, Get clients:** inbound and outbound, where clients are. Cold DMs and emails, Loom video outreach, following up. Value-first: editing someone's video for free. Pitching and showing value. Upwork, Fiverr and job boards: worth it or not. Nigeria against abroad, and why you learn to work for free first.
- **Part 4, Pricing and working with clients:** how to price yourself, and what moves the price. Deposits, contracts and not getting stiffed. Briefs, revisions, deadlines, communication. Turning one job into a testimonial and a referral.
- **Part 5, Network and grow:** networking with other editors and joining editor communities. One-off gigs against recurring clients. Packaging gigs. Add-on services (content strategy, thumbnails, scripting). Using AI to edit faster. Agency: mentioned as possible, not taught. Coaching and teaching once you're good.
- **Close:** a week-by-week first 30 days. The Academy offer. Q&A.

**Samuel's cuts, so you don't re-add them:** the basics of a paid edit, where to get assets and templates, starting on a phone, the live breakdown of a real DM, and getting paid from abroad (Payoneer, Cleva and similar). He added: working for free or low pay as a learning mechanism, and Loom video outreach.

**Still open:** which topics get only a headline (his call), and the final outline. **Samuel's outline is the source of truth.** If it differs from this section, his outline wins. Samuel also has to supply the stories and numbers only he knows: what he learned in what order, his first client, what he paid to start, how he prices.

## 6. How registration, payment and joining work

1. Someone lands on calledtoedit.netlify.app from a post, a DM, a community or an ad.
2. They tap "Get my seat" and fill the form (name, email, phone, WhatsApp number). A row lands in the Sign-ups tab with a source tag.
3. They pay NGN 5,000 on Flutterwave.
4. Flutterwave sends them back to the welcome page. It records the payment (the Paid tick) and opens the WhatsApp group. No payment, no group link.
5. **Joining the Meet [Proposed, Samuel to confirm]:** the PM or an admin adds each payer's email to the Google Calendar event as a guest, daily. The Meet link is never posted publicly. The alternative, posting the link inside the WhatsApp group, is easier but can leak. **[Open]**
6. **Reminders in the WhatsApp group [Proposed]:** 7 days, 1 day, 3 hours, 30 minutes before, and when live.
7. **Closing sales at 7:00pm on 25 October [Decided].** Someone switches the Flutterwave link off and the page to "registration closed". Samuel can publish a page change with one command; agree who is on point that evening.

**Weekly admin check:** match the WhatsApp group members against Flutterwave's transaction list and remove anyone who did not pay. (The group link can be reached by anyone who opens the welcome page address directly.)

**Seat cap.** Samuel's Google plan (Google One AI Pro) is believed to cap Meet at 100 participants. That needs checking in Meet itself. If sign-ups reach about 80, decide on a Workspace upgrade then, not at 101, because it takes setup time. The landing page currently says seats are capped at 100, so it also needs updating if the cap rises.

**Replay.** Payers get a view-only Drive link **[Decided: replay yes]**. Target: within 24 hours **[Proposed]**.
