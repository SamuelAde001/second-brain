## The teaching order, and why

**The teaching order is the editing order.** They learn each principle at the point in a real edit where they'd use it, so the class doubles as the workflow they follow afterwards.

| # | Principle | The question it answers | Why it's here in the order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Editing for your audience** | Who is this for, and what should they feel or do? | Decides every choice after it: speed, music, captions, length. Decide it before you touch the timeline |
| 2 | **Storytelling** | What's the story, and what's the hook? | Once you know the audience, you know what to keep and where the video starts |
| 3 | **Cutting** *(the tool for 2)* | How do I remove what doesn't serve the story? | Storytelling decides, cutting does it. Not on his list, but it's the edit itself, so it stays |
| 4 | **Pacing** | How long does each moment stay? | You can only pace a video that's already cut |
| 5 | **Composition** | What do they see, and where on screen? | Framing, punch-ins, captions, text and B-roll go on a locked cut |
| 6 | **Sound** | What do they hear? Voice, music, levels | Music is chosen for the audience and story, and sits under the final cut |
| 7 | **SFX** | Which moments need a sound? | Last, because effects land on things that already exist: a cut, a text pop, a B-roll |
| 8 | **Export and the phone check** | Is it ready to post? | The finish |

**The filter for everything else.** A topic made the class only if it's in every kind of video, works the same in every app, and a beginner can use it tonight. Said out loud as **"not tonight":** keyframe animation, motion graphics, transition packs, speed ramps, colour grading looks, LUTs, plugins, green screen, a software tour. His rule: *"Don't learn what you are not going to be able to pitch out to your client."*

**How to teach a room on four apps.** Teach the concept, demo it once in the app he's fastest in, say the general name every time (*"this is a split, every app has one"*). The cheat sheet below goes in the chat before class. His old course rule: **say the principle before you touch the mouse.**

**One video, start to finish.** The class follows one raw clip from raw to exported, principle by principle. Beginners learn "nuggets" and never finish a video ([[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-02-learning-the-skill|Learning the skill]]); this shows them the whole road once.

**Depth: an introduction, honestly.** Seven principles in 60 minutes is 5 to 9 minutes each. Each block gives the one idea, one demo and one trap, enough to use tonight. Each one can be its own deeper class on Sundays and Wednesdays.

## Run-of-show (60 minutes)

Each block: **say it → show it on the clip → the trap**. If you're behind, the blocks marked *(can shrink)* give back time.

### 0:00–0:03 · Open: before and after
- Play 20 seconds of the **raw** clip, then the **finished** one. *"By the end of tonight you'll know every step between these two."*
- *"Which app are you on? Type it in the chat."* Point them to the cheat sheet.

### 0:03–0:06 · The one idea under everything
- **The gun** (Call 2026-11-04): *"If I give you a gun and tell you how the gun works, does that mean you are a good shooter?"* The app is the gun. Tonight is the shooting.
- **The one rule:** every cut, sound, text and B-roll does one of two jobs: **help the viewer understand** or **help the viewer feel**. If it does neither, it goes. ([[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-04-editing-decisions|Editing decisions]])
- Put **the 8 steps** on screen (the table above). *"Tonight we go through them in this order, on this one video."*

### 0:06–0:11 · 1. Editing for your audience
*"You're not editing for yourself. You're editing for the person who will watch it."*
- **Three questions before you open the app:**
  1. **Who** is watching? (age, what they already know, what they care about)
  2. **Where** will they watch it? (TikTok, Reels, YouTube, WhatsApp status: each moves at its own speed)
  3. **What should they feel or do at the end?** (learn something, laugh, trust the person, buy)
- **The answers decide the edit:** a business owner on LinkedIn and a teenager on TikTok watching the same clip need a different speed, music, caption style and length.
- **The client's goal wins** (his teaching): a flashy video can get views and no sales; a simple, clear one can sell. *"Every video has a goal."*
- **Demo:** answer the three questions for tonight's clip out loud, and write them at the top of the screen.
- **Trap:** editing in the style *you* like, or copying a viral style that doesn't fit who's watching.

### 0:11–0:19 · 2. Storytelling
*"Every video is a story, even a 30-second one."*
- **The four parts:**
  1. **Hook** (first 1 to 3 seconds): why should I keep watching? A question, a bold line, the result first.
  2. **Setup:** the problem or the situation, as short as possible.
  3. **Payoff:** the point, the answer, the moment. What they came for.
  4. **Ending:** land it. One last line, or what to do next. Don't let it trail off.
- **The editor finds the story in the raw.** You don't have to keep the order it was filmed in. **Move the strongest line to the front** as the hook.
- **Open a question, answer it later.** That gap keeps people watching.
- **Emotion first.** The best-known rule for choosing a cut (Walter Murch) ranks emotion above story above rhythm: when unsure, keep what makes them feel.
- **Demo:** find the strongest line in the middle of the clip, move it to the start. Play both versions.
- **Trap:** starting with "Hi guys, welcome back" while the viewer scrolls away; keeping a part because you like it, not because the story needs it.

### 0:19–0:28 · 3. Cutting: how the story gets made
*"Storytelling decides what stays. Cutting is how you remove the rest."*
- **What to cut:** the slow start · false starts (**keep the best take, usually the last**) · "ums", long pauses, dead air · the same idea said twice · anything that doesn't serve the story or the audience.
- **The three moves, same in every app:** **split** at the playhead → **delete** → **close the gap**. Cut at the end of a thought or breath, never mid-word. Listen to every cut.
- **Jump cuts:** normal in talking heads. To smooth one, **punch in** (scale the next clip to 110 to 120%) or cover it with B-roll (block 5).
- **Demo in passes:** false starts and dead air out, play. Repeats out, play. Gaps tightened, play. Watch 90 seconds become about 45.
- **Trap:** cutting every breath until it sounds like a machine. Leave a beat after an important line.

### 0:28–0:34 · 4. Pacing *(can shrink)*
*"Pacing isn't how fast you cut. It's how fast new information arrives."*
- A shot stays until it has done its job, then it goes.
- **Change the rhythm.** One speed all the way through goes numb. Move fast through the setup; **slow down on the payoff** so it lands.
- **The audience and platform set the speed** (back to block 1).
- **The test:** watch it at normal speed without stopping. **The moment you get bored, the viewer left a second earlier. Cut there.**
- **Trap:** cutting fast to hide weak content. It just makes weak content frantic.

### 0:34–0:41 · 5. Composition: what they see and where
*"Composition is where you put things in the frame so the eye goes where you want."*
- **One focus per frame.** The viewer should know where to look in half a second.
- **Faces:** eyes around the top third of the frame, a little space above the head, not cut off at the chin.
- **Punch-ins:** when you zoom in, **keep the eyes in the same place** on screen, so the cut feels smooth instead of jumpy.
- **Reframing for vertical:** a horizontal clip in a vertical video: scale it to fill and move it so the subject stays in the frame. **Demo** with the horizontal B-roll clip.
- **Safe zones:** captions and text **never over the face**, and never at the very bottom or right edge where the app's buttons and username sit.
- **Captions** (most people watch on mute): auto-captions, then **fix the mistakes**; **1 to 4 words at a time**; big, bold, high contrast.
- **B-roll shows what's being said:** *"Whenever the client is saying something and you can show that thing, show it."* *"A real thing always beats a fake thing."* **Demo:** one B-roll over the line it matches, covering a jump cut.
- **Match the clips:** if one clip is darker or more orange, fix brightness and warmth so they look like one video. That's all tonight.
- **Trap:** captions covering the mouth; text everywhere so nothing stands out; random B-roll *"just to make it look like it is being edited"*.

### 0:41–0:48 · 6. Sound
*"People forgive a bad picture. They don't forgive bad sound."*
- **The order of importance:** **voice** on top, always · **music** underneath · effects (next block).
- **Voice:** clear and steady. If one part is quieter, raise it so the whole video is even.
- **Music for the audience and the feeling** (blocks 1 and 2): calm for teaching, energy for hype, soft for a story. Music that fights the mood breaks the video.
- **Ducking:** music lower while the voice speaks, a bit up in the gaps.
- **Rule-of-thumb levels:** voice peaking around −6 dB, music under it around −20 dB or lower. **Never in the red.** Then trust your ears.
- **Silence is a tool:** cut the music right before the big line and it hits harder.
- **Demo:** voice first, drop the music in too loud on purpose, play it, fix it. **Play it on your phone speaker**, where the viewers hear it.
- **Trap:** music louder than the voice, the number one beginner mistake.

### 0:48–0:53 · 7. SFX *(can shrink)*
*"Sound effects are punctuation. Use them like commas, not like a whole sentence."*
- **The five a beginner needs:**
  - **whoosh:** something moves or comes in
  - **pop or click:** text appears, a screenshot, a button
  - **impact or hit:** a reveal, a big number, an emphasis
  - **riser:** builds up right before a reveal
  - **ambience:** the sound of the place (crowd, street, keyboard), so B-roll feels real
- **Match what's on screen** (his teaching): a car sound needs something car-related on screen.
- **Every effect needs a reason** (understand or feel). **One with a reason beats five without.**
- Effects sit **under the voice**, never over the words.
- **Demo:** add a pop on a text, a whoosh on the B-roll coming in, an impact on the payoff line. Play it.
- **Trap:** the same whoosh on every single cut; effects so loud the voice gets lost.

### 0:53–0:55 · 8. Export and the phone check
- **Vertical (Reels, TikTok, Shorts): 1080 × 1920. YouTube: 1920 × 1080.** Frame rate the same as you filmed (usually 30).
- **Watch the exported file on your phone, start to finish, before you post or send it.**
- **Trap:** black bars from the wrong frame size; low-quality export to save space.

### 0:55–1:00 · Homework and questions
**Your first full edit, all 8 steps.**
1. Record yourself on your phone for 60 to 90 seconds: *"Why I want to become a video editor."*
2. **Before editing, write the three audience answers** (who, where, feel or do).
3. Edit it in the 8 steps, in order.
4. Post it in **#feedback** with your three answers and one line: which app, and the part you found hardest.
- He gives feedback on members' edits (Call 2026-11-01). *"Don't learn without implementation."*
- Stuck on a button? Search YouTube for **that one task in your app** (*"how to duck music in CapCut"*), not "how to edit". Starter tutorials for each app are in #resources.

Questions until the hour. Close on what's next (Sunday and Wednesday calls).

## The cheat sheet (paste into Discord before class)

> **Tonight's 8 steps, in order:** Audience → Story → Cut → Pace → Composition → Sound → SFX → Export and watch on your phone
>
> **The one rule:** every cut, sound, text and B-roll must help the viewer *understand* or *feel*. If it does neither, delete it.
>
> **Before you edit, answer:** Who is watching? Where will they watch it? What should they feel or do at the end?
>
> **Where the basic tools are**
>
> | Do this | CapCut (phone) | CapCut (PC) | Premiere Pro | DaVinci Resolve |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Split at playhead | tap clip → **Split** | **Ctrl+B** | **Ctrl+K** (or Razor, **C**) | **Ctrl+B** (or Blade, **B**) |
> | Delete and close gap | **Delete** (main track closes the gap) | **Delete** (main track magnet on) | **Shift+Delete** (ripple delete) | **Shift+Delete** (ripple delete) |
> | Clip volume | tap clip → **Volume** | Audio panel → **Volume** | Effect Controls → **Volume** | Inspector → **Volume** |
> | Volume keyframe (ducking) | keyframe ◇ button | keyframe ◇ next to Volume | **Ctrl+click** the volume line | **Alt+click** the volume line |
> | Punch in / reframe | pinch and drag the preview | **Scale** and **Position** in Video panel | Effect Controls → **Scale**, **Position** | Inspector → **Zoom**, **Position** |
> | Auto captions | **Text → Auto captions** | **Captions → Auto captions** | **Text panel → Transcribe → Create captions** | **Timeline → Create Subtitles from Audio** (Studio) |
> | Export | export arrow, top right | **Export**, top right | **Ctrl+M** | **Deliver** page |
>
> Buttons move between versions. If yours isn't there, search YouTube for *that task + your app*.
>
> **Story:** hook in the first 1–3 seconds · setup short · payoff · land the ending.
> **Composition:** one focus per frame · eyes near the top third · captions never over the face or under the app's buttons.
> **Sound:** voice loudest · music under it (around −20 dB) · never in the red · check on your phone speaker.
> **SFX:** whoosh = movement · pop = text · impact = reveal · riser = build-up · only with a reason.
> **Export:** vertical 1080×1920 · YouTube 1920×1080 · same frame rate you filmed.

*Check before posting:* the cheat sheet's app paths are from general knowledge plus the research below, not tested on each app. Some CapCut features (auto captions in some regions) may need CapCut Pro; Resolve's subtitles from audio needs Studio. A member on each app can confirm in the chat.

## If he has 5 minutes less (Discord trouble)

Shrink in this order: pacing to the boredom test only → SFX to "the five types and one demo" → composition to safe zones and captions. **Never cut audience, storytelling, cutting or sound**: they're what make a beginner's video watchable.
