The first beginners-only skill class of the Called to Edit Community, on Discord in live-call. Announced 2026-11-05 ([[03-Areas/called-to-edit/called-to-edit-log|log]]). Area: [[03-Areas/called-to-edit/called-to-edit|Called to Edit]].

The skill, not the business. *"I can't teach all that but I want to cover the basics"*, things *"actually helpful and not random things they won't use"*, that help *"in every editing they do"*.

## The design: why these things and not others

**The filter.** A beginner topic made the class only if it passes all three:
1. It's in **every** video they will ever edit (talking head, podcast, event recap, advert, vlog).
2. It works the **same in every app**. *"Cutting a video is cutting a video"* (Call 2026-11-01).
3. A beginner can **use it tonight** on a phone.

**What passes:** the order you edit in · cutting · pacing · sound (voice, music, effects) · text and captions · B-roll with a reason · export. That's the whole class.

**What fails, and is said out loud as "not tonight":** keyframe animation, motion graphics, transitions packs, speed ramps, colour grading looks, LUTs, plugins, green screen, a button-by-button software tour. Each is either niche (it depends on the kind of video, so it goes on their learning list) or software-specific (YouTube and #resources already teach it). This is his own rule: *"Don't learn what you are not going to be able to pitch out to your client."*

**How to teach a room on four different apps.** Teach the **concept**, demo it **once** in whatever app he's fastest in, and say the general name each time (*"this is a split, every app has one"*). The cheat sheet below says where each one lives in all four apps, posted in the chat before the class so nobody hunts for a button while he's teaching pacing. His old course rule: **say the principle before you touch the mouse.**

**One video, start to finish.** The whole class edits **one raw clip** from raw to exported, step by step. Beginners learn "nuggets" and never finish a video (his words, [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-02-learning-the-skill|Learning the skill]]); this shows them the whole road once.

## Run-of-show (60 minutes)

Each block: **say it → show it → the trap**. Times are targets. If you're behind, the two blocks marked *(can shrink)* give back time.

### 0:00–0:04 · Open: before and after
- Play the **raw** clip (just 20 to 30 seconds of it), then the **finished** one. *"By the end of tonight you'll know every step between these two."*
- Ask in chat: *"Which app are you on? Type it."* (So you know the room. Point them to the cheat sheet.)
- Set the frame: *"Tonight is the basics that are in every edit, whatever app, whatever kind of video. Not fancy stuff."*

### 0:04–0:09 · The one idea under everything
- **The gun** (Call 2026-11-04): *"If I give you a gun and tell you how the gun works, does that mean you are a good shooter?"* The app is the gun. Tonight is the shooting.
- **The one rule of every edit:** every cut, sound, text and B-roll must do one of two jobs: **help the viewer understand** or **help the viewer feel**. If it does neither, it goes. (His own rule, [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-04-editing-decisions|Editing decisions]].)
- **Editing in one sentence:** deciding what the viewer sees, what they hear, and for how long.
- Trap: adding things *"just to make it look like it is being edited"*.

### 0:09–0:13 · The order you edit in
Put this on screen; they screenshot it.

**1. Cut → 2. Pace → 3. Sound → 4. Text and captions → 5. B-roll → 6. Fix the look → 7. Export → 8. Watch it on your phone**

- **Why the order matters:** if you add text, effects or music before the cut is final, every cut you make after breaks them and you redo the work. Beginners start with the fun part (effects) and never finish.
- Trap: spending an hour on one animation in a video that isn't cut yet.

### 0:13–0:28 · Cutting (the biggest block: this is the edit)
*"The cut is the edit. Everything else is decoration on top."*

**What to cut** (demo each on the raw clip):
- the slow start: cut everything before the first interesting line; **start where it gets interesting**. Delete "Hi guys, so today...".
- false starts and restarts: **keep the best take, which is usually the last one**.
- "ums", "you know", long pauses, dead air.
- the same idea said twice: keep the better version.
- anything that doesn't help the video's goal, even if it was hard to film.

**How to cut** (show the three moves, they're the same in every app):
1. **Split** at the playhead.
2. **Delete** the bad part.
3. **Close the gap** (ripple delete; CapCut's main track does it on its own).
- Cut at the **end of a thought or breath**, not in the middle of a word. Listen to every cut.

**Jump cuts and how to hide them.** When you cut out a pause, the face jumps. In talking heads that's normal and fine. To smooth it:
- **punch in**: scale the next clip to about 110 to 120% so it reads as a new angle, then back.
- or **cover the cut with B-roll** (block 0:46).

**Demo in three passes** (from his old course lesson 06): pass 1 remove false starts and dead air, play it. Pass 2 remove repeats and weak sentences, play it. Pass 3 tighten the gaps, play it. Show the time go from about 90 to about 45 seconds.

**The trap:** cutting every breath until it sounds like a machine. Leave a beat after an important line.

### 0:28–0:34 · Pacing *(can shrink)*
*"Pacing isn't how fast you cut. It's how fast new information arrives."*
- A shot stays on screen until it has done its job, then it goes.
- **Change the rhythm.** Same speed all the way through goes numb, fast or slow. Speed up through the setup; **slow down on the point, the punchline, the emotional line** so it lands.
- **Platform sets the speed:** TikTok and Reels faster, YouTube long form more room (his teaching, [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-04-editing-decisions|Editing decisions]]).
- **The test anyone can do:** watch it back at normal speed without stopping. **The moment you get bored, the viewer left a second earlier. Cut there.**
- Trap: cutting fast to hide weak content. It just makes weak content frantic.

### 0:34–0:46 · Sound (half of the video)
*"People forgive a bad picture. They don't forgive bad sound."* They scroll away from audio they can't hear or that hurts.

**Three layers, in this order of importance:**
1. **Voice** on top, always. Clear and steady: if one part is quieter, raise it.
2. **Music** underneath. Choose it for the **feeling** of the video (understand or feel, again). Lower it until the words are easy to follow; lower still while he talks, a bit up when he stops. That's **ducking**.
3. **Sound effects** for punctuation only: a whoosh when something moves, a pop when text appears, a click on a screenshot. **One with a reason beats five without.**

**Rule-of-thumb levels** (a starting point, then trust your ears): voice peaking around −6 dB, music under the voice around −20 dB or lower, effects in between. **Never let the meter hit red.**

**Demo:** set voice first, drop in music too loud on purpose, play it, then fix it. Add two effects. **Play the mix on your phone speaker**: that's where their viewers hear it.

**The traps:** music louder than the voice (the number one beginner mistake), the same whoosh on every cut, music that fights the mood.

### 0:46–0:53 · What they see on top: captions, text, B-roll *(can shrink)*
**Captions** (most people watch on mute):
- Auto-captions are fine, then **read them and fix the mistakes**, especially names.
- **Short: 1 to 4 words on screen at a time.** Big, bold, high contrast (white with an outline or shadow).
- Keep them in the **middle area**, away from the bottom and right edge where the app's buttons and username cover them.

**Text on screen:** only for the key word or the number you want remembered. If everything is highlighted, nothing is.

**B-roll: show, don't tell.** *"Whenever the client is saying something and you can show that thing, show it."* *"A real thing always beats a fake thing."* B-roll goes over what is being said, not randomly. Demo: put one B-roll clip over a jump cut and over the line it matches.

**Transitions:** a straight cut is the default and it's what professionals use most. A transition needs a reason, like any other element.

**Fix the look** (30 seconds, not a grading lesson): if a clip is too dark or too orange, fix brightness and warmth so all clips match. That's all tonight.

### 0:53–0:55 · Export and the phone check
- **Vertical (Reels, TikTok, Shorts): 1080 × 1920. Horizontal YouTube: 1920 × 1080.** Frame rate: the same as you filmed (usually 30).
- **Watch the exported file on your phone, start to finish, before you post or send it.** Every mistake you didn't see in the app shows up here.
- Trap: black bars from the wrong frame size; exporting at low quality to save space.

### 0:55–1:00 · Homework and questions
**The homework: your first full edit.**
1. Record yourself on your phone for 60 to 90 seconds: *"Why I want to become a video editor."*
2. Edit it in your app with tonight's 8 steps, in order.
3. Post it in **#feedback** with one line: which app, and the part you found hardest.
- He gives feedback on members' edits (Call 2026-11-01). Ties to his rule: *"Don't learn without implementation."*
- If something in your app isn't on the cheat sheet, search YouTube for **that one task in your app** (*"how to duck music in CapCut"*), not "how to edit". The CapCut, Premiere and Resolve starter tutorials are already in #resources.

Questions until the hour. Close on what's next (Sunday and Wednesday calls).

## The cheat sheet (paste into Discord before class)

> **Tonight's 8 steps:** Cut → Pace → Sound → Captions/text → B-roll → Fix the look → Export → Watch on your phone
>
> **The one rule:** every cut, sound, text and B-roll must help the viewer *understand* or *feel*. If it does neither, delete it.
>
> **Where the basic tools are**
>
> | Do this | CapCut (phone) | CapCut (PC) | Premiere Pro | DaVinci Resolve |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Split at playhead | tap clip → **Split** | **Ctrl+B** | **Ctrl+K** (or Razor, **C**) | **Ctrl+B** (or Blade, **B**) |
> | Delete and close gap | **Delete** (main track closes the gap) | **Delete** (main track magnet on) | **Shift+Delete** (ripple delete) | **Shift+Delete** (ripple delete) |
> | Clip volume | tap clip → **Volume** | Audio panel → **Volume** | Effect Controls → **Volume** | Inspector → **Volume** |
> | Volume keyframe (ducking) | keyframe ◇ button | keyframe ◇ next to Volume | **Ctrl+click** the volume line | **Alt+click** the volume line |
> | Punch in (zoom) | pinch the preview | **Scale** in Video panel | Effect Controls → **Scale** | Inspector → **Zoom** |
> | Auto captions | **Text → Auto captions** | **Captions → Auto captions** | **Text panel → Transcribe → Create captions** | **Timeline → Create Subtitles from Audio** (Studio) |
> | Export | export arrow, top right | **Export**, top right | **Ctrl+M** | **Deliver** page |
>
> Buttons move between versions. If yours isn't there, search YouTube for *that task + your app*.
>
> **Sound:** voice loudest · music under it (around −20 dB) · effects only with a reason · never in the red · check on your phone speaker.
> **Export:** vertical 1080×1920 · YouTube 1920×1080 · same frame rate you filmed.

*Check before posting:* the cheat sheet's app paths are from general knowledge plus the research below, not tested on each app tonight. Some CapCut features (auto captions in some regions) may need CapCut Pro; Resolve's subtitles from audio needs Studio. Samuel or a member on each app can confirm in the chat.
