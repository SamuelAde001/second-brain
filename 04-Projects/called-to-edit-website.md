## The concept (Brand manager, 2026-10-26; proposal, not Samuel's decisions)

**One front door, separate rooms.** calledtoedit.com is one site. The homepage is the front door; every offer is its own room with a link that works on its own. Built from the research ([[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|research]]), not taste.

- **Homepage, in this order:** (1) the promise *"I coach video editors to get paid"* with Samuel's photo, one email field for a free guide, and one proof line (78 editors paid for the first webinar · edits for a US company); (2) proof; (3) **"Where are you right now?"**: three doors (still learning DaVinci Resolve → Academy · can edit but no clients → mini webinar, recordings, Community · want Samuel 1:1 → Mentorship); (4) every offer with its naira price, a status (open / waitlist / next date) and one button; (5) four lines about Samuel → /about; (6) FAQ (bank transfer, paying from outside Nigeria, replays, refunds); (7) the free guide again. Why: 57% of attention is on the first screen and 74% on the first two (NN/g); leading creator sites open with one free offer; beginners stall when shown six options at once (choice overload hits unsure buyers).
- **Offer pages share one template:** outcome headline + the price in the button above the fold, three outcomes, proof that matches the offer, who it's for / not for, what's inside, a price box saying how to pay and what happens after, an FAQ in the buyer's words, the button repeated, a sticky price bar on phones, and *"Not ready? Get the free guide"* so nobody leaves empty-handed. Offer pages hide the full menu (logo + one button).
- **Every visitor leaves something:** one-field email forms on every page, a waitlist per Academy cohort, a list for the next mini webinar, an application for mentorship. All of it lands in one place with the page, the offer and consent recorded.
- **Honest urgency only:** deadlines and seat counts come from the server and really change the page when they pass. No reset timers (fined in the UK since April 2025; his buyers compare notes on WhatsApp).
- **VSL:** not in the homepage hero. The evidence is against it there (pages with embedded video converted lower across ~35,000 pages, Unbounce) and it costs his visitors data. Instead: a 60-90 s "who I am and who this is for" video under the hero, 2-4 min on /community, 2-3 min on /academy and /mentorship. All tap-to-play, captioned, with the same points in text under them. Scripts once the pages are up.
- **Look:** his brand guide. Navy hero band in both themes; dark mode = navy/midnight with champagne; light mode = white and cream with bronze for gold text (champagne on white is 1.3:1). Gloock for the big word, Plus Jakarta Sans for reading. Few words: ~300-450 per offer page above the FAQ.
- **Speed:** built for a 360 px Android on slow data: ≤ 400 KB per page before any video, fonts self-hosted with the ₦ sign (Google's font files lack it and add a second download), no third-party scripts before the first tap.

## Payments: three rails (research + fact-checks)

1. **Nigeria: Flutterwave in naira** (bank transfer, card, USSD, Opay), as now. Samuel absorbs the fee (his rule for the community).
2. **Rest of Africa: Flutterwave in the buyer's own currency** (GHS, KES, UGX, ZAR…), which is what makes mobile money (Ghana MoMo, M-Pesa) appear. Charged in naira, foreign buyers can only use a card, and Flutterwave blocks foreign cards until the merchant asks for international payments. **That is the likely reason the Ghana and Zimbabwe buyers failed.** Zimbabwe has no mobile money on any gateway: it takes the USD card route.
3. **Rest of the world, India included: Flutterwave USD card** once international access is on; later a merchant-of-record platform (Polar) for the recordings, and Razorpay's free foreign-business option for India's UPI. A US company (Stripe Atlas) only if India or foreign coaching demand proves itself (USD 500 to set up, then about USD 500 a year plus filings).
- **Big-ticket naira sales (NGN 50,000 and up): Paystack as a second rail** once Called to Edit is registered with CAC: its fee is capped at NGN 2,000, against about NGN 10,750 on Flutterwave for the NGN 500,000 mentorship. Paystack gives only 16 business hours to answer a chargeback, so proof of delivery gets logged automatically.
- **The page picks the rail** from the visitor's country (Netlify knows it), with a visible "Paying from outside Nigeria?" switch. Links never redirect.
- **Fallback for odd cards:** a Selar listing for digital products, linked from the failed-payment screen.

**Samuel's steps in Flutterwave (this week):** (1) open the failed Ghana and Zimbabwe attempts and note the decline messages; (2) Settings → Business Preferences → Payment Methods → request **international payments** (decision within 48 h); (3) set the **fee bearer to merchant** so buyers see NGN 5,000, not NGN 5,107.50; (4) ask support in writing whether this account can charge in GHS, KES, UGX and ZAR with mobile money, and in USD; (5) turn on Apple Pay. CAC business-name registration helps here and unlocks Paystack.
