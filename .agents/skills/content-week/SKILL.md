## 1. Pitch 7 ideas (one message)

Never pitch an idea already scripted or posted, unless as a new angle he'd want, and say so.
2. **Teaching ideas (5)** come only from the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]] (Samuel, 2026-10-24: *"I don't want to come up with new teaching concepts when my teachings are already there"*). 3. **Story ideas (2)** come from his own stories: the story bank in [[03-Areas/personal-brand/personal-brand-ideas|ideas]] and the stories in the teachings (Story and mindset, the "Stories he hadn't told before" table). *"Storytelling majorly my stories, my experiences that could be of help to my target audience."* A story must carry a lesson for editors building a career.

## 2. His picks

He keeps, drops or swaps. Record the approved list in the week note under **Approved**, his words if he gives reasons. Dropped ideas stay in the pitched table, marked dropped. Spares he takes move up.

## 3. One script per idea, from his ramble

For each approved idea he sends a voice-note ramble. Don't ask questions first: his ramble is the brief.

1. Build the script from **his sentences, in his order**, plus his lines on the topic in the archive. Cut stumbles and repeats; keep his connectors ("you see", "right?", "let's say", "the truth is", "now"). Something he says not to put in (*"don't put that one"*) stays out.
2. **Teaching:** run [[write-script]] → "A teaching": the spine (hook, why me, the meat, one real example, takeaway + CTA) and the best-fit format, with punch-ins and on-screen text in braces for the seated shot. **Hook in the first line, on screen. 1:00–1:30 is best, 2:00 is the ceiling** (Samuel, 2026-10-29: *"maybe at most 2 minutes, best 1 minute, 30 seconds"*). At his pace that's about 250 words, 330 at most.
3. **Storytelling:** run [[write-script]] → story structure (Hook → Context → Conflict → Pivot → Resolution) as a voice-over. B-roll notes in braces, from the [[03-Areas/personal-brand/b-roll-archive|B-roll archive]] where a clip fits (check the name on disk), never illustrating the line literally.
4. **Not AI-sounding.** *"I don't want videos to be too AI looking. It must feel like it is still me."* No tidy triads, no slogan turns, no numbered "One, two" (he says "First / And then / And lastly"), no punchlines of our own, no comedy unless he asks. Sound like his own scripts in Notion (Samuel, 2026-10-29: *"Read past scripts on notion, not just in the second braind"*). Before the first script of a week, fetch one or two of his Done cards (each fetch is ~15k tokens because of the template above `Script`; don't fetch more). What they show (2026-10-29): one short line per thought; asides like *"omo"*, *"Hmmm"*, *"not to brag though"*, *"trust me"*; a rhetorical question (*"Like what do you mean…"*); *"But there was an issue"*, *"And then one day"*, *"The truth is"*; his own experience before the lesson; a bold one-line truth near the end (*"limitations birth creativity"*).
5. Give 2–3 hooks, the full script, its length, and any line you're unsure of as a question. Never fill a missing fact with a guess.
6. Save each as `03-Areas/personal-brand/scripts/<kebab-title>.md` and list it in the week note's **Scripts** table.
