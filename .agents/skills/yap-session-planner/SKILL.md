# Yap Session Planner

## Why this exists

Full scripts cause stumbling on camera because the brain tries to chase exact wording under the pressure of a silent, unresponsive lens. Zero prep causes rambling because there's no shape to fall back on when the ideas run out. This skill sits in between: it gives the person a handful of short phrases per beat — the *shape* of what they're about to say — so they can talk freely in their own words without either scripting every sentence or wandering with no structure at all.

## The process

Run this as a live back-and-forth conversation, not a form to fill out. Wait for a real reply at each step before moving to the next.

### Step 1 — Ask the topic

Ask one simple, open question — something like "What do you want to talk about?" or "What's on your mind for this one?" Don't pair it with a wall of sub-questions. Just ask, then wait.

### Step 2 — Classify the content type

Read their answer against the table below and decide which of the 5 types fits best. State the call in one line so they can correct you if you're wrong — e.g. "This sounds like a story, so let's build it with the Story framework." Don't ask "which type is this?" as a separate question; deciding and saying it out loud is the whole point of this step.

| Type | Signal in what they said |
|---|---|
| **Story** | A personal anecdote, "this one time," a clear turning point or struggle |
| **Topic / Concept** | Explaining an idea, trend, or how something works — no personal stance |
| **Opinion / Take** | A stance, a debate-bait claim, "unpopular opinion," a hot take |
| **Educative / Teaching** | A replicable process, "how to," a skill or tool walkthrough |
| **Experience / Review** | Trying, using, or testing something firsthand, ending in a verdict |

If the topic is too vague to classify (two or three words, no context), ask ONE narrowing question before deciding — e.g. "Is this more about something that happened to you, or an idea you want to explain?" Only do this when you genuinely can't tell; don't add this question by default.

If it's a genuine blend (common — a story that leads into an opinion, for example), say so, pick the dominant frame, and borrow a beat from the second framework if it helps (e.g. Story's beats, but the Directive states the Opinion).

### Step 3 — Interview beat by beat

Pick the matching framework from the table below. Ask about ONE beat at a time, in plain conversational language — never list all the beats at once and never let it feel like a form. Before asking about a beat, check whether their topic answer (or an earlier beat) already covered it — if so, reflect it back in one short line instead of asking again ("Sounds like your hook is already 'X' — good, moving on") .

Keep questions specific to the beat, not generic ("tell me more"). Use the sample questions below as a starting point and adapt them to what's already been said. For any "steps" beat, it's fine to ask once broadly ("walk me through the steps in order") — only follow up individually if their answer leaves a step out.

### Step 4 — Write the final structure

Once every beat is filled, compress their answers into short phrases — roughly 3-6 words each, not full sentences — and return the result in the Output Format below. This runs the opposite direction of a script: they gave you full sentences, but what they take into the recording is only the shape.

### Step 5 — Wrap up

Show the structure, then lightly ask if they want to (a) run through another topic right now, or (b) save this one to a file. Don't push either — default to showing it in chat only, since the whole point is a quick glance-and-put-away reference, not a document to read from.

## Frameworks

**Story** — Hook, Orient, Obstacle, Key Moment, Effect, Directive
- Hook: "What's the one line that'd stop someone scrolling? Lead with the wildest or most surprising part."
- Orient: "What's the one sentence of context needed before this makes sense?"
- Obstacle: "What was working against you — the tension or the problem?"
- Key Moment: "What was the exact turning point?"
- Effect: "What happened after, or what changed?"
- Directive: "What should the viewer walk away doing or believing?"

**Topic / Concept** — Hook, What, Why, How (up to 3 points), Directive
- Hook: "What's a bold or surprising claim about this that'd stop the scroll?"
- What: "In one sentence, what is this thing?"
- Why: "Why should someone care about this right now?"
- How: "What are the 2-3 things that actually explain how or why this works?"
- Directive: "What should they do or think differently after hearing this?"

**Opinion / Take** — Hook, The Take, Reasons, Counterpoint, Directive
- Hook: "What's the most provocative way to say this?"
- The Take: "Say the actual opinion in one clear sentence."
- Reasons: "What are your top 1-2 reasons for believing this?"
- Counterpoint: "What would someone who disagrees say — and why are you still right?"
- Directive: "What do you want people to do with this — agree, argue back, reconsider something?"

**Educative / Teaching** — Hook, What You'll Learn, Step 1, Step 2, Step 3, Recap/Directive
- Hook: "What will they be able to do after this that they can't do now?"
- What You'll Learn: "In one sentence, what's the outcome or promise?"
- Steps: "Walk me through the steps, in the order someone should actually do them."
- Recap/Directive: "What's the one-line recap, and the action you want them to take?"

**Experience / Review** — Hook, Context, Expectation vs Reality, Verdict, Directive
- Hook: "What's the most surprising result from trying this?"
- Context: "What did you try or use, and why?"
- Expectation vs Reality: "What did you expect going in, and what actually happened?"
- Verdict: "What's your honest take or rating?"
- Directive: "Would you recommend it, and to who specifically?"

See `references/frameworks.md` for alternate phrasings of each question and fully worked examples — useful for keeping repeat sessions from feeling identical, or when a topic doesn't cleanly match the sample questions above.

## Output format

Always finish with this exact shape (swap the beat labels for whichever framework was used):

```
🎬 [one-line topic/title]
Framework: [Story / Topic / Opinion / Educative / Experience]

[BEAT 1]: [3-6 word phrase]
[BEAT 2]: [3-6 word phrase]
[BEAT 3]: [3-6 word phrase]
[BEAT 4]: [3-6 word phrase]
[BEAT 5]: [3-6 word phrase]
[BEAT 6 if applicable]: [3-6 word phrase]
```

Follow it with one line reminding them how to use it: glance at it once before recording, then put it away and talk.
