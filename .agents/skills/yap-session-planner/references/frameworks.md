# Framework Reference — Extended Examples & Alternate Questions

This expands on the condensed table in SKILL.md. Reach for it when a repeat session needs a fresh way to ask the same beat, or when a fully worked example would help calibrate the output.

## Story

Alternate questions per beat:
- Hook: "If you only had one sentence to make someone stop scrolling, what would it be?" / "What's the punchline if someone asked 'so what happened?'"
- Orient: "Where were you, or what was going on, right before this started?"
- Obstacle: "What almost made this not happen, or what was the hard part?"
- Key Moment: "What's the moment you'd replay if you were watching this back?"
- Effect: "What was different afterward?"
- Directive: "If someone's in the position you were in, what do you want them to do?"

### Worked example
Topic given: "I want to talk about the first time a client almost fired me because I missed a deadline."

Classification: Story (clear anecdote, tension, turning point).

Filled beats:
```
🎬 The client who almost fired me
Framework: Story

Hook: Client almost fired me over deadline
Orient: Third month running the business solo
Obstacle: Overpromised turnaround, underestimated the edit
Key Moment: Called the client before they called me
Effect: Kept the client, rebuilt the process
Directive: Own the mistake before they find it
```

## Topic / Concept

Alternate questions:
- Hook: "What's the thing most people get wrong about this?"
- What: "If you had to explain this to a 10-year-old, what would you say?"
- Why: "Why does this matter to your audience specifically?"
- How: "Break it into the 2-3 moving parts — what are they?"
- Directive: "What's the one thing you want them to remember?"

### Worked example
Topic given: "I want to explain why human trust matters more than the AI's actual accuracy when it comes to adoption."

Classification: Topic / Concept (explaining an idea, no personal anecdote).

Filled beats:
```
🎬 Why trust beats accuracy in AI adoption
Framework: Topic / Concept

Hook: Accuracy isn't why people trust AI
What: Trust is belief the tool won't blindside you
Why: Low trust kills adoption, no matter how good the tool
How: Consistency, transparency, and staying in control
Directive: Design for trust, not just accuracy
```

## Opinion / Take

Alternate questions:
- Hook: "What's a version of this take that'd make someone comment 'nah, disagree'?"
- The Take: "Finish the sentence: 'Everyone thinks X, but actually...'"
- Reasons: "What's your strongest piece of evidence for this?"
- Counterpoint: "Steelman the other side for a second — what's their best argument?"
- Directive: "What do you want the comment section arguing about?"

### Worked example
Topic given: "Unpopular opinion — most people don't need a fancy camera to grow on social media."

Classification: Opinion / Take (a stance, debate-bait framing).

Filled beats:
```
🎬 You don't need a fancy camera
Framework: Opinion / Take

Hook: Your camera isn't why you're not growing
The Take: A $200 phone beats a $2,000 camera on hooks
Reasons: Algorithm rewards watch time, not resolution
Counterpoint: Gear matters later, once retention is solved
Directive: Fix your first 3 seconds before buying gear
```

## Educative / Teaching

Alternate questions:
- Hook: "What's the outcome someone gets if they follow this?"
- What You'll Learn: "Complete this: 'By the end you'll be able to ___.'"
- Steps: "What's step one — literally the very first thing they do?"
- Recap/Directive: "What's the one thing that trips people up if they skip a step?"

### Worked example
Topic given: "I want to teach the 3 steps I use to write a UGC ad script fast."

Classification: Educative / Teaching (a replicable process).

Filled beats:
```
🎬 Write a UGC ad script fast
Framework: Educative / Teaching

Hook: I write ad scripts in under 10 minutes
What You'll Learn: A repeatable 3-step script structure
Step 1: Hook pulled from the customer's own words
Step 2: Problem stated in their language, not the brand's
Step 3: Product as the plot twist, not the pitch
Recap/Directive: Steal the words, don't invent them
```

## Experience / Review

Alternate questions:
- Hook: "What's the one result that surprised you most?"
- Context: "Set the scene — what were you hoping this would solve?"
- Expectation vs Reality: "Where did it match your expectations, and where didn't it?"
- Verdict: "If a friend asked 'is it worth it,' what's your actual answer?"
- Directive: "Who specifically should — or shouldn't — try this?"

### Worked example
Topic given: "I tried a new AI voice cloning tool for my content for a week."

Classification: Experience / Review (firsthand trial, ending in a verdict).

Filled beats:
```
🎬 I tried AI voice cloning for a week
Framework: Experience / Review

Hook: It sounded like me on the third try
Context: Wanted to cut re-record time on flubbed lines
Expectation vs Reality: Expected robotic, got 90% convincing
Verdict: Worth it for pickups, not full narration yet
Directive: Try it for fixes, not whole scripts
```
