# Teachings: Webinar Q&A

The questions attendees asked at the end of the live webinar (Fri 2026-10-23) and how Samuel answered them. These are real beginner questions, so each one is a ready-made "you asked, here's the answer" video. Names are left out. Times are from the start of the OBS recording.

Part of the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]]. Source: the webinar transcript (machine-made, see the archive's sources).

## "An agency wants a specific educational editing style. I can't find a tutorial for it." [3:00:45]

- Almost every style is on YouTube somewhere, sometimes far down the results.
- Some styles aren't (his own client style isn't), but someone who has mastered their software can work it out: *"I can look at that video now and tell you how to do it, because I know how to use the tools."*
- Ask editors who already do that style, in communities or by DM. He once **paid an editor to teach him a style** he wanted.

## "I edit with CapCut on my phone. How do I learn DaVinci Resolve?" [3:03:37]

- Resolve doesn't run on a phone, so you'd need a computer.
- But don't wait for it: *"Start with what you have."* The result matters more than the tool, so get results for clients with the phone and CapCut you have, and grow into bigger software.

## "Premiere and After Effects were too heavy for my laptop and I got confused about what to learn. Will the community teach the software?" [3:05:18]

- It feels too complex because you're thinking of "video editing" as one thing. Break it into pieces: colour grading is one piece, typography is another. Each is learnable on YouTube; put the pieces together into a full video.
- **Learn to be self-taught.** *"I never bought one course. The only thing I ever paid for in my video editing journey was a community."* He read a 1,000-page DaVinci Resolve book on Fusion to master it. *"Don't always wait for someone to come and guide your hand... If you don't know how to learn things by yourself, you'll never become a master."*
- He'll guide people in the community, and plans the **Called to Edit Academy**, a cohort for advanced work (Resolve Fusion, animation), *"but you really don't even need it if you know how to be self-taught."*

## "I've never edited before. How long does it take to learn?" [3:11:48]

- Try to learn everything and it takes a year. Learn only what your niche needs (say, cutting podcasts and adding subtitles) and it can take a week.
- With a proper niche, **about one month**, if you're serious. *"I've seen it work."*

## "How do you get on a call with a client overseas?" [3:12:44]

- Google Meet or Zoom, booked online, not a phone call. He has also taken client calls on Instagram and Twitter, but prefers Meet.

## "If I re-edit a video someone else already edited, can it go in my portfolio?" [3:13:01]

- If you copied another editor's edit (their style, their idea): **no**. *"It is that person that came up with the idea of that edit."*
- If you took a raw video and edited it your own way: **yes**, the ideas are yours.

## "I can only find talking-head raw footage. Where do I get footage for other niches, like UGC or finance?" [3:15:12]

- Go where that niche lives. UGC (user-generated content) lives on TikTok, and most of it is lightly edited: download it, re-edit it, use it to practise or to pitch that creator. You won't find podcasts on TikTok.
- Or ask a creator to send you their raw footage, even if they already have an editor.
- **Can it go in the portfolio?** *"The edit is your intellectual property. The person owns the footage."* Don't post their footage as your own content (they can flag it), and if they ask you to remove it, remove it. A portfolio is seen by other potential clients, who mostly won't know the original creator. He re-edited an MKBHD video and posted it on Twitter to show his edit. *(He added that a re-edited video counts as new content "by law"; that's his view, not checked.)*

## "Niches look similar when you edit them. Do skills from one niche carry to another?" [3:19:33]

- Yes, the skills transfer. He has edited business talking heads, trading videos, vlogs and drop-shipping videos.
- But an editor who understands finance will bring out what matters in a finance video better than a general business editor. *"It's still transferable, but the best is specialized."*

## "When should a beginner start sending cold DMs?" [3:23:26]

- Once you've finished **three full videos in your niche**, start to finish, even practice ones, ideally including one for a real person.
- *"Don't wait till you have five videos in your archive."* *"I started outreaching even when I was not that good. What you think is not yet good is someone else's best. Someone is willing to pay you for that thing that you think is not good."*

## "If SaaS clients are slow, can I add podcasts as a second niche?" [3:24:04]

- You can mix niches, but **only similar ones** (business, SaaS, AI). *"You can't be mixing talking-head long-form business with someone on TikTok dancing."*

## "Clients send big files on Google Drive and it's slow. What do you use?" [3:26:36]

- *"Google Drive is frustrating."* His clients use **Frame.io**: paid, but faster to upload and download. Sometimes the client pays for it (his current client does), sometimes you will. WeTransfer exists but he doesn't recommend it for professional work.
- Same asker: Fusion tutorials will go into the community; an advanced Academy cohort that teaches Fusion *"probably around November, December."* How to pitch so you're worth USD 500 instead of USD 100: *"there's a way,"* to be covered in the community.

## "Will the community give us access to clients?" [3:30:21]

- He'll guide each member on where to find clients for their niche.
- When he has client work he thinks a member can handle, he'll post it as an opportunity; members will recommend each other.
- His hope: *"that clients will be coming to find video editors in the community... that any video editor that is in the Called to Edit brand is good enough."*
- A weekly call, and the right tutorials dropped per niche for beginners.
