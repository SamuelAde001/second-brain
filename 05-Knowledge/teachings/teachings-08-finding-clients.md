# Teachings: Finding clients

Inbound vs outbound, warm outreach, the dream client list, warm-ups, job boards.

Part of the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]]. From his prep voice notes (2026-10-16 to 2026-10-21, [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-teachings|prep record]]); what he added live on 2026-10-23 is marked **Live** with the time in the recording.

## Inbound: getting clients to come to you

**Opening for section 5: skip the groundwork and this gets hard.** If you skipped being specific (your niche, who you serve) or being visible, *"getting clients will be difficult for you."* Establish yourself in one niche and be *"visible and loud about it."*

**Two ways to get clients**
| | What | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Inbound** | the client comes to you: an *"inbound lead"* | *"Hey, I saw your work, I would love to know how much you charge"* |
| **Outbound / outreach** | you approach the client | you see someone whose videos you could edit and reach out |
- **Do both.** *"You can't rely on only inbound and you can't rely on only outbound."* Established editors get more inbound; beginners have to go looking.

**What makes a client DM you rather than another editor**
- **Specificity**: your personal brand makes you unique.
- **Reliability.** Clients want editors who are reliable, diligent, hardworking and deliver on time. *"Clients are tired of editors that know how to do flashy edits, but they are not reliable"* and miss deadlines.
- **So document your day.** A client scrolls your profile, sees your day-to-day and thinks *"this guy is hardworking."* His time-lapses of himself working got that comment: *"this guy is locked in."*
- *"People are not just looking at the fancy videos. They want to see... the behind the scenes."* That attracts quality clients.

**Every view counts:** *"Those 200 views, one of them could be your client."* Or one of them knows your client and recommends you. Many people recommended him to clients because they followed him on Twitter.

**Trust:** *"I can't give you my money if I don't trust you."* Your content builds that reputation.

**What makes clients DM you, as a list:** showing your work · how you came up with the work · the person and personality behind it · your credibility: testimonials and the results the work brought · posting consistently (not once a week, not once every 2–3 days).

**Be patient:** 1, 2, 3 months of consistency. Once other editors comment, follow and like your posts, clients follow, *"because if you are valuable to other video editors, it means that you are valuable."*

**Teaching:** inbound only comes from work on your personal brand. Show your work, the process and you, every day, and be patient for 1–3 months.

**In his words:**
- *"Clients are tired of editors that know how to do flashy edits, but they are not reliable."*
- *"Those 200 views, one of them could be your client."*
- *"I can't give you my money if I don't trust you."*
- *"There are a lot of clients looking for people. You are invisible. That's why they can't find you."*

## Outbound: where to find clients, which platforms

Outbound is you looking for clients. Don't wait for them to find you, at any stage, and especially at the start, when no one knows you yet.

**Two kinds of outreach**
| | Who | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm outreach** | people who already know you: friends, family, colleagues doing content, people in your DMs and on your WhatsApp list, editors you know (*"we'll talk about reaching out to other editors later on"*) | they already know or trust you. *"Warm outreach converts the fastest."* |
| **Cold outreach** | people who don't know you | it takes research and warming up (below) |

**Warm outreach**
- His own first client was warm: his mentor had just started on YouTube. *"Sir, I love your videos and I see what you do. I would love to help you out in your videos."*
- Ask people you know: *"I'm a video editor, I edit videos, do you know anyone that needs my service?"* They can link you up. Even your father who works in a bank may know someone.
- **Beginners: work with someone first, whoever it is.** Real projects are the fastest way to grow; most people stay in practice mode too long. Free or low pay, start there.
- It may not be the best payer. But without results and evidence, *"the high-paying clients will not even consider you. They want to see experience."*
- **Homework: *"Every one of you guys that is here must do a warm outreach after this meeting."*** Do your research on who to reach first.

**Finding cold clients** (he'll demo this live on screen)
1. **Go to the platform where your client is.** For YouTube clients, search YouTube for creators in your niche, watch their videos and get to understand what they do. Scroll past the many who already have great editors.
2. **A potential client has three things:**
   - they're in your niche
   - they already make money in some form, so they can afford you
   - they need an editor: videos performing badly, badly edited, or not edited at all. Even well-edited creators may need an extra editor.
3. **Find their email.** On YouTube many creators list one.
4. **Find them everywhere else:** Instagram, LinkedIn, Twitter. Note the kind of content they make and save examples you can use as references.
5. **On Instagram, go smaller.** The top accounts for your niche have big audiences and editors already. Look for creators with about 1,000 to 20,000 followers (on the recording he said "500 followers" for the big accounts; he meant 500K, confirmed 2026-10-21). Many of them edit by themselves or have editors who aren't good.

**The dream client list** (*"This is the most important thing for every video editor."*)
- A table in Notion (his pick, *"I always recommend Notion"*) or a spreadsheet. Columns: name · channel or handle · email · other platforms (Instagram, LinkedIn, Twitter) · type of content · follower count · short or long form · links to example videos · follow-ups done.
- You're gathering leads, not reaching out yet.
- Aim for **20 to 50**. His was **100, his "dream 100"**, built by hand before AI could research.
- Keep adding every day until you land your dream 1, 2, 3 clients.
- Keep two lists: **potential clients** (prospects, dream clients) and **clients** (people you already work with; keep records of them too).

**Multiple points of contact**
- He emailed each of the 100 one by one, then sent the same person an Instagram DM, then a Twitter message. *"You don't know which of the platforms they might open."*
- What the creator thinks: email, *"I don't really need this guy"* → Instagram, *"this guy is very serious"* → Twitter, *"oh boy, this guy, he really means business. Let me respond to him."*

**Warm up the list before you DM**
- Turn on notifications for the creator. Be the first to comment, and say something good about what they teach.
- After **4 or 5 posts** (smaller creators especially) they notice you: *"this guy likes my content."* By the time you DM, you're not a stranger.
- Comment enough and they check your page: *"who is this guy?"* They see you're an editor; if they need one right then, they may reach out first.
- His own: on Twitter, *"As they're posting, I'm commenting."*

**Research with AI** (if you can pay for it)
- ChatGPT on its own *"won't even give you the best results"*. Agent tools can: Codex, Claude Code, Grok.
- He pays for Claude Code and has an agent that researches and pulls potential clients for him from YouTube, Instagram and TikTok. Best on a laptop. *"I think it's just 33,000 naira a month."*
- He won't go deep on agents here: *"you master it later on, maybe in the community."*

**Sites that help you find clients** (researched 2026-10-21 on his ask, *"help me take note of those websites"*; he did it by hand)
| Site | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **YouTube itself** | a channel's About page shows a business email only when the creator has added one; also check video descriptions and linked sites | free |
| **Channel Crawler** | search YouTube channels by niche, country and subscriber count; export lists | search free; emails and exports on credits, paid ([Channel Crawler](https://channelcrawler.com/features)) |
| **TubeMailer** | Chrome extension: search YouTube by keyword, export the public business emails to a CSV | free to try ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hiphjffbgpjmkkcdjfhapibeldcappng)) |
| **Modash** | finds creators on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube by niche and follower range | 14-day free trial, then paid; a separate free YouTube email finder ([Modash](https://www.modash.io/youtube-email-finder)) |

**Teaching:** do warm outreach first, then build a dream client list and warm each person up before you reach out, on every platform they use.

**In his words:**
- *"Warm outreach converts the fastest."*
- *"They want to see experience."*
- *"You need to have a list. This is the most important thing for every video editor."*
- *"By the time I get to their DM, I am not really a stranger."*

## Upwork, Fiverr and job boards: worth it or not

| Route | How | His verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **"Looking for a video editor" posts** on Twitter, LinkedIn, TikTok | search that phrase | his least favourite. About **80% are scams**; editors (some automated) flood the DMs within minutes. *"I recommend it with 0.1% chance."* |
| **Communities and Discord servers** | job channels | free ones are *"filled up with scammers"*: USD 2 edits, people asking you to send money, links that are viruses. Real jobs are in **paid communities**: he got one or two clients from **VP Plus**, at about NGN 50,000 a month |
| **Job sites** (Jobberman and others) | apply | *"It doesn't work."* One editor post gets about 5,000 applicants |
| **Upwork and Fiverr** | profile and bids | a race to the bottom on price: *"if you are not the best video editor there, you are the cheapest."* Saturated. Some editors he knows did succeed there |

**Teaching:** don't rely on one way of getting clients. Use all of them. These routes are the weakest, and they're full of scams.

**In his words:**
- *"Don't rely on one means."*
- *"I recommend it with 0.1% chance."*

## Live at the webinar, 2026-10-23

What he said live that the prep didn't have, or said better. Times are from the start of the OBS recording.

**Know, like and trust** (new at the webinar) [2:13:29]
- They have to **know** you (you're visible), **like** you (they see your personality) and **trust** you (they see work you've done for past clients) before they reach out.
- *"A client might know you're a video editor, but if they don't like you, they won't reach out to you."*

**What makes them DM you, live**
- **Live [2:15:39]** Show the behind the scenes: him *"sitting down for five hours editing a video"* tells a client he's reliable and disciplined.
- **Live [2:16:27]** *"Another video editor that is posting every day would beat you that is posting once a week."*
- **Live [2:16:45]** **"You won't have what to post if you are not doing the work."** *"There's no week I don't edit videos, even till now."* Keep working on real projects so you always have something to show.
- **Live [2:17:34]** The five, as he listed them: show your work · show the person behind it · be reliable · be credible · be consistent.
- **Live [1:31:51]** *"If you are a generalist, getting your first client will be very, very difficult."*

**Warm outreach, live**
- **Live [2:18:24]** His mentor was warm outreach: *"He didn't meet me, I met him."* Think of a coach posting one or two videos who isn't consistent yet.
- **Live [2:19:10]** **"Some of your clients are inside your contact list... that 200-contact list on WhatsApp."** *"Opportunities are not always far away from you. Our problem is we are too hidden. We are too scared. The highest they can tell you is no."*
- **Live [2:19:41]** **He once broadcast to all 250 of his WhatsApp contacts** with his flyer: *"I'm a video editor and I'm starting to do video editing."* One of the 250 recommended him.
- **Live [2:20:25]** Other editors get too busy and pass work on.

**Cold outreach, live demos**
- **Live [2:20:42]** Cold is where the *"bigger boys"* are, but they don't know you, like you or care about you yet. And they have to be in your niche: *"If you do vlogs and I'm a sports creator, I'll tell you you can't edit my type of videos."*
- **Live [2:22:01]** **YouTube:** search your niche ("podcast"), skip the big shows (they have editors), use **Filters → Channels**. He found a crime podcast with 9,000 subscribers, about 2,000 views a video and little editing: a potential client. The email was on the channel page.
- **Live [2:24:47]** **Instagram:** search the niche ("crypto videos"), find creators whose videos aren't well edited, DM: *"I love your videos, but I feel that they need better editing, and I can do something for you."*
- **Live [2:27:05]** **AI research:** he had Claude search YouTube for *"fitness creators below 20,000 subs that I can edit for."* For Instagram, the **Meta AI app** can search for potential clients. Plus Channel Crawler, TubeMailer and AI agents. *"Every video editor must learn how to use AI in this day and age."*
- **Live [2:29:55]** *"Most of them will tell you no... But if you do 50 people, among those 50 at least one person might consider you."* He showed his old dream list (name, email, Instagram, Twitter); his Dream 100 lived in Notion.
- **Live [2:31:04]** Warm-up: turn on notifications and be among the first to comment, three times, and they start to notice. *"Don't just say 'nice post'."* Only with creators around 10,000–20,000 followers, never accounts with a million comments. *"You have started to move them from being a cold client to a warm client"* [2:33:03].

**Other routes, live**
- **Live [0:33:56]** Agencies are good for beginners, bad when you want to scale.
- **Live [2:48:40]** Job-site roles pay a fixed salary *"like an employee"*, so you can't raise your value or negotiate, but they're still a start. Upwork and Fiverr: *"competing for the lowest price."*

**When to start outreach** (Q&A, [3:24:31]): after three full videos in your niche, even practice ones. *"Don't wait till you have five videos in your archive."* *"I started outreaching even when I was not that good. What you think is not yet good is someone else's best. Someone is willing to pay you for that thing that you think is not good."*

**Raw footage to practise and pitch with** (Q&A, [3:16:04]): go where that niche lives. UGC lives on TikTok, where most videos are lightly edited: download, re-edit, use it to practise or pitch that creator. Podcasts aren't on TikTok. Or ask a creator to send you their raw footage even if they have an editor.
