# Teachings: Choosing a niche

Who pays, how to choose, the offer sentence, short form vs long form.

Part of the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]]. From his prep voice notes (2026-10-16 to 2026-10-21, [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-teachings|prep record]]); what he added live on 2026-10-23 is marked **Live** with the time in the recording.

## The niches, pros and cons

**He asked for a researched list** of the major popular niches across YouTube, Instagram and TikTok (*"you will help me do some research and list out some of the niches"*). Below: his ranking first, then the list.

**His ranking: who pays**
- **Best: business people and anyone selling a product.** *"In any niche, business guys pay more"*, because they already understand marketing.
  - Top of that: **SaaS, software, AI**. *"Those guys have money. They can pay you a lot."*
  - Also good: **real estate agents, marketers, logistics companies, individual brands.**
  - **Educative creators** are good; most are business people (0.3).
- **Lowest: entertainment, gaming, skits, people who dance on TikTok.** *"To be honest, they are mostly broke."* They live on brand deals, adverts and sponsorships; margins are low and depend on views. By the time they hit ~1M views and can pay, *"they already have a video editor that is good."* *"I warn you against entertainment."* Fine to start with, not where the best money is.
- **The follower trap:** *"A business guy can have 10,000 followers"* and possibly make USD 100,000 a month because he markets to them. An entertainer with 100,000 followers may not afford USD 500, or even USD 100, per video.
- **Corporate companies:** they hire you like a normal employee on fixed pay, whatever you generate, and you can't negotiate. *"I always advise against it."* He prefers **solo content creators and small businesses that make money**: easier to negotiate, more flexible.
- **Low-ticket sellers:** creators selling something cheap may not make enough to justify paying an editor well.

## How to choose one

**His questions to choose by**
1. **What excites you most, and what you watch most.** *"Edit niches that they watch videos from the most."* He watches a lot of business videos, so he understands how they're edited.
2. **How much does this niche usually generate? Can they afford an editor?**
3. **Do they value marketing?** *"That's why business guys are always the best, because they already understand why marketing is."*
4. Plus the 1.1 factors: who you serve and what video, who pays, your hardware, your software.

**Then make an offer for that niche: the offer sentence.** Once you've picked, put yourself out there as that: *"I edit videos for SaaS companies."*
- **Who** you help (the specific target audience)
- **What result** you get them (more views? more sales? *"There must be a promise"*)
- **How** (video editing, motion design, visually engaging content, storytelling)
- **How fast** (a time range)
- His example, as he said it: *"I help business creators get 5,000 views in 6 months or 10,000 views in 3 months through video editing or through visual engaging content."*
- *"You have to always make sure you are specific."*

## Short form vs long form: where the beginner money is

- **Long form pays more.** *"It's in long form that you easily hear USD 500 per video, USD 1,000 per video, USD 2,000 per video."*
- **Short form pays less per video**, so you package volume: *"I'm doing 5 videos for you in a month, 10 videos for you in a month."*
- **Ads are the best-paying short form.** Ads tie directly to the clients a business gets, so it's easy to measure how your videos perform. *"I know guys that run ads or edit ads, and bro, they make a lot of money."*
- **Both have strengths:** *"A short form editor can earn more than a long form editor if he knows how to package his stuff well."*

**Teaching (section 3):** pick a niche you watch and enjoy, one that makes money and values marketing (business, SaaS, AI, real estate), not entertainment. Then say it in one offer sentence: who, what result, how, how fast.

**In his words:**
- *"In any niche, business guys pay more."*
- *"Entertainment, gaming, skits... to be honest, they are mostly broke."*
- *"There must be a promise."*
- *"A short form editor can earn more than a long form editor if he knows how to package his stuff well."*

## Live at the webinar, 2026-10-23

What he said live that the prep didn't have, or said better. Times are from the start of the OBS recording.

**We learn for the wrong clients**
- **Live [0:32:13]** He polled the room: which creators pay most? Business. *"But most of us learn the skill of video editing that is only meant for entertainment guys. That's the problem."*
- **Live [0:53:05]** **Personal finance and investment creators pay well, and their videos aren't hard to edit**: no flashy edits, so a mobile editor can do them. *"We are focusing on entertainment guys whose videos are hard to edit, and at the end of the day we wonder why we can't build a career."*
- **Live [0:54:11]** **Ads: "a secret niche that they drop money like this."** Companies doing ads for businesses and brands pay big, and most ads aren't hard to edit.
- **Live [0:56:13]** Weddings pay too: *"If I save money for my wedding, I must get a good video editor."*
- **Live [0:48:16]** A business creator with 10,000 followers can earn what an entertainer needs 500,000 followers to earn. Business people *"literally budget for marketing"*, and editing is part of marketing [0:50:51].
- **Live [0:48:56]** SaaS and AI companies are *"almost at the top of the chain in this economy"* (one of his clients is a SaaS founder in AI). Big Nigerian business names (Tony Elumelu, Dangote) have someone editing behind them, but they already have editors: start with people closer to you.

**Specialist beats generalist** (new at the webinar)
- **Live [0:44:12]** **"Generalists will always lose to specialists."** A specialist doctor earns more than a general doctor.
- **Live [1:25:33]** *"Master one skill and become a specialist in it."* Wedding editor? Become the best wedding editor. Podcast editor? The best podcast editor.
- **Live [1:30:26]** That's the answer to "why should they choose you?": *"As a podcast creator, I will prefer a video editor that focuses on podcasts."* There are Web3 and crypto editors, even editors for government: every niche has its specialists [1:31:27, 1:32:39].
- **Live [1:00:44]** He practises it: he edits only long form for clients, no short form.

**Choosing, new stories**
- **Live [0:56:43]** A programmer once offered him about **NGN 1.5 million for around 100 videos**. They were boring, he hated editing them, and he turned it down. Pick a niche whose videos you enjoy watching. *(He said "2004"; it must be a recent year. Not checked.)*
- **Live [0:57:22]** Edit what you watch most: you've watched enough to know what it should look like. Sports fan, edit sports. Podcast lover, edit podcasts.
- **Live [0:58:29]** His gaming passion died because gamers *"are mostly broke"*: excitement without money fades.
- **Live [0:59:01]** If a niche doesn't value marketing, *"you have to convince them on marketing."*
- **Live [0:59:34]** The four questions as one list: what excites you · how much the niche makes · do they value marketing · can your tools handle it.
- **Live [0:46:41]** Every niche has its own look: get-ready-with-me videos have one aesthetic, sports another. A phone editor's offer: *"I help lifestyle creators edit Get Ready With Me content with my CapCut."*

**The offer sentence, live** [1:01:43]: who you help (*"they have a name, they have an occupation"*) · what result · which method. *"I help fitness coaches drive more sales through well-created engaging videos for their Instagram."* And *"Do you see me saying 'I am a video editor'?"* Another: *"I help sports creators edit their videos so that they can get more views"* [0:39:50]: *"now your editing has a purpose."*

**Mixing niches** (Q&A, [3:25:41]): only similar ones (business, SaaS, AI). *"You can't be mixing talking-head long-form business with someone on TikTok dancing."* Skills transfer across niches (he has edited business talking heads, trading, vlogs, drop-shipping), *"but the best is specialized"* [3:21:31].
