# Teachings: Tools and money to start

Phone vs laptop software, what it really costs, the computer you need.

Part of the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]]. From his prep voice notes (2026-10-16 to 2026-10-21, [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-teachings|prep record]]); what he added live on 2026-10-23 is marked **Live** with the time in the recording.

## The editing tools, pros and cons: phone (CapCut) vs laptop (Resolve, Premiere)

**What decides your software:** the video type you chose (and the skills it needs) + the hardware you have or can afford. *"If you don't have the hardware that can support that software, then don't even try to focus on videos that need a skill that you can only achieve with a particular software."* No laptop means Resolve, Premiere and Final Cut (mostly Mac) aren't options for you.

**The tools he named**

| | Phone | Laptop |
|---|---|---|
| Apps | CapCut · Premiere Pro mobile · the "Edit" app | DaVinci Resolve · Premiere Pro (+ After Effects) · CapCut Desktop · Final Cut (Mac) |
| Cost | All free on phone (paid tiers exist) | Resolve free · Premiere paid · CapCut free with Pro |

**Pros and cons, his words**
- **DaVinci Resolve:** *"a great all-rounder, especially color grading."* Free. Needs a laptop that can run it.
- **Premiere Pro + After Effects:** best for *"crazy motion design stuff."* Resolve can do it, but these work best. Paid.
- **CapCut:** the free version is good and *"enough to get you paid work"*, but most major professional tools need the paid version. If you can afford a laptop, start with CapCut on the laptop, depending on your niche.
- **The "Edit" app (phone):** he uses it, especially for Instagram-style music. *(Likely Meta's Edits app; check the name.)*
- **Colour grading** is part of video editing, and some people sell colour grading alone.

**Invest in your software**
- *"If you really want to do this thing, guys, invest in this software."*
- **Never use cracked software.** He once used a cracked Premiere Pro while trying to move from Resolve, and got hacked: *"not joking, I was hacked on my computer. And it affected my work."* He went back to Resolve.
- It's a ladder: he used free Resolve for client work for two to three years before buying Resolve Studio (paid).
- Phone editors: pay for CapCut Pro. If you don't have the money, *"you can borrow money to pay for the first subscription"*, then work hard that month to earn the next one.

**Subscriptions are capital.** *"Whatever you're paying for a subscription is your capital."* Once you see it as a business you think *"I'm already spending capital. I need to start making profit"*, and that pushes you to pitch.

**AI tools in the stack:** depends on your niche.
- He pays for Claude now, but a beginner shouldn't pay for Claude if they don't need it.
- Other paid AI tools exist, e.g. ones that cut long form into short forms. *(He started to name one, "Opus", then corrected himself; likely OpusClip. Check.)*
- If you need one, invest, then make a profit quickly to cover it.

**His story:** started on DaVinci Resolve because it was free and he didn't know CapCut existed. His laptop could just about run it (*"It was hanging, but it could do something"*).

**Teaching:** your niche and your hardware pick the software. Start free if you must, never cracked, and pay for the pro version as soon as you can: it's your capital.

**Examples:** hacked on a cracked Premiere Pro · free Resolve for client work for 2–3 years before Studio · the Edit app for Instagram music · he pays for Claude.

**In his words:**
- *"Invest in this software."*
- *"Whatever you're paying for a subscription is your capital."*
- *"CapCut is enough to get you paid work."*
- *"I was hacked on my computer."* (the cracked-software warning)

## How much money you need to start

**Video editing is expensive.** *"It costs you a lot."* The costs he named:
- software: subscriptions or one-off payments
- **data**: *"Data is very important"*
- **electricity**: he had to buy a generator and fuel it to edit; now also solar inverters
- storage
- headphones
- mic

**"Free" isn't really free.** Even a phone editor needs data to watch tutorials, download videos, upload and show your work, and needs electricity.

**The first one or two months are capital**, an investment in your business and your skill.

**That's why you move from free to paid fast** (1.6, 1.7): paid clients relieve the cost, then you break even, then you profit.

**How much? No specific price:** *"I'm not giving any specific price because everything varies."* If you have a phone and can afford data, you can start.

**His example:** his first laptop cost **NGN 150,000** at the time, *"a lot"* (0.1: a whole salary). *"Later on, things start to change."*

**Teaching:** editing costs real money (data, power, software, gear), so treat the first months as investment and get your first paid client as soon as possible.

**In his words:**
- *"Video editing is expensive."*
- *"You can start from free, but it's not really free."*
- *"It's all an investment."*

## The computer you need for each niche

**Minimum for DaVinci Resolve:** at least **16 GB RAM**, an **SSD** and a **dedicated GPU**. These are expensive.

**Start with what you have**
- Don't pick niches that need heavy edits if you can't afford a laptop that handles them.
- Ask: what laptop do I have now? What laptop or phone can I afford? Start with what you can do on it.
- *"I can't mention every computer that you need for every niche... it all varies and it changes."*
- Example: proper colour grading can't be done on a phone. You need a better computer and Resolve.

**His hardware ladder**
1. First laptop: **4 GB RAM**, *"very bad"*.
2. Second: **8 GB**, upgraded to **16 GB**.
3. Third: came with **16 GB**, upgraded to **64 GB**.
4. Now: a **PC, 32 GB RAM, a big RTX GPU**.

**Reinvest your editing money in your tools.** *"Don't spend your video editing money on yourself yet."* Putting his earnings back into his tools was *"one of the fastest ways I grew."*

**Mac:** *"The best laptops for video editing"* are Apple Macs (he named the M1 Macs). He couldn't afford one, so he used Windows. If you can, save up for one: *"it will make your life easier."*

**A bad laptop frustrates you**, but it can still edit some kinds of video.

**Don't wait for the perfect machine**
- Some say *"I'm going to wait till I earn 1 million before I buy my first laptop"*. It will take a while.
- If all you can afford is an **NGN 300,000** laptop, buy it and use it until you can upgrade to an **NGN 600,000** one.
- **Buy a laptop you can upgrade:** he upgraded RAM instead of buying a new laptop.

**Teaching:** start on the machine you can afford, buy one you can upgrade, and put your editing money back into your tools.

**In his words:**
- *"Don't spend your video editing money on yourself yet."*
- *"A bad laptop is going to frustrate your video editing life."*
- *"Don't wait till you have all the money to buy the high-end laptops."*

## Live at the webinar, 2026-10-23

What he said live that the prep didn't have, or said better. Times are from the start of the OBS recording.

**Your tool doesn't matter** (new at the webinar)
- **Live [1:05:42]** *"I used to downplay CapCut editors... I was like, you're editing with CapCut? But let me tell you guys, it's a lie. It's a big lie."*
- **Live [1:06:04]** **"Your tool does not matter as long as you produce results."** (He had everyone type it in the chat.) If a CapCut editor brings a business more views or sales, *"do I need to pay a Premiere Pro editor anymore to do fancy editing?"*
- **Live [1:07:19]** So don't pitch the app. Pitch the result: *"I can help you get more clients through videos. I can help you get more sales through videos."*
- **Live [3:04:20]** (Q&A, a phone CapCut editor asking about Resolve) *"Start with what you have. Don't wait to have the biggest software."* Resolve doesn't run on a phone; get a computer when you can, but get results with the tool you have first.
- **Live [1:08:19]** Phone apps he named: CapCut, Premiere Pro mobile, Instagram's Edits app. All free to start.
- **Live [1:09:26]** *"If you want to become a pro video editor in this creator economy, DaVinci Resolve is the best."* Premiere means a subscription, or a hacked copy that you'll pay for one way or another.
- **Live [1:10:05]** The cracked Premiere, live: *"they hacked my computer and I almost lost everything I've built on my computer."*

**Subscriptions are capital, new lines**
- **Live [1:10:52]** *"A lot of video editors focus too much on getting free tools."* Free is fine at the start, but you'll have to invest. From your first money, pay for one month of CapCut Pro and make sure you land a client that month.
- **Live [1:12:41]** **"People are not willing to pay for the software. That's why they are not in a hurry to get clients."** His own subscriptions come to almost **NGN 200,000 a month**, many of them AI tools, and he earns five to ten times that. *"In business there must be input and there must be output."*
- **Live [1:13:45]** Data, electricity, software, storage, mic: these costs are why you get your first paid client fast and *"stop staying in tutorial hell."*
- **Live [1:14:19]** His laptops by price: the first **NGN 150,000** (his salary), the second **NGN 300,000** (salary plus editing money), the third **NGN 900,000** (editing money), then his PC (editing money). *"Stop spending all your video editing money on yourself. Spend it more on your tools."*
- **Live [0:44:52]** Your tools limit what you can serve: *"you can't be doing 3D motion graphics on a 2015 computer... it'll catch fire."*

**Sharing big files** (Q&A, [3:27:05]): *"Google Drive is frustrating."* His clients use **Frame.io** (paid, faster up and down; his client pays for it). WeTransfer exists but he doesn't recommend it for professional work.
