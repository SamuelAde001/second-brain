# Teachings: Visibility and personal brand

Why visibility comes first, the profile checklist, which platform, the 40/40/20 rule.

Part of the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|Teachings archive]]. From his prep voice notes (2026-10-16 to 2026-10-21, [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-teachings|prep record]]); what he added live on 2026-10-23 is marked **Live** with the time in the recording.

## Why visibility comes first (opens the section)

- For his first two or so years, *"no one ever taught me... the importance of visibility."*
- **Editing is a visible skill.** To prove you're credible you must show your work: *"That's your selling point. That's your marketing. That's your advert."*
- **His mentor's question:** *"Where can people find you? When they need you, where can people see your work?"* He had nowhere to show it. So he opened brand-new Twitter, LinkedIn and Instagram accounts.
- **Step 1, curate your feed.** He searched "video editors" and followed every major editor he found (about 50 on Twitter, many on LinkedIn and Instagram), so his feed constantly fed him editing content. *"You want to build something around a niche, you need to surround yourself with that niche."* For the next month, lock in: all your social media about video editing, especially your niche. *"A secret that most people don't know."*
- **Step 2, post your work.** He set his phone to record him working, took pictures of his timeline, screenshotted everything he did in a day, and posted **no less than 3 times a day**.
- *"That visibility is what actually made me successful."*
- *"No matter how good you are, if no one knows..."* When people think of your name, they should think of what you do. Announce it and be unashamed.
- *"You can't succeed in this industry if you are hidden."* *"Showing your work is your leverage."*

## Building your profile on social media

**Pick one platform and own it.** *"Pick one platform and focus on it."* Two if you have the capacity. Become the known person in your niche: *"If you are a real estate editor, let it be that you are the best real estate editor that can be found on Twitter."*
- His start: Twitter was home, LinkedIn the backup (he reposted everything from Twitter). He woke up on Twitter, studied new editing styles, posted his latest work, engaged with other editors, *"posting radically."*

**The profile checklist**
1. **Photo: your face.** *"People trust who they can see."* *"A lot of us hide ourselves."* Show your face, often.
2. **Name + what you do:** *"Samuel Adebayo, long form video editor."*
3. **Bio = your offer** (the 3.2 sentence): I help **who** get **what result** in **what time** through **how**. His example here: *"I help business owners get their first 10,000 views in 3 videos."* Be specific about the result, how fast and how (video editing, motion graphics).
4. **Clients you've worked with:** simple tags, e.g. "I've edited for [client]".
5. **Portfolio link in the bio, always:** where they can see all your work at once (4.4).
6. **Pinned post = your best edit**, the one you're proudest of, so the first impression is *"this guy is a professional."*
7. **Banner** (LinkedIn, Twitter): make it good.

**Not Facebook:** *"one of the worst places to build a personal brand for a video editor. You will get low ticket clients and frustrating clients."*

## Which platform suits which editor

**Beginners: LinkedIn and Twitter (X).**
- Agencies and clients search there. Big creators go to hiring agencies; the agencies search "video editor" on Twitter, find 5, 20 or 30 who look good, message them all, and give a trial to whoever replies. Individual clients do the same: *"The fastest way for me to find a video editor is LinkedIn and Twitter."*
- **Low friction:** text and pictures. Screenshot your edit, post *"check out my timeline"* or *"here's a video I did for a client."* No face, no extra editing.
- It's where you meet and build relationships with other editors (section 8).

**Instagram and TikTok: harder, but big if you put in the work.**
- You need to show your face and record yourself talking about your experiences: a personal brand.
- More friction: you're busy editing for clients, and now you also edit your own videos.
- Instagram builds two things at once, **your personal brand and a mini portfolio**: show your work, before and afters, behind the scenes, stories. *"If you can put in the work, Instagram will succeed hugely for you."*
- He has now *"fully moved from Twitter to Instagram"* to build authority and his personal brand.

**Who finds you where (his experience)**

| Platform | Clients he gets there |
|---|---|
| Twitter, LinkedIn | Mostly **international**: agencies and corporate organisations; few solo creators (it happens). He hardly got Nigerian clients, likely because he followed and engaged mostly international editors |
| TikTok | A lot of **Nigerian** DMs asking him to edit |
| Instagram | **Mixed**, international and Nigerian, but takes the most work |

**Niche and format don't decide the platform:** long form or short form, *"there are business clients on every of those platforms."*

## A personal brand as a video editor

**Why:** people need to know you're a genuine, real human. *"This is the AI age"*: people impersonate, copy others' posts and generate with ChatGPT.

**What to post: your life around video editing**, nothing else for now (no politics, no football: *"You can, but right now you are trying to build a reputation"*).
- a normal day: you working, you on a call with a client
- **your story.** His soldier-to-editor story *"always hits as bangers every single time."*
- struggles and failures, e.g. a client who bounced you
- wins: a new client, a new laptop
- challenges: *"Day one of trying to get my first client, day two, day three."*

**The 40 / 40 / 20 rule: three kinds of post**
| Type | Share | What | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authentic** | 40% | your story, timeline pictures, behind the scenes, you working, wins and failures | builds emotion |
| **Authority** | 40% | educational posts, e.g. *"Video editors, here are 3 ways to get a client"*, or for your niche: *"Business owners, these are the mistakes you make in your videos: 1, 2, 3"* | proves you know what you're doing: *"You are not just a dummy"* |
| **Sales** | 20% | *"Hey, I'm a video editor and I'm looking for a new business client"*: introduce yourself, say what you want | pitches you |

**How often:** at least **2–3 posts a day.** He posted 3 a day for 60 days, and it built his engagement.

**Followers don't matter as much as you think:** *"I only had 700 followers on my Twitter, but that 700 followers got me the best clients I needed."*

**Teaching (section 4 so far):** you can't get hired if no one can see you. Pick one platform, build a profile that says who you help and shows your best work, and post 2–3 times a day: 40% authentic, 40% authority, 20% sales.

**Examples:** his mentor's "where can people find you?" · following ~50 editors to curate his feed · 3 posts a day for 60 days · 700 Twitter followers, best clients · the soldier story as his best post · TikTok bringing Nigerian DMs.

**In his words:**
- *"You can't succeed in this industry if you are hidden."*
- *"Showing your work is your leverage."*
- *"You want to build something around a niche, you need to surround yourself with that niche."*
- *"People trust who they can see."*
- *"I only had 700 followers on my Twitter, but that 700 followers got me the best clients I needed."*

What he taught on Called to Edit Community calls. Times are from the start of each call's recording; each call's note is listed in the [[05-Knowledge/teachings/teachings-archive|archive]].

- **Call 2026-10-28 [0:48:03]** *"You might think that your outreach does not have anything to do with your posting, but let me tell you: every time you outreach, the client will try to find out who you are, and that's where your personal brand will speak for you."*
- **Call 2026-10-28 [0:48:16]** He has seen it *"over and over again"*: the client researches who you are, *"and once they see who you are and they love what you've been putting online, then they can trust you enough"* to take your outreach.

### Call 2026-10-28: edit the videos you want to be known for
- To a member with no personal brand yet: **Call 2026-10-28 [0:54:40]** *"That's your first work. People need to know you. Video editing is a visual work. People need to see your work and evidence of your work."*
- **Call 2026-10-28 [0:54:51]** *"Start putting yourself out there. Edit the videos that you want people to know you for, and share your journey."*

### Call 2026-11-01: the five core pillars of any skill
- Taught on the whiteboard as the frame for the session, for video editors and any other creative skill.
1. **Skill set**: the software *and* the principles that govern it. Mastering CapCut doesn't mean you know how to edit. *"Most of us think learning the software is learning the skill set, and that is wrong."* [0:09:53]
2. **Visibility.** **Call 2026-11-01 [0:11:30]** *"If you are good at a skill but you are not visible, you will not get clients."*
3. **Competence**: delivery, not skill. **Call 2026-11-01 [0:12:22]** *"Do we agree that a video editor can be good in his skill but bad in delivery?"* He did it well, but missed the deadline or gave excuses.
4. **Outreach**: visibility makes people come to you; outreach is you going to them, without waiting for them.
5. **Recommendation.** **Call 2026-11-01 [0:13:52]** *"You can be so good in your skill that people will start to recommend you so much that you don't even need visibility."* That's how a business with no store still sells. But **Call 2026-11-01 [0:14:24]** *"recommendation only comes based on your network"*, and *"visibility brings network."*
- Visibility is the one he started with because it's *"the forefront of your brand as a video editor"* [0:14:58]: video is visual. *"Can I tell someone that I'm a good video editor without showing them that I'm a good video editor?"* [0:17:09]

### Call 2026-11-01: don't wait until you're good to post
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:04:32]** Thinking you must get better before you post is *"the wrong strategy"*: *"if you follow that approach, number one, your growth will be slow, and number two, you would miss out on a lot of things."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:17:53]** *"Documentation of your work is the most important aspect of your credibility as a video editor."*
- **His proof, shown live on his Twitter** [0:20:00–0:23:27]: opened the account around 2024; his first post was a typography practice edit in DaVinci Resolve that got about 10 views. He wanted to be a motion designer then, so he recreated animations he saw on Pinterest and YouTube (one took two or three days), then redid one on his mentor's podcast. *"Looking at these edits right now, they are crappy."* He screen-recorded himself learning 3D in Resolve. The first time he showed his face on Twitter was a phone video of him editing on his laptop, while he was still in the military.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:21:57]** *"I wasn't posting for views. I was posting to document my journey."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:22:50]** *"If a client comes to meet you, sometimes he will scroll down to see your old work, to see your journey. And what does that do? It helps boost your credibility."*

### Call 2026-11-01: the five things to post, and they add up to credibility
- **Documentation is five kinds of post** [0:23:52–0:25:36], summed up at [0:51:02]:
  1. **Practice work**: for those still growing, the finished result of your practice.
  2. **Behind the scenes**: how you came up with the edit: your timeline, your storyboarding, Fusion nodes, a time-lapse of you editing.
  3. **Your story**: your journey.
  4. **Your knowledge**: teaching something in video editing.
  5. **Case studies**: a client liked your work, recommended it, or said *"wow, this is a good job"*: screenshot it and post it.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:26:07]** *"This credibility is the reason why clients want to work with you. Clients don't just want someone that is good. They want to see how you came up with stuff."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:29:37]** *"Our problem is that we think that doing the finished work is enough."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:51:02]** *"These are the five things I want to see in your personal brand."*

### Call 2026-11-01: behind the scenes proves the edit is yours
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:27:51]** *"We are in the day and age of AI."* **Call 2026-11-01 [0:28:02]** *"Someone will download your video edit and post it on their socials as their own video edits."* A member said it had happened to them.
- Showing your timeline, your nodes, a time-lapse is what proves the work is yours. He showed a 2024 trial video next to the post of its timeline [0:29:12].

### Call 2026-11-01: your story is your most valuable asset
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:29:44]** *"Guys, I am not joking. Storytelling has changed my video editing career entirely."* He asked who joined the community because of his story video; many said yes.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:30:27]** *"Your story is the most valuable asset of your video editing career. I repeat: your story is the most valuable asset of your video editing career."*
- A graduate starting out, someone who tried crypto or graphic design first, a phone editor, someone editing for the first time: *"these are your stories"*. **Call 2026-11-01 [0:31:21]** *"Your story is unique to you, and that is what makes you valuable."*
- **His proof** [0:31:30]: a company hired him *"not because of how good my video editing was"*: they went through his profile and saw how far he had come.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:32:02]** *"You don't need to share anything about your personal life. You just need to share everything that involves video editing."* You bought a new laptop: that's a story. The challenges you faced, the wins you've had.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:32:28]** *"Human beings relate with stories."* They see you didn't come out of nowhere.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:32:46]** *"Even if you are a good video editor in skill, your story will make it that you can enter rooms that your skill alone cannot take you."*

### Call 2026-11-01: a phone editor who documented his way to a laptop
- A case study he showed live on Instagram [0:33:06–0:38:45]: an editor he mentored last year. He came to Samuel editing on his phone only, *"he wasn't good at video editing but he was trying."* Samuel's advice: document your journey and share it.
- He posted his phone edits and the behind the scenes of editing on his phone, to about four likes. A lot of clients rejected him because he was a mobile editor. *"This guy did not relent."*
- He landed clients while still on a phone, earned enough to buy his first laptop, and is now landing bigger clients. *"His skills was not the best initially, but he kept pushing."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:37:31]** *"One thing about documentation is that it will make you better. You will get feedbacks from people."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:37:45]** Posts that don't relate to you as an editor: *"The question you ask yourself is: the thing I'm posting, does it help my brand as a video editor? Does it show people my story?"*

### Call 2026-11-01: it's documentation, not content creation
- Posting three times a day beats once a week [0:39:15]. *"You'll be wondering, how can I post three times a day? It's not possible."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:40:00]** *"Every day, there must be something you are doing in your video editing."* No client today doesn't mean no practice or no outreach. *"If every day you are doing something in your video editing career, that means every day you have something to post."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:40:23]** *"Let's not look at it like content creation. Let us look at it as documentation"*, of what you did that day in your career.
- **His proof** [0:41:06]: in a community he joined, he recorded part of every call and posted it on Twitter: *"today I learned this, this, this from this person."*
- One edit is several days of posts [0:41:38]: today you storyboard, post a picture of it; tomorrow you record yourself cutting; then the motion graphics; a new client DM'd you. Anything that affects your editing (a new computer, a new phone, data) can be documented.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:41:19]** *"Stop looking at visibility as 'I need to create content'. Look at it as you are documenting what you do every day in your journey."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:43:02]** *"There is always something to post every day, and you will never lack what to post. The only reason why you think you don't know what to post is because you didn't do anything."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:54:28]** *"This 60-day challenge is literally you doing the work and documenting the work that you are doing."*

### Call 2026-11-01: other editors are not your clients, but they boost you to them
- A member asked: most of the people who see and comment on my posts on X are video editors; will that content attract clients?
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:57:05]** *"We have clients that scout the internet"* for editors, and clients who hire agencies to find talent: *"talent hunting."*
- It works with outreach: **Call 2026-11-01 [0:57:51]** the client *"might not be sure whether you are worthy of listening to, but sometimes they will secretly check your social media. And when they check your social media and like what they see, they will respond to your outreach."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [0:59:47]** Video editors *"help boost your content for clients to see."* When editors love an edit and comment, it blows up; of all those views, not all are editors, some are potential clients. **Call 2026-11-01 [1:00:19]** *"Some of those video editors know clients that are looking for video editors. So a video editor can recommend you because your video went viral."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [1:00:47]** *"We are skilled in video editing; does not mean we are good in content creation. Content creation is another ball game."* So *"why don't you use your work to boost your post enough for potential clients to see it?"* [1:00:57]

### Call 2026-11-01: one main platform, one you just repost to
- A member on LinkedIn, X and Instagram asked whether to narrow down to one. **Call 2026-11-01 [1:04:01]** *"Yes, you should have one platform, but no, you should not have one platform."*
- **Call 2026-11-01 [1:04:16]** *"It is very, very difficult to grow any social media account."* On Twitter you don't just post, you engage: the *"comments guy"* who comments on people's posts. **Call 2026-11-01 [1:04:40]** *"It takes a lot of work to build a reputation on one platform, now talk less of multiple."*
- **His setup** [1:04:53–1:06:16]: Twitter was his major platform, where he did the grind. He reposted the same posts to LinkedIn, his secondary, without engaging there. He only opened LinkedIn because a company he wanted to work for asked for his profile. Instagram was a third account, *"but it started to burn me out trying to post everywhere"*, so he abandoned it and focused on Twitter.
- **Call 2026-11-01 [1:06:11]** *"Have a major platform, because it takes a lot of work to grow any other platform."*
