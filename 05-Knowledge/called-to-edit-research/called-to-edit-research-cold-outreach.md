## 1. What his recordings say he does

Topics 5.3 (cold DMs and emails), 5.8 (following up) and 5.10 (Loom) are **not recorded yet**, so his full cold method is not in the Brain. What he has said so far:

1. **Warm ask to someone he knew.** His mentor had just started a YouTube channel: *"I reached out to him and I said, sir, I would love to be editing your videos for you if you don't mind. And I think that move was the best move I made."*
2. **DMs to learn, not to sell.** *"I asked questions. I entered people's DMs. I got rejections, but I was learning."*
3. **A time-boxed free trial for a real person.** A friend with a business: *"would you record a video for your business? Let me help you edit it."* Or *"sir, please, can I edit your videos for one month for free? I'm trying to build my portfolio"*. Never forever, and *"Don't tell the person you're learning."*
4. **Study the client where they are first.** For business YouTubers: search YouTube, watch their videos, understand what they do (5.2, recorded in part).
5. **Agencies post on X and LinkedIn that they need an editor.**
6. **His paid clients came to him.** The USD 20 first client (*"he has been watching my stuff"*), then a USD 250-per-video agency, then Route Rise (*"they've been watching my stuff"*), plus Twitter followers recommending him.

**The blunt point:** as recorded, none of his paid clients came from a cold message to a stranger. His record is warm outreach plus inbound. That matches the data (section 2), and it means a cold-outreach lesson has to rest on the evidence below, unless he has a cold win that isn't recorded yet.

## 3. What a "good" reply rate is

The same market reads very differently depending on how replies are counted:

| Source | Sample | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| Smartlead (V) | 850M+ emails, Jan-Jun 2026 | **Median 0.74%**, top 25% 1.43%, top 10% 2.63% ([read](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)) |
| Belkins (V) | 7.5M emails, 12 months | ~0.45% per email sent, auto-replies excluded (read, via Lemlist) |
| Instantly (V) | 2025, "billions" | Average 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%+, elite 10%+, follow-ups included ([read](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)) |
| Hunter (V) | 31M emails, 2025 | Average 4.5% per sequence; sales 3%, marketing 6.2% (checked) |
| Alex (Frontal), in the AI B2B video Samuel edited, 2026 | 551 campaigns | Median below 1%; "for us 2% is a good campaign" ([[04-Projects/routerise-ai-b2b-marketing-visual-plan|AI B2B visual plan]], beats C7a-C7b) |

Rule of thumb: **per email sent, under 1% is normal; per person across a whole sequence, 3-7% is good**. Compare only figures that count replies the same way.

## 4. Cold email: the practices with the strongest evidence

1. **Small, chosen lists.** 21-50 people per sequence: 6.2%. 500+: 2.4%. 1-2 people per company: 5.1%; 3+: 3.5% (Hunter, checked).
2. **3-5 touches, not one.** One message: 3.3%. Three: 6.8% (Hunter, checked). 58% of replies come from step 1 and 42% from later steps (Instantly, read). Steps 2-6 hold 58.6% of replies and step 3 books 35.6% of meetings; after 3 silent emails, switch channel (Belkins, read). Each follow-up adds something new; "just checking in" does worst (Fuzzy, read).
3. **Custom domain, never free Gmail.** 5.2% against 2.5% (Hunter, checked). Free Gmail caps at about 500 recipients a day (unverified). His main address carries his client mail: a spam-complaint spike there is the real risk (Brand manager's inference).
4. **Plain text, open tracking off.** Tracking off: 7.4% against 4.4% (Hunter, checked). HTML emails bounced far more than plain text (Hunter, read; not a controlled test).
5. **Short first email, about 50-100 words.** Replies drop past 100 words (Gong, read). Under 50 words did best on Smartlead's first emails (1.47%). Hunter found 20-39 words best (4.5%) but the gap small.
6. **Ask for interest, not a call, on the first touch** ("worth a look?"). Gong Labs, 304,174 emails: interest asks do best cold; a specific-time ask does better once a deal is live (read; vendor, correlational).
7. **Short subject lines.** 2-4 words: 46% opens; 10 words: 34% (Belkins, 5.5M emails, read). Judge by replies: Apple and Gmail pre-fetching make open rates unreliable.
8. **20-50 sends a day per inbox**, bounces under 2%, spam complaints under 0.1-0.3% (Hunter, Smartlead, Google).
9. **Timing.** Tuesday and Wednesday, 8:30-10:30 the recipient's time (Instantly, read). 64% of replies come within an hour and 88.5% within a day (Smartlead, read).

**Rules in force:** Gmail needs SPF or DKIM, TLS and spam under 0.3% from every sender; bulk senders (about 5,000 a day) also need DMARC and one-click unsubscribe ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126), read). Yahoo matches it. Microsoft has enforced the same for senders over 5,000 a day since 2025-05-05 (unverified). **Law:** the US (CAN-SPAM) needs a true sender, an honest subject, a postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days; up to USD 53,088 per email ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), read). The UK lets you email companies without consent if you say who you are and give an opt-out; sole traders need consent ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/), read). EU: varies by country, not researched.

**Finding a creator's email:** the YouTube About tab ("View email address", behind a CAPTCHA), video descriptions, linked sites, bios. It works for only 30-40% of creators in one sample, and big channels list a manager (unverified).

## 5. Cold DMs

**LinkedIn**
- **Free accounts get 5 personalised connection notes a month** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555), checked). "Always add a note" is impossible on free.
- A note barely changes acceptance: 25.3% with, 27.6% without. It does lift replies after acceptance: 8.18% against 5.28% (Belkins (V), 112,005 sequences, read). A blank request booked 3.77% calls, an honest sales note 6.62% (La Growth Machine (V), ~9,000 leads, read).
- Average acceptance 28.5%; message reply 10.4%; connection-note reply fell from 3.5% (May 2025) to 2.2% (April 2026) (Expandi (V), 13.2M requests, read).
- **Comment or react first, DM 48-72 hours later:** 13.1% against 3.1% (Fuzzy (V), read). A profile visit plus a message: 11.87% against 4.88% (Belkins via Overloop, read).
- Limits: LinkedIn publishes no number. Vendors say about 100 invites a week, and 20-30 for accounts under 3 months old (unverified).
- Voice notes and video DMs: vendor claims only, and only to existing connections.

**X (his main inbound platform)**
- Since July 2023 messages from accounts you don't follow land in Message Requests, and X planned to limit DMs to non-followers to paid or verified accounts (read, Social Media Today). Whether an unverified account's cold DM is seen in 2026 is not confirmed. X's help pages blocked the helper.
- About 500 DMs a day for unverified accounts (unverified).

**Instagram**
- Messages from non-followers land in Requests. Photos, video and voice notes are blocked until the request is accepted; filtered requests go to Hidden Requests (unverified, Instagram help via search). The sender can't see whether a request was read (read, Inrō (V)).
- Templated text, links in the first message and bursts to many new people push messages toward Hidden Requests (Inrō, read). Reply to a Story or comment first.
- No reply-rate benchmark exists for cold DMs on X or Instagram.

**What holds across platforms:** engage publicly first so the DM isn't cold; name a specific video or post; keep it under about 400 characters; 2-3 follow-ups, 2-5 days apart, each adding something.

## 6. Video and sample outreach

- **Video in outreach: vendor data only.** The one sourced number is old: Salesloft 2018, +26% replies for video emails (via Sendspark (V), read). Vidyard's ">70% of sales leaders say video beats text" is opinion, not measurement. **Loom: no data found.**
- **Format:** a static thumbnail linking to a hosted video is fine; animated GIFs cut opens (about 36% against 40%+) and attached files hurt (Lemlist (V), read). Length 30-60 seconds (Sendspark FAQ, read).
- **Free re-edit or sample as a converter:** no data found, no creator quoted on it, nothing on copyright. A blog's claim of about 10% replies and 1% landing a job is unsourced. One creator post said *"my DMs are full"* and asked editors to reply publicly with their best work (unverified). Inference: a sample cut from their own public video, labelled as a sample and never posted publicly, carries the least risk.

## 7. The known frameworks, and what backs them

| Framework | What it says | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hormozi, *$100M Leads* | Core Four: warm outreach, free content, cold outreach, paid ads. 100 outreaches a day. Lead with value | None independent |
| Dream 100 (Chet Holmes; Brunson) | Pick ~100 ideal buyers; touch them with value every week | The author's own story (167 of 2,200 companies bought 95% of ad space); not audited |
| Josh Braun, "poke the bear" | Ask one neutral question that surfaces a problem instead of pitching | No study |
| Jason Bay, REPLY method | Persona fit and relevance | No data |
| Warm before cold | Engage, then ask | Best supported of all (Fuzzy 13.1% vs 3.1%, observational) |

## 8. Where his teaching could go further (Brand manager's read, a suggestion)

His recorded methods are on the strong side of the evidence. The parts not yet in his recordings that the data supports most:
1. **A small named list** (20-50 or a Dream 100), not mass sending.
2. **Warm it before the DM:** comment on two or three of their posts or videos, then message 2-3 days later.
3. **2-3 follow-ups that each bring something new**, then switch channel.
4. **Email from a separate domain**, plain text, tracking off.
5. **A paid test after the free month**, so free work has an exit.
6. **Know the free-LinkedIn limit** (5 notes a month): blank invite, pitch in the first DM after acceptance.
