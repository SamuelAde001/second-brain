# Website research: Real sites studied

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

I fetched 14 live sites, as of 2026-10-26, each through its homepage and at least one offer, opt-in, checkout or store page. They fall into four groups:
- Editing and motion education: Film Editing Pro, School of Motion, Motion Design School, Cinecom.
- Creator courses: Ali Abdaal's Part-Time YouTuber Academy and Tharun Speaks, an Indian video-editing cohort that includes a freelancing and client-outreach module.
- Conversion-led solo creators: Justin Welsh, Amy Porterfield, Marie Forleo, Dan Koe, and Iman Gadzhi's Monetise sales letter.
- African sellers: AltSchool Africa, Utiva, and Tayo Aina, a Nigerian creator who sells his YouTube Creator Academy on Selar.

Main findings:
1. The best sellers put the price on the button and repeat one CTA at the top, middle and bottom. Ali Abdaal's button reads "Enroll now for $995" and Tharun's reads "Buy now for ₹6,999".
2. Free content is the front door, and it is one specific magnet with its contents listed. Film Editing Pro offers 3 free lessons for first name and email, with lesson 1 playable on the opt-in page. Justin Welsh offers one 30-page guide that also signs you up to his weekly essay.
3. A closed offer becomes a waitlist with a free gift attached. B-School's page reads "SOLD OUT! Join the VIP Waitlist & Get My 2026 Success Guide!"
4. Webinar sign-up pages follow a fixed shape. Amy Porterfield's /training has a date and time picker, "In 60 minutes, you'll discover:" with 3 points, "No replay!", "Save My Seat!" three times and SMS reminder consent.
5. Pages that sell feedback and community embed live community posts and member counts. School of Motion does this; Motion Design School shows its Discord count.
6. How they show prices and pay:
   - AltSchool shows USD only: "$390 Total Cost", $30/month, $80/quarter or $200/year.
   - Utiva shows $100 to $400 per programme with "Payment plan available".
   - Neither names a payment provider on the page.
   - Tayo Aina sells in NGN only through Selar: ₦150,000, with ₦400,000 Black Friday listings still live.
7. Almost none of the 14 use an autoplay video sales letter. The $995 academy page sells with no video at all. Video appears as previews, lesson clips and video testimonials. Caveat: WebFetch reads text, so embeds and visual styling may be under-counted.
8. Trust-breaking mistakes are common and easy to avoid:
   - Broken counters: "Join 0+ Editors" (Film Editing Pro); a stats block showing "1" learners and "0 %" (AltSchool).
   - Conflicting numbers on the same site: 200,000+ vs 100,000+ graduates (AltSchool); 3,840 vs 3,349 reviews (Monetise).
   - Expired deadlines left live: Tharun's checkout still read "Last Day to Join: 15th October 2026" on 2026-10-26.
   - Empty offer pages: tayoaina.com/youtube-creator-academy/ has no copy, price or buy link.

Things I could not verify:
- aliabdaal.com returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch. Both Ali Abdaal pages were read through the r.jina.ai reader instead.
- Hayden Hillier-Smith's bootcamp (creatornow.com/haydenbootcamp) did not resolve (DNS failure on three tries).
- danielschiffer.com refused the connection.
- petermckinnon.com is now a photography portfolio with no education offer, so I excluded it.
- Iman Gadzhi's Monetise now sells digital products, not editing. I kept it only as a direct-response example.
- sahilbloom.com has no offers beyond a newsletter and book, so I excluded it.
- The shared web-search budget ran out mid-research. I could not find a Nigerian video-editing coach with a live naira-priced sales page; Tayo Aina is the closest Nigerian creator-educator.
- Coachli's 2026 Nigerian workshop guide (https://www.coachli.co/blog/how-to-run-a-paid-online-workshop-in-nigeria-2026-guide, 2026-04-27) is from a platform that sells checkout, so treat it as promotional. It gives seat-price ranges of ₦5,000–₦15,000 (2-hour intro) and ₦15,000–₦35,000 (3–4-hour masterclass), and warns that manual bank transfer plus WhatsApp confirmation loses sales.

## Patterns

- **Price inside the button; one CTA repeated at top, middle and bottom** (Ali Abdaal's academy ('Enroll now for $995' at hero, price box and close); Tharun Speaks checkout ('Buy now for ₹6,999'); Amy Porterfield /training ('Save My Seat!' x3); School of Motion ('Join All-Access' in header, hero and paths)). The visitor never scrolls to find out 'how much', each click is pre-qualified, and every scroll depth has a way to checkout For Called to Edit: Every standalone page has one button with the price in it: 'Join the Community — ₦5,000/month', 'Get the recording — ₦10,000', 'Book the call — ₦50,000'. It repeats after the hero, after the proof and after the FAQ. On phones, a sticky bottom bar (price + button) appears once the hero scrolls away.
- **One specific free magnet as the front door: contents listed, preview shown, delivery that enrols the visitor in a recurring send** (Film Editing Pro (3 free video lessons for First Name + Email; lesson 1 playable on the opt-in page; run-times listed); Justin Welsh (30-page guide with 7 numbered 'Capacities', which also subscribes you to The Saturday Essay); Dan Koe ('Receive 2 free letters a week'); Tayo Aina (newsletter is the main CTA, 'Join 112,458+ members')). A cheap yes. A numbered contents list makes a free item feel worth an email, and the recurring send turns a one-off download into a list you can sell to For Called to Edit: /resources leads with ONE guide (e.g. 'How to outreach'), its numbered contents shown, and an email-only form (first name optional). The thank-you page has the download button straight away, so delivery doesn't depend on Gmail or the Promotions tab in in-app browsers. The same email gets the PDF and joins a named, scheduled Called to Edit letter. The other guides are offered on the thank-you page, not all at once.
- **A closed offer is a waitlist with a gift, never a dead end** (Marie Forleo B-School ('B-School is SOLD OUT!' / 'Join the VIP Waitlist & Get My 2026 Success Guide!'); Film Editing Pro ('JOIN THE WAITLIST', 'COMING WINTER 2026'); Motion Design School ('Register for the pre-sale'); Monetise ('[JOIN WAITLIST GROUP]')). The traffic still turns into emails between launches, and the waitlist becomes the first, warmest launch audience For Called to Edit: /academy shows each cohort (Resolve: beginner to Fusion nodes first; branding, outreach and career later) with its own waitlist form, tagged by cohort in the Sheet. The gift is useful today (e.g. a Resolve shortcut sheet or lesson 1). The page promises waitlisters hear first and get the early-bird or deposit option, but only if Samuel decides that.
- **Fixed anatomy for the webinar registration page** (Amy Porterfield /training: 'Choose your day and time:' -> host credential line -> 'No replay!' -> 'In 60 minutes, you'll discover:' (3 numbered points) -> founder story -> who-it's-for list -> 3-question FAQ (fit, format, replay) -> 'Save My Seat!' x3 -> SMS reminder consent). Every common doubt (when, how long, what will I get, is it for me, is there a replay) is answered before the button. Reminders protect show-up rates. For Called to Edit: /webinar runs: eyebrow 'LIVE · 60 MINUTES' -> one-topic outcome headline -> 3 numbered things you'll learn -> date and time in WAT plus the visitor's local time worked out in JS (Ghana, Kenya, India, US) -> 'Save my seat — ₦5,000' -> proof '78 editors paid for the first one' -> who it's for / not for -> FAQ (replay? phone OK? paying from outside Nigeria?) -> optional WhatsApp reminder checkbox. Use 'No replay' only if true; if the recording becomes the ₦10,000 product, saying so is honest urgency.
- **Route by 'where are you now?' instead of a menu of products** (Ali Abdaal 'How Can I Help You?' (4 intent cards); AltSchool (4 audience doors + quiz 'Free · No signup required · Takes 3 minutes'); Utiva (3-step finder 'Which future sounds most like you?')). Visitors pick their own situation, not a product name, and land on the one offer that fits. It shrinks choice overload on a ladder with 6+ offers. For Called to Edit: The homepage has 3–4 large tap targets: 'I can edit but have no clients' -> /resources (outreach guide) or /webinar; 'I have clients, I want better pay and accountability' -> /community; 'I want to master DaVinci Resolve' -> /academy; 'I want Samuel 1:1' -> /mentorship. Unlike Utiva ('No signup. No lead form.'), each landing offers its matching free resource for an email.
- **The whole ladder in one card template, each card with price and status** (Dan Koe (thumbnail, title, one-liner, one button for every product); Utiva cards (duration · format · schedule · next cohort · tuition · 'Payment plan available'); Marie Forleo nav ('Available Now 🔥' badges); Tharun tiers ('Everything In Essential')). Same-shaped cards make the offers easy to compare. Status chips show what can be bought today and what needs a waitlist. For Called to Edit: One homepage section, 'Ways to work with me': Free guides / Mini webinars ₦5,000 / Recordings & e-book ₦10,000 / Community ₦5,000 a month / Academy ₦50,000 per cohort / Mentorship ₦50,000 call or ₦500,000 month. Each card: name, one-line outcome, price, a status chip (Open / Waitlist / Next: date), and one button to its standalone URL.
- **Proof that is quantified and tagged by starting point** (Ali Abdaal's academy case studies ('complete beginner ↓', 'intermediate, stuck ↓', 'already advanced ↓', each with subscribers and months); Tayo Aina testimonials (each creator's subscriber count + 'have generated $5,000'); Tharun Speaks (Prachi: $800 for one video from a foreign client); Film Editing Pro (job titles, 'I landed a job in LA!')). Visitors find someone who started where they are, and a number with a timeframe reads as real For Called to Edit: Testimonial card: photo, name, city or country, 'Before:' line, result in clients, ₦ or $, with the timeframe, and a link to the person's IG or portfolio. Collect these this week from the 78 webinar buyers and the first community members. The Route Rise video testimonial goes near the hero as 'the coach is a working editor'.
- **Paid counts beat follower counts; never show a weak, broken or conflicting number** (Strong: Justin Welsh 'Read by over 200,000', Dan Koe '196,000+', Tayo Aina '112,458+'. Broken: Film Editing Pro 'Join 0+ Editors'; AltSchool stats '1' learners / '0 %'; AltSchool 200,000+ vs 100,000+; Monetise 3840+ vs 3349 reviews). One wrong or contradictory figure undoes the rest of the proof, especially with a skeptical, scam-wary audience For Called to Edit: Lead with '78 editors paid to attend the first webinar' (paid proof beats a follower count while the list is small). Keep every figure in one shared constants file so all six URLs show the same number. Show a live member count (from the Discord bot via Netlify Blobs) only above a threshold Samuel sets.
- **Sell feedback and a live room, and show the room working** (School of Motion (hero promises 'working pros tearing apart your work'; 'Unlimited Critiques'; embedded community feed; world map of member avatars; '4,500+ members learning right now'); Motion Design School ('Learn Skills → Get Certificate → Win Competitions → Get a Job'; '6200+ Already joined' Discord); Utiva ('Build evidence of what you can do')). Content is free on YouTube; feedback, accountability and a visible group are what people pay for monthly For Called to Edit: /community headline sells the weekly live call and feedback on your edits and outreach, not 'resources'. Show 3–6 real, consented screenshots from the Discord wins and feedback channels, the next live call date and time in WAT, and what happened last week.
