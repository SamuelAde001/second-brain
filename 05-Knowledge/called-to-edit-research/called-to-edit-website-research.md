# Called to Edit website: research (2026-10-26)

Reference research for [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-website|the Called to Edit website]], run by the Brand manager on Samuel's ask (*"Do research on, don't rely on just your knowledge"*): seven researchers, then adversarial fact-checks on email, payments and infrastructure. Each part lists its findings with sources and as-of dates. **Fact-check corrections override the findings above them.** Kept apart from his teachings, per his 2026-10-19 rule.

Back to [[03-Areas/called-to-edit/called-to-edit|Called to Edit]]


## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-conversion|Conversion: what makes a creator-education site sell]]

The evidence points to one clear homepage design: one primary free offer above the fold, then a short "where are you?" router with three paths. A store-style grid of six offers is the wrong approach. The top creator-educator homepages checked today (Justin Welsh, Amy Porterfield, Marie Forleo) all lead with a free guide, training or video and put paid offers lower down. NN/g's eyetracking found 57% of viewing time goes to the first screen and 74% to the first two. Choice-overload research says too many options hurt most when the buyer is unsure what they want, which describes beginner editors. Unbounce's 2024 data for education pages favours short, plain copy: about 363 words at the median-converting page, and reading level at or below 12th grade converting 54% to over 2x better. About 85% of that traffic was mobile, and the top Nigerian phone width is 360 px (StatCounter, July 2026). Show every price in NGN, including the NGN 500,000. Hiding prices reads as evasive (NN/g), and Stripe reports 76% of buyers pick their local currency when offered. Put the strongest proof next to the most expensive offers, because reviews lift higher-priced items more (Spiegel: 380% vs 190%). Urgency should only ever be real. A 2022 meta-analysis (131 studies) found time limits work best for high-involvement purchases like a cohort, and the UK has fined fake timers under the DMCC Act since April 2025. No credible waitlist-to-buyer benchmark exists. Vendor guides say 3-7% for passive lists and 15-25% for engaged ones; a real 442-person cohort waitlist converted 2%. The best signal is engagement before launch: Maven buyers who attended 5+ free lessons were 15x more likely to buy. The case for a VSL is weak. Unbounce's study of about 35,000 pages found pages with embedded video converted lower than pages without, on mobile and desktop. Engagement falls with length (Wistia 2025: 50% under 1 min, 21% over 30 min). A VSL should therefore be an optional click-to-play facade with a face poster, captions and the length shown, and the page must sell without it. For the NGN 500,000 mentorship, the B2B data supports a short application with the calendar shown right after it. No independent study compares application forms with direct booking for coaching. With thin proof, show specific, consented proof (paid attendance, verbatim quotes, the Route Rise video) rather than small raw counts, which NN/g found can backfire. My web search budget ran out before I could check anchoring meta-analyses, Nigeria's FCCPC rules on urgency, and Flutterwave's multi-currency display.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-inspiration|Real sites studied]]

I fetched 14 live sites, as of 2026-10-26, each through its homepage and at least one offer, opt-in, checkout or store page. They fall into four groups:
- Editing and motion education: Film Editing Pro, School of Motion, Motion Design School, Cinecom.
- Creator courses: Ali Abdaal's Part-Time YouTuber Academy and Tharun Speaks, an Indian video-editing cohort that includes a freelancing and client-outreach module.
- Conversion-led solo creators: Justin Welsh, Amy Porterfield, Marie Forleo, Dan Koe, and Iman Gadzhi's Monetise sales letter.
- African sellers: AltSchool Africa, Utiva, and Tayo Aina, a Nigerian creator who sells his YouTube Creator Academy on Selar.

Main findings:
1. The best sellers put the price on the button and repeat one CTA at the top, middle and bottom. Ali Abdaal's button reads "Enroll now for $995" and Tharun's reads "Buy now for ₹6,999".
2. Free content is the front door, and it is one specific magnet with its contents listed. Film Editing Pro offers 3 free lessons for first name and email, with lesson 1 playable on the opt-in page. Justin Welsh offers one 30-page guide that also signs you up to his weekly essay.
3. A closed offer becomes a waitlist with a free gift attached. B-School's page reads "SOLD OUT! Join the VIP Waitlist & Get My 2026 Success Guide!"
4. Webinar sign-up pages follow a fixed shape. Amy Porterfield's /training has a date and time picker, "In 60 minutes, you'll discover:" with 3 points, "No replay!", "Save My Seat!" three times and SMS reminder consent.
5. Pages that sell feedback and community embed live community posts and member counts. School of Motion does this; Motion Design School shows its Discord count.
6. How they show prices and pay:
   - AltSchool shows USD only: "$390 Total Cost", $30/month, $80/quarter or $200/year.
   - Utiva shows $100 to $400 per programme with "Payment plan available".
   - Neither names a payment provider on the page.
   - Tayo Aina sells in NGN only through Selar: ₦150,000, with ₦400,000 Black Friday listings still live.
7. Almost none of the 14 use an autoplay video sales letter. The $995 academy page sells with no video at all. Video appears as previews, lesson clips and video testimonials. Caveat: WebFetch reads text, so embeds and visual styling may be under-counted.
8. Trust-breaking mistakes are common and easy to avoid:
   - Broken counters: "Join 0+ Editors" (Film Editing Pro); a stats block showing "1" learners and "0 %" (AltSchool).
   - Conflicting numbers on the same site: 200,000+ vs 100,000+ graduates (AltSchool); 3,840 vs 3,349 reviews (Monetise).
   - Expired deadlines left live: Tharun's checkout still read "Last Day to Join: 15th October 2026" on 2026-10-26.
   - Empty offer pages: tayoaina.com/youtube-creator-academy/ has no copy, price or buy link.

Things I could not verify:
- aliabdaal.com returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch. Both Ali Abdaal pages were read through the r.jina.ai reader instead.
- Hayden Hillier-Smith's bootcamp (creatornow.com/haydenbootcamp) did not resolve (DNS failure on three tries).
- danielschiffer.com refused the connection.
- petermckinnon.com is now a photography portfolio with no education offer, so I excluded it.
- Iman Gadzhi's Monetise now sells digital products, not editing. I kept it only as a direct-response example.
- sahilbloom.com has no offers beyond a newsletter and book, so I excluded it.
- The shared web-search budget ran out mid-research. I could not find a Nigerian video-editing coach with a live naira-priced sales page; Tayo Aina is the closest Nigerian creator-educator.
- Coachli's 2026 Nigerian workshop guide (https://www.coachli.co/blog/how-to-run-a-paid-online-workshop-in-nigeria-2026-guide, 2026-04-27) is from a platform that sells checkout, so treat it as promotional. It gives seat-price ranges of ₦5,000–₦15,000 (2-hour intro) and ₦15,000–₦35,000 (3–4-hour masterclass), and warns that manual bank transfer plus WhatsApp confirmation loses sales.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-email|Lead capture, email platform and Nigerian data law]]

I recommend MailerLite, starting on Free and moving to Comfort once the list passes 250. At 1k, 5k and 10k subscribers it costs about half what Kit charges (Comfort USD 19 / 49 / 89 a month against Kit Creator USD 39 / 89 / 139). It has a documented upsert API with consent fields (opted_in_at, optin_ip), up to 50 automations on Comfort, and it requires an authenticated sending domain, so mail goes out as samuel@calledtoedit.com. Two catches. First, MailerLite cut its free plan in June 2026 to 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month and 3 automations, so Samuel will pay almost at once. Second, Comfort caps sends at 10 times the tier ceiling, so 10,000 emails a month at the 1k tier, and a webinar launch month can go over it. Kit is the fallback if MailerLite turns the account down. Its official Free plan carries no sequences or automations (one third-party review says it has one of each, so check). I found no published country ban for Nigeria at any of the 8 tools. MailerLite and Brevo do vet accounts automatically and can reject them without giving a reason, so apply only after calledtoedit.com is live with a privacy page and a working opt-in form. Paying in USD is no longer the obstacle it was: naira card limits rose sharply in 2026 (GTBank USD 40,000 a quarter from August 2026), and the Cleva USD card is a backup. For capture, use one field: Sleeknote's 2026 data shows 4.41% conversion with one field, 2.90% with two and 1.93% with three. On phones, popups converted better than on desktop (5.60% vs 2.86%). Exit intent does not fire on touch screens, so phones should get a small teaser bar that opens a form on tap (Wisepops measured 28.79% for click-triggered popups) rather than a full-screen popup on entry, which Google advises against. A quiz such as 'What's stopping you from getting clients?' on its own landing page is the strongest magnet I found data for (ConvertFlow, September 2026: 11.28% of visitors become leads, against an 8.4% median for education landing pages in Unbounce's 2023-24 data). Ask for WhatsApp on the thank-you page, never in the main form, with its own unticked box: Nigerian commentary on the GAID reads it as requiring express consent for direct marketing, and Meta has its own opt-in rules. Legal minimum: affirmative opt-in wording, a consent log, a privacy and cookie notice, an unsubscribe link in every email that takes effect at once (NDPA s.36), and no ad pixels until a visitor accepts. The DCPMI registration thresholds are reported inconsistently (200 data subjects in six months in the 2024 notice; 2,500 to 9,999 for the lowest tier in GAID commentary) and need confirming with the NDPC. One operational risk: Apps Script on a consumer Gmail account can email only 100 recipients a day, which the 78-buyer webinar already came close to. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few points below are marked as not checked.

**Fact-check:** 1) MailerLite stays the pick, but restate the cost case. 'Half of Kit' holds only at 1k on Comfort. Comfort caps sends at 10x the tier and Kit Creator does not, so in a launch month the fair comparison is MailerLite Power against Kit Creator: USD 39 vs 39 at 1k, 69 vs 89 at 5k, 129 vs 139 at 10k. Bill monthly, not yearly, until the launch send pattern is known, so he can move between Comfort and Power. 2) Drop the claim that MailerLite refuses Gmail sign-ups; a Gmail login is allowed. The real rule is that the sender address must be on calledtoedit.com and authenticated after the 14-day trial. Also note Free support ends after 14 days and prices exclude VAT. 3) Replace the Wisepops 28.79% with the current 54.42% labelled 'self-selected, not comparable', or drop it, and rest the teaser-bar case on Sleeknote's teaser data (4.54% vs 2.74%). Change 'under ~300 words' to Unbounce's ~363. Present the quiz as one strong option, not 'the strongest': Unbounce's 18.3% median for online-course pages beats it, and ConvertFlow reports means, not medians. 4) Rewrite the DCPMI item. Use NDPC Schedule 7 (Updated Guidance Notice, 2024-12-19, built into GAID Art. 8-9): OHL covers more than 200 data subjects in 6 months (NGN 10,000 a year, renewed annually), EHL 1,000 to 4,999 (NGN 100,000), UHL over 5,000 (NGN 250,000), and the Education sector and 'Corporate Training Service Providers' are designated outright. Samuel likely qualifies now. Plan to register as OHL through services.ndpc.gov.ng (cheap), name a DPO, and have a licensed DPCO confirm whether the small-trader exclusion (para 4(b)) applies. Also add GAID Art. 7(b) and 7(e): a compliance audit within 15 months of starting business, then yearly, and semi-annual internal data protection reports. 5) Rewrite the cookie item. GAID Art. 7(l) and 19 require a cookie notice on the homepage with accept/decline. It must sit centre, left or right, never at the bottom, be visible without scrolling, and disclose every tracker in the first screen. Non-necessary cookies need an explicit yes/no. Even with cookieless analytics, ship a small conspicuous notice rather than 'skip the banner', and keep the email teaser bar from colliding with it. Keep pixels off until accepted. 6) Webinar highlights magnet: GAID Art. 20(8)(a) bars using images of event participants 'for profit or commerce-oriented advertisement without the express consent of the data subject'. Cut attendee faces, names and voices from the Google Meet recording, or get written consent. 7) Re-permission of the 78 buyers: Art. 18(1)(a) requires consent for 'any direct marketing activity', and the NDPA has no UK-style soft opt-in. A Gmail note offering a free highlights cut is itself arguably direct marketing. Safer: put the opt-in inside a transactional or service message buyers expect (access or replay delivery) and log only the opt-ins it brings in. 8) Fix the bank-limit claim to 'reported 2026-09-07' and remove the dead Cleva help link.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-payments-africa|Payments: Nigeria and the rest of Africa]]

Keep Flutterwave as the main rail, but your account is probably set up wrong for foreign buyers. Flutterwave treats any card issued outside your sign-up country as international. A Nigerian merchant has to ask for that access (Settings > Business Preferences > Payment Methods > request access, decision promised within 48 hours). So the Ghana and Zimbabwe buyers were most likely blocked at the merchant side, not just by their banks. Check the failed transactions in the dashboard to be sure. Flutterwave's official Nigeria rates are 2.0% local, with no cap shown, and 4.8% international (it was 3.8% until 2024-11-11), plus 7.5% VAT on fees. By default the customer pays the fee on top, which puts a total like NGN 5,107.50 in front of the buyer. Switch that off. Paystack Nigeria charges 1.5% + NGN 100 (the NGN 100 is waived under NGN 2,500), capped at NGN 2,000. International cards are 3.9% + NGN 100, and dedicated virtual accounts are 1% capped at NGN 300. Paystack also offers PalmPay, OPay and Google Pay. But Paystack's mobile money only works for businesses in Ghana, Kenya and Côte d'Ivoire, so it cannot solve the African cross-border problem. Flutterwave's checkout can show Ghana, Kenya and Uganda mobile money when the charge currency is GHS, KES or UGX. Whether a Nigerian Flutterwave account is allowed to collect in those currencies is NOT confirmed anywhere public: test it with a small live charge and ask support. Zimbabwe has no mobile money rail on any of these gateways. Treat it as a USD card country with a fallback. On cost, Flutterwave is cheaper for the NGN 5,000 and 10,000 items. Paystack is cheaper above about NGN 15,000-20,000, and far cheaper on the NGN 500,000 mentorship (about NGN 2,000 against about NGN 10,750). Add Paystack as a second rail for three things: high-ticket NGN sales, transfer-based community renewals through a dedicated account per member, and failover. Leave cross-border on Flutterwave. Add a hosted fallback (Selar) for digital products when a foreign card fails. Choose currency and payment methods by visitor country, which Netlify Functions already provide (context.geo.country.code), and let the buyer override the country. Register Called to Edit with CAC: Paystack Starter accounts have a lifetime cap of NGN 8,000,000 and USD 5,000, and Flutterwave lists missing documents as a top reason for refusing international access. Caveat: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Flutterwave virtual-account behaviour, Paystack dedicated-account eligibility, Paystack chargeback rules, Bumpa, Paystack Storefront, OPay merchant checkout and Indian recurring-card rules could not be checked and are listed as open questions.

**Fact-check:** The core plan stands: fix Flutterwave first, route by country, add Paystack for high-ticket NGN sales, add a fallback. The checks force these changes.

1) CAC registration becomes a requirement, not an option. Paystack dedicated virtual accounts are 'only available to Nigeria-based registered businesses' (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2124866). The transfer-renewal plan through those accounts doesn't work on a Starter account. Register 'Called to Edit' before opening Paystack, or drop the virtual-account renewal and use only the emailed checkout link.

2) Plan for Paystack's 16-hour chargeback window, not Flutterwave's 1-2 days. Paystack gives Nigerian businesses 16 business hours, and a missed deadline means automatic acceptance and a full refund (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2127234). The mentorship and Academy sales would sit on Paystack, so the proof-of-delivery log (access email, Discord role, call attendance, keyed to the transaction reference) must be written automatically at grant time. The disputes email set in the Paystack dashboard must reach Samuel's phone. Build the evidence logging before taking the first NGN 500,000 sale on Paystack.

3) Prepare Paystack's international request for digital products. It wants a 'Valid website or social media page showing where the ebooks or digital products are sold' and full compliance documents (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2127682). The calledtoedit.com product pages, refund policy and contact details should be live before applying.

4) Rewrite the Zimbabwe explanation. Don't call it sanctions: the US Zimbabwe programme ended on 2024-03-04 (https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20240304). Treat it as issuer or provider risk filtering and keep the fallback.

5) Add wallet routes the researcher left out. Enable Apple Pay in Flutterwave (Settings > Business Preferences > Payment Methods > Wallets; https://flutterwave.com/ng/support/payment-methods/apple-pay-frequently-asked-questions-faqs) for US/UK iPhone and Safari visitors. Paystack Google Pay also takes foreign customers once international payments are on. The exact Flutterwave payment_options value for Apple Pay is unverified, so test it.

6) Fix the onboarding list (the meter number is for a sole proprietorship, not Starter). Ask for limit increases through dashboard support, not the flutterwavego.com address.

7) Selar: drop '4% + NGN 50' (the cited source doesn't support it) and treat every Selar fee as unknown until read in the dashboard.

8) Checkout copy: Flutterwave's transfer account expires in 1 hour (https://flutterwave.com/ng/support/payment-methods/pay-with-bank-transfer-pwbt). Paystack's expires in 30 minutes, and late or wrong amounts are auto-refunded (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2128642). Show the time limit and 'send the exact amount'.

Netlify context.geo.country.code and context.ip are confirmed (https://docs.netlify.com/functions/api/). The fee crossover (about NGN 15,400-20,000) and the per-sale cost examples are arithmetically right.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-payments-global|Payments: India and the rest of the world]]

This setup needs three rails, and the cheapest working version needs no US company. (1) Nigerians: keep Flutterwave (NGN bank transfer and cards, with the card-saving auto-renew already built). Add Paystack only for big NGN tickets: its local fee is capped at NGN 2,000 and its dedicated-virtual-account transfers at NGN 300. Flutterwave charges a flat 2% plus 7.5% VAT on the fee, and its page shows no cap, so a NGN 500,000 mentorship costs about NGN 10,750 there. (2) Other Africans: still Flutterwave, but charge in the buyer's own currency (GHS, KES, ZAR, UGX, RWF, TZS) so Ghana mobile money and M-Pesa appear. Flutterwave hides those methods when you charge in NGN, which is probably part of why the Ghana buyer failed. Flutterwave has no Zimbabwe method, so Zimbabwe goes to the USD rail. (3) Rest of world: Polar.sh as merchant of record for the e-book, recorded masterclasses and the USD community. Polar pays out to Nigerian bank accounts through Stripe, handles VAT/GST, auto-grants Discord roles, and has clean webhooks. Polar, Lemon Squeezy, Paddle, Dodo and Stripe Managed Payments all ban 1:1 coaching, so foreign mentorship buyers pay by Flutterwave or Paystack international card. Lemon Squeezy also pays to Nigeria, but it says it is moving users to Stripe Managed Payments, which does not accept Nigeria-based businesses or coaching. Paddle is built for software and bans coaching and community access. Dodo Payments no longer onboards Nigerians. dLocal Go accepts Nigerian merchants but has no Indian buyers. Gumroad's Stripe payout list leaves out Nigeria. UPI for India: of the options checked, only Stripe through a US company (Stripe Atlas) clearly allows a Nigerian-owned business to take it (1.8% + 30c, plus 1.5% international and 1% FX; AutoPay up to INR 15,000). That costs USD 500 at setup, then USD 100 a year for the registered agent, USD 400 a year Delaware LLC tax (Delaware's own figure; Atlas's page still says 300), and a yearly Form 5472 that carries a USD 25,000 penalty if missed. Stripe direct is only about 1.1% + 20c cheaper per sale than Polar, so the LLC pays off through UPI and through selling coaching to foreigners, not through lower fees. Until then, sell Indian buyers one-time multi-month passes, not auto-renew: RBI rules make recurring charges on Indian cards fragile (24-hour pre-debit notice, extra check above INR 15,000, cards domestic-only by default). Routing: Netlify geolocation (context.geo.country.code) picks the default currency and rail on each offer page. A visible 'Paying from Nigeria / elsewhere' switch overrides it. Never auto-redirect the offer URLs. Take the email before checkout and pass it in. The web search budget ran out partway through, so later checks used official pages fetched directly. Selar, Mainstack, Whop's Nigeria payouts, PayPal-through-Paga merchant receiving, Stan, Teachable and Thinkific payouts stay partly unverified.

**Fact-check:** The three-rail design stands: Flutterwave as the NGN core, local-currency Flutterwave for other African countries, a merchant of record for the rest of the world, and geo-default plus a manual switch with no redirects. Rail 3 is weaker than the findings present it, and five things should change.

1) Narrow Polar to what its policy clearly allows: recorded masterclasses sold as courses. Do NOT plan the community, live Academy cohorts, early-bird deposits/waitlists or paid webinar seats on Polar. Polar prohibits 'community access' and 'human services' and restricts 'Ticket sales', 'Pre-orders and paid waitlists' and 'eBooks'. The e-book can go to Lemon Squeezy (eBooks explicitly acceptable; pays out to Nigeria; no shutdown date announced) or to Polar after a written review.

2) Foreign buyers of the community, cohorts and mentorship default to Flutterwave international card (4.8% + 7.5% VAT on the fee) or Paystack international (3.9% + NGN 100). Sell fixed-term passes, not renewals, until a mandate-capable processor exists.

3) In the Flutterwave dashboard, check the fee-bearer setting. The default makes customers pay the fee on top of the price. Decide deliberately, and only then compare against Paystack's NGN 2,000 cap.

4) Before paying for Atlas, submit Razorpay's free foreign-business form for UPI, cards and netbanking from Indian buyers, settled abroad. Atlas is only worth its cost if Razorpay refuses. The cost case for Atlas is weaker than stated: a foreign-card subscription on Stripe direct with Billing (0.7%) and Tax (0.5%) costs about 6.6% + 30c, against Polar's 6.5% + 50c. Fees are a wash, so Atlas is justified only by UPI and coaching. Fixed costs are about USD 500 a year plus an accountant, and the USD 25,000 Form 5472 penalty can double if it runs past 90 days after IRS notice.

5) Use Polar's documented currencies. INR, KES, ZAR and NGN prices are possible (GHS is not), and checkouts created server-side must pass customer_ip_address or the currency will be wrong.

Also, before launch, confirm the Netlify plan. On the credit-based Free plan (300 credits, hard limit, 15 credits per production deploy), running out pauses every site, including checkout and webhook functions. Move to a paid credit plan with auto-recharge, or confirm the account is on a legacy plan.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-infra|Infrastructure: domain, mailbox, sending, video, booking, analytics]]

Simplest robust setup: merge the two Netlify sites into one site at calledtoedit.com, with each offer on its own path (/webinar, /community, /academy, /mentorship, /resources). Use Netlify DNS so the bare domain is fast and SSL is automatic. Keep video, mail and booking on specialist services, called from Netlify Functions.
Merging beats both alternatives. A proxy rewrite (/community/* to the other site) only works inside one Netlify team, allows one hop and times out after 26 s. You also can't 301 the proxied site's .netlify.app address without causing a redirect loop. A subdomain splits cookies, Blobs, env vars and webhooks across two sites. Blobs belong to one site but can be read from another with siteID plus a token, so community data can be moved safely.
Before launch, check whether the Netlify team is on a legacy plan or the new credit plan. On the credit Free plan, every production deploy costs 15 of 300 monthly credits and bandwidth costs 20 credits per GB. When credits run out, every site is paused. Agents that deploy often would hit that limit; the Personal plan is USD 9 a month for 1,000 credits.
Free branded email: ImprovMX forwards samuel@ to the Gmail he already uses (free tier: 500 forwards a day). Gmail 'Send mail as' then sends through Brevo's or Resend's SMTP, which signs mail with calledtoedit.com's DKIM. Sending through Gmail's own servers fails DMARC alignment and can show 'on behalf of'. Zoho Free (no IMAP, select data centres only) and Google Workspace are the paid or real-mailbox upgrades.
Move customer emails off Apps Script. A personal Gmail account can only send to 100 recipients a day through it, which a webinar day can exceed. Brevo's free plan allows 300 a day, Resend's 100 a day (3,000 a month).
For paid recordings, use Bunny Stream: no encoding fees, a Lagos PoP, Africa delivery at USD 0.06/GB, and signed embed tokens. Mux is a strong alternative because its first 100,000 delivery minutes each month are free. YouTube unlisted and Google Drive can't stop link sharing.
No scheduler (Cal.com, Calendly, TidyCal, Google) takes Paystack or Flutterwave payments. Stripe in Nigeria routes to Paystack, so use pay first, then book: the Flutterwave webhook calls the Cal.com API to create a single-use private booking link.
Analytics: Umami Cloud free (one website, 100k events a month, fits a merged site), plus Microsoft Clarity, plus a server-side funnel log. Add Meta Pixel and Conversions API only when ads start, deduplicated by a shared event_id.
WebSearch's shared budget ran out mid-research. Vimeo pricing, Nigeria-specific Zoho and Google prices, Brevo's daily-cap details and Gmail's late-2025 enforcement changes could not be verified and are listed as open questions.

**Fact-check:** The core architecture stands and every infra number it rests on checked out: one merged Netlify site at calledtoedit.com, Netlify DNS with the apex as primary, and the proxy, Blobs, function, credit and Bunny figures. Five things must change.

1) The email recommendation (rec 3) must be rebuilt. Gmail 'Send mail as' for non-Google addresses is being removed in January 2027 (support.google.com/mail/answer/17101213), and Gmail may already refuse new setups during Q3-Q4 2026, so ImprovMX plus 'Send as' through Brevo or Resend is a dead end. Replace it with one of these:
(a) Google Workspace Business Starter (EUR 6.12 intro / EUR 6.80 seen; check the NGN price). It keeps the Gmail app, signs DKIM as calledtoedit.com, and lifts Apps Script MailApp to 1,500 recipients a day. It best fits a stack already built on Apps Script.
(b) Zoho Mail, at NGN 0 if Forever Free is offered in his data centre (Zoho app only, no IMAP), or Lite (annual only) added to the Gmail Android app over IMAP/SMTP, which Google says keeps working. Check that Lite includes IMAP first.
ImprovMX can stay only for receive-only aliases that forward into the real mailbox.

2) Webhook handler: confirm whether the Flutterwave keys are v3 or v4. v3 sends the secret as plain text in verif-hash. v4 sends an HMAC-SHA256 of the payload in 'flutterwave-signature'. Support both, and keep the server-side verify call. Add a scheduled reconciliation job that lists recent Flutterwave transactions and grants any missed access. Retries stop after 3 tries 30 minutes apart, so a Netlify pause (credit Free out of credits) or a bad deploy longer than about 90 minutes loses paid events for good.

3) Blobs migration: the cross-site token is a Netlify Personal Access Token with account-wide reach. Samuel creates it and sets it as a temporary env var. The agent never sees it, and it is revoked right after the copy.

4) Pay-then-book: before writing code, confirm that Cal.com's free plan allows API keys and private links. The pricing page lists APIs only under paid tiers, and Teams is USD 12/user/month yearly. Keep the manual one-off-link fallback ready.

5) ESP: Brevo's 300/day could not be confirmed from any Brevo page. Read the real cap in the dashboard after sign-up before making Brevo the main sender. Resend's numbers are confirmed, but its 100/day is no better than personal-Gmail Apps Script. If Workspace is chosen in point 1, MailApp's 1,500/day covers transactional mail during launch, and the ESP is needed mainly for list broadcasts with one-click unsubscribe.

## [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research-performance-ux|Phones, speed, light/dark and UX]]

Design for Nigeria's 75th-percentile mobile user, not the median. Alex Russell's November 2025 estimate puts Nigeria's P75 connection at 3.1 Mbps down, 1 Mbps up and 190 ms round trip (RTT), the slowest of the countries he lists. Ookla's 44.14 Mbps median for December 2025 overstates what most visitors get. The audience is 75% mobile and 84% Android (StatCounter, Sept 2026): Tecno, Samsung, Infinix, Xiaomi and Itel phones, Opera at about 18% of mobile browsing, and about 43% of screens 360 px wide. Google's field data (CrUX, which feeds PageSpeed Insights) excludes Android WebViews (the Instagram and TikTok in-app browsers) and Opera. So Samuel's real audience is invisible in PageSpeed Insights, and the site must measure itself with the small web-vitals library. At 190 ms RTT, each new third-party origin costs roughly 570 ms just to connect (my estimate: about 3 round trips). Hitting LCP under 2.5 s therefore needs a critical path of about 170 KB or less and zero third-party origins before the first tap. A full page under about 400 KB is about one fifth of the 2025 median mobile home page (2,164 KB). A concrete font finding: Google Fonts' 'latin' subset does not include the naira sign (U+20A6), so every price shown as '₦5,000' also downloads the latin-ext files. With Google Fonts that is about 76 KB from a second origin; a self-hosted custom subset with ₦, ₵ and ₹ is about 30 KB (estimated). The VSL must sit behind a click-to-load cover image (about 3 KB, against 540 KB for YouTube's player, per Chrome's docs). It must never be hosted on Netlify: on the credit plans bandwidth costs 20 credits per GB, and the Free plan has 300 credits a month. Each production deploy also costs 15 credits, so 20 agent pushes use up a Free month. Light and dark mode: use colour tokens, set the theme with a tiny inline script in the head before first paint, and include the color-scheme meta tag, which Android WebView needs before it honours the page's dark styles. Gold text on light backgrounds must be bronze #8A5E17 (5.68:1 on white); champagne on white is 1.31:1, and bronze on navy fails at 2.86:1. Checkout must be a plain same-tab form POST to a Netlify Function that redirects to Flutterwave Standard, with fulfilment by webhook, server-side verification and an emailed status link. It must not rely on the Inline popup, window.open or localStorage, all of which break in Opera Mini and are unreliable in in-app browsers. On the stack, the firm recommendation is Astro 6 with static output on Netlify. It gives one shared layout and a single offers data file, hashed assets that can be cached long-term, build-time AVIF/WebP and srcset, a built-in self-hosting Fonts API and zero JavaScript by default, while existing Netlify Functions stay as they are. Eleventy is mid-rename to 'Build Awesome' with v4 still in alpha. Plain HTML would copy prices and the header into about 20 files.
