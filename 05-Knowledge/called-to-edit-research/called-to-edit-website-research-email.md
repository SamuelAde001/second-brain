# Website research: Lead capture, email platform and Nigerian data law

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

I recommend MailerLite, starting on Free and moving to Comfort once the list passes 250. At 1k, 5k and 10k subscribers it costs about half what Kit charges (Comfort USD 19 / 49 / 89 a month against Kit Creator USD 39 / 89 / 139). It has a documented upsert API with consent fields (opted_in_at, optin_ip), up to 50 automations on Comfort, and it requires an authenticated sending domain, so mail goes out as samuel@calledtoedit.com. Two catches. First, MailerLite cut its free plan in June 2026 to 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month and 3 automations, so Samuel will pay almost at once. Second, Comfort caps sends at 10 times the tier ceiling, so 10,000 emails a month at the 1k tier, and a webinar launch month can go over it. Kit is the fallback if MailerLite turns the account down. Its official Free plan carries no sequences or automations (one third-party review says it has one of each, so check). I found no published country ban for Nigeria at any of the 8 tools. MailerLite and Brevo do vet accounts automatically and can reject them without giving a reason, so apply only after calledtoedit.com is live with a privacy page and a working opt-in form. Paying in USD is no longer the obstacle it was: naira card limits rose sharply in 2026 (GTBank USD 40,000 a quarter from August 2026), and the Cleva USD card is a backup. For capture, use one field: Sleeknote's 2026 data shows 4.41% conversion with one field, 2.90% with two and 1.93% with three. On phones, popups converted better than on desktop (5.60% vs 2.86%). Exit intent does not fire on touch screens, so phones should get a small teaser bar that opens a form on tap (Wisepops measured 28.79% for click-triggered popups) rather than a full-screen popup on entry, which Google advises against. A quiz such as 'What's stopping you from getting clients?' on its own landing page is the strongest magnet I found data for (ConvertFlow, September 2026: 11.28% of visitors become leads, against an 8.4% median for education landing pages in Unbounce's 2023-24 data). Ask for WhatsApp on the thank-you page, never in the main form, with its own unticked box: Nigerian commentary on the GAID reads it as requiring express consent for direct marketing, and Meta has its own opt-in rules. Legal minimum: affirmative opt-in wording, a consent log, a privacy and cookie notice, an unsubscribe link in every email that takes effect at once (NDPA s.36), and no ad pixels until a visitor accepts. The DCPMI registration thresholds are reported inconsistently (200 data subjects in six months in the 2024 notice; 2,500 to 9,999 for the lowest tier in GAID commentary) and need confirming with the NDPC. One operational risk: Apps Script on a consumer Gmail account can email only 100 recipients a day, which the 78-buyer webinar already came close to. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few points below are marked as not checked.

- **Inbox-provider rules:** Gmail requires SPF or DKIM, valid PTR, TLS and a spam rate below 0.3% from all senders. Senders of more than 5,000 a day need SPF, DKIM and DMARC (p=none is allowed) with From-domain alignment, plus one-click unsubscribe and a visible unsubscribe link on marketing mail. DKIM keys must be at least 1024-bit (2048 recommended); target a spam rate below 0.10%. Meet the bulk-sender standard from day one, even below 5,000 a day. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126
- **Lead capture benchmarks: popups:** Sleeknote (516.9M views, 21.9M leads): average popup conversion 4.23%; mobile 5.60% vs desktop 2.86%; 1 field 4.41%, 2 fields 2.90%, 3 fields 1.93%; timer triggers 4.42% vs scroll 2.64% (about a 6-second timer recommended); with a teaser 4.54% vs without 2.74%; sticky bars 2.85%; on-site quizzes 8.65%; with an image 4.05% vs without 0.66%. Exit intent needs a cursor and does not fire on mobile. Wisepops: click-triggered popups 28.79%, exit 2.81%, scroll 2.18%; 2025 mobile 4.98% vs desktop 3.67%. Both are vendor data from mostly e-commerce customers, so read them as relative effects (one field beats two, click-triggered beats passive), not as targets. *(medium, as of Sleeknote 2026-08-13; Wisepops 2025 (page undated))* Sources: https://sleeknote.com/?p=139710 · https://wisepops.com/popup-stats
- **Lead capture benchmarks: landing pages, quizzes, webinars:** Unbounce (July 2023 to July 2024, 464M visitors): all-industry median landing page conversion 6.6%, education 8.4%, online-course pages 18.3%. Education pages get far more mobile traffic, yet desktop converts 17.6% better; keep pages under about 300 words. ConvertFlow (1,063 quiz campaigns): quizzes on their own landing page capture leads from 11.28% of visitors, embedded quizzes 10.45%, all formats 7.85%. ON24 (2024): 57% of B2B webinar registrants attend. The common '30-55% quiz opt-in' figures count only people who finish the quiz, not all visitors, so they cannot be compared with the numbers above. *(medium, as of Unbounce 2024; ConvertFlow 2026-09-23; ON24 2025)* Sources: https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/education-conversion-rate/ · https://www.convertflow.com/benchmark-reports/lead-generation-quiz-funnel-benchmark-september-2026 · https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/52917/b2b-webinar-benchmarks-conversion-attendance-personalization
- **Mobile popups and Google:** Google's guidance: do not cover the whole page with interstitials, and promotional dialogs that block content can lead to poor search performance. Small banners that use a fraction of the screen (including newsletter sign-ups) are acceptable, and legally required gates are exempt. On phones, use a bottom teaser bar or inline forms and open the modal only when the visitor taps. 'Back-button' exit triggers could not be checked against current Chrome behaviour (search budget used up); treat them as unreliable and hostile. *(high, as of page updated 2025-12-10)* Sources: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/avoid-intrusive-interstitials
- **Email engagement benchmarks:** MailerLite (Dec 2024 to Nov 2025, 3.6M campaigns): overall open rate 43.46%, click 2.09%, unsubscribe 0.22%. Coaching: 48.07% open, 1.42% click. Online courses: 43.10% / 1.39%. Africa region: 36.53% open, 2.29% click. Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens; judge engagement and sunset decisions on clicks. *(medium, as of published 2025-12-03, modified 2026-04-07)* Sources: https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks
- **WhatsApp as a second channel:** Meta's opt-in must say clearly that the person agrees to receive messages from the named business. It can be collected on a website, does not have to mention WhatsApp, and opt-outs must be honoured; businesses with sustained low quality get rate-limited. Pricing has been per delivered message since 2025-07-01, by category and country code (Nigeria has its own rate). From 2026-10-01, service messages are charged after the first 1,000 a month per number, and utility messages inside the 24-hour window become chargeable. Click-to-WhatsApp ads open a free window of up to 7 days. Nigeria's marketing rate is only on the rate-card file, which I did not read. Adding a WhatsApp field to the main form is a second field, and the Sleeknote data above shows one field converting at 4.41% against 2.90% for two. *(medium, as of 2026-10 (pages undated; reference 2026-10-01 changes))* Sources: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/overview/getting-opt-in/ · https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/
- **Nigeria law: NDPA 2023 + GAID 2025:** The GAID was issued 2025-03-20 and took effect 2025-09-19, replacing the NDPR 2019 and its 2020 framework. Commentary reads it as requiring express consent for direct marketing, consent records the controller can produce, withdrawal as easy as giving consent, refusal that costs the person nothing, and published privacy and cookie notices (Article 7). NDPA s.36(3)-(4) gives an absolute right to object to direct marketing, after which processing for marketing must stop. GAID Article 8 says 'operating in Nigeria' includes targeting Nigerian data subjects. Breaches go to the NDPC within 72 hours (Art. 33). Cross-border transfers need adequacy, a transfer instrument, or another basis such as explicit consent (Art. 45). I could not get the GAID text itself (the NDPC PDF link returned 404), so article numbers come from law-firm summaries. A 2026 Federal High Court ruling (Onimisi v. GTCO) reportedly ordered a bank to stop unsolicited marketing to non-customers: https://oal.law/?p=1013185 (single source). The NDPC sent compliance notices to more than 1,300 organisations in 2025: https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/nigeria-a-2025-retrospective-and-2026-outlook. For scale, Fidelity Bank was fined NGN 555.8m in 2024: https://infusionlawyers.com/2024/08/31/ndpcs-555-8-million-fine-against-fidelity-bank-over-alleged-data-privacy-violation-insights-and-lessons/ *(medium, as of commentary 2025-09 to 2025-11)* Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1627428/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc · https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1700306/gaid-2025-unpacked-series-ii-data-protection-officers-mandatory-consents-and-emerging-technologies · https://blog.lawpavilion.com/ndp-act-2023-gaid-2025-a-comprehensive-guide-to-nigerias-new-data-protection-landscape · https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1693812/navigating-the-nigeria-data-protection-act-general-application-and-implementation-directive-ndpa-gaid-a-guide-for-businesses · https://www.lawglobalhub.com/?p=66789
- **Nigeria law: cookies:** The GAID requires a published cookie notice. Commentary treats non-essential cookies (analytics, ad pixels) as needing prior, affirmative consent under the GDPR-style standard; scroll-as-consent, continued browsing and pre-ticked boxes fail; strictly necessary cookies are exempt. The banner and reject-button rules come from vendor readings, not quoted GAID text. If the site sets only essential cookies and uses cookieless analytics, a short cookie notice in the privacy policy is the minimum; a Meta or TikTok pixel brings the banner requirement in. *(low, as of 2026-07-09 (FlexyConsent))* Sources: https://flexyconsent.com/blog/nigeria-ndpa-cookie-consent-compliance-guide/ · https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/ng-ndpa · https://blog.lawpavilion.com/ndp-act-2023-gaid-2025-a-comprehensive-guide-to-nigerias-new-data-protection-landscape
- **Nigeria law: DCPMI registration thresholds:** The thresholds are reported inconsistently. The February 2024 guidance notice covered anyone processing more than 200 data subjects in 6 months. GAID commentary lists Ultra-High 50,000+, Extra-High 10,000-49,999 and Ordinary-High 2,500-9,999, with fees in Schedule 7, although the NDPC text reportedly names 5,000 as one Ultra-High factor. Ordinary-High entities renew registration yearly and file no Compliance Audit Return (CAR); Ultra-High and Extra-High file CARs by 31 March. A court ruled parts of the 2024 notice ultra vires, and an updated notice followed. Under the GAID reading, Samuel probably falls below the threshold today but could reach it within a year. Confirm the threshold with the NDPC or a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation; this is not legal advice. *(low, as of 2024-02 notice; GAID commentary 2025-10)* Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1693812/navigating-the-nigeria-data-protection-act-general-application-and-implementation-directive-ndpa-gaid-a-guide-for-businesses · https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1434270/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance · https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025
- **GDPR for EU visitors:** Under GDPR Art. 3(2), a non-EU controller is covered when it offers goods or services to people in the EU (paid or free) or monitors their behaviour there. A Naira-priced site with occasional EU or UK visitors is a grey zone. Building to the stricter standard anyway (unticked boxes, clear purpose, one-click unsubscribe, erasure on request, no pixels before consent) covers both regimes at no extra cost. The UK has its own UK GDPR. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/

## Recommendations

- **Use MailerLite as the single ESP for all marketing email: Free up to 250 subscribers, then Comfort (USD 19/mo at 1k, 33 at 2.5k, 49 at 5k). Move to Power (USD 39 at 1k) if launch months push sends past 10 emails per subscriber per month.** Why: It is the cheapest ESP that has everything Samuel needs: a documented upsert API with consent-proof fields for Netlify Functions, groups and segments per offer, up to 50 automations, and a required authenticated domain so mail comes from samuel@calledtoedit.com. It is about half Kit's price at every tier and has no 300-a-day wall like Brevo Free. Its strict vetting also keeps its shared sending reputation clean. Trade-offs: Free is now tiny, so expect to pay within weeks. Comfort's 10x send cap can bite during webinar launches. Approval is automated and opaque, and I could not verify MailerLite's treatment of Nigerian accounts. MailerLite does not support custom API setups. Its groups are not as flexible as Kit's tags, though segments make up for most of it. Steps: 1) Get calledtoedit.com DNS live, a mailbox or forwarding for samuel@calledtoedit.com, and publish /privacy and one working opt-in page before signing up; a site under construction risks rejection. 2) Sign up with samuel@calledtoedit.com (not Gmail). In 'Tell us about yourself', say: coaching business for video editors, sign-ups from website forms and opted-in buyers, no bought lists. 3) Domains: add the DKIM and SPF records MailerLite gives you at the registrar, then add _dmarc TXT 'v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@calledtoedit.com'; move to p=quarantine after about 4 clean weeks. Add the domain to Gmail Postmaster Tools. 4) Groups: lead-guide-branding, lead-guide-outreach, lead-quiz, webinar-reg-YYYY-MM, buyer-webinar, buyer-masterclass, buyer-ebook, community-active, community-lapsed, waitlist-academy-resolve, waitlist-academy-branding (one per cohort), mentorship-applicant. Custom fields: source_page, first_magnet, quiz_result, country, whatsapp, consent_text_version. Segments: engaged-30d (clicked), buyers-any, never-bought. 5) Create an API token, store it only as a Netlify environment variable (MAILERLITE_TOKEN), never in the Brain or the repo. 6) Write netlify/functions/subscribe.mjs: honeypot plus a minimum time-on-form check, validate the email, POST https://connect.mailerlite.com/api/subscribers with {email, groups:[id], fields:{...}, status:'active', opted_in_at, optin_ip}, then append the row to the existing Apps Script Sheet CRM, then return the thank-you page with the resource shown on screen at once. 7) Extend the Flutterwave webhook to upsert buyers into the buyer-* group, which moves them out of pitch emails for what they just bought. 8) Build the automations in the sequence recommendation below. 9) Import only contacts with consent evidence. For webinar buyers who never opted in, send one Gmail note offering a free highlights cut and slides through the opt-in form. 10) First sends go to the most engaged opted-in buyers, to warm the domain.
- **Keep Kit as Plan B if MailerLite refuses the account; Systeme.io only if cost at 5k-10k becomes the deciding factor.** Why: Kit Free holds 10,000 subscribers with tags, unlimited forms, landing pages and API, so nothing is lost while waiting, but sequences need Creator (USD 39/89/139 a month at 1k/5k/10k). Systeme.io is USD 17 at 5k and USD 47 at 10k with unlimited sends, but it duplicates the Netlify stack, only takes Stripe or PayPal, and its API and sending-domain features are unverified. Trade-offs: Kit costs roughly twice MailerLite at every tier, and sources conflict on Kit Free's automation and API limits. Brevo Free's 300/day cap rules it out for launches. Mailchimp Free (250 contacts, 500 sends, no flows) is too small. Beehiiv costs the most and is built for newsletters. Loops and Resend need more custom code for funnels. Steps: If MailerLite rejects the account: open Kit with samuel@calledtoedit.com, authenticate the domain, create the same groups as tags, and point subscribe.mjs at Kit's v4 subscribers and tags endpoints (the function's interface stays the same, so the site does not change). Upgrade to Creator before turning on the welcome sequence.
- **Move all bulk and launch email off Apps Script and consumer Gmail.** Why: Consumer Gmail Apps Script stops at 100 recipients a day (Workspace: 1,500), and the first webinar already had 78 buyers. Sending through the ESP also brings in one-click unsubscribe and the DMARC alignment Gmail requires. Trade-offs: A separate transactional provider (MailerSend, Resend transactional at 3,000 a month and 100 a day free, or Workspace) was not priced or compared in this pass. Steps: Keep Apps Script for internal notifications and the Sheet CRM. Send receipts and access emails either from MailerLite automations triggered by the Flutterwave webhook (unsubscribed buyers would still need a transactional route) or from a transactional service. Check whether the current scripts run on gmail.com or Workspace.
- **Use a one-field capture system on every page, with instant delivery and a phone-specific trigger.** Why: One field converts at 4.41% against 2.90% for two (Sleeknote 2026). Mobile popups convert better than desktop when they don't block the page. Exit intent doesn't work on touch screens, and Google penalises full-page interstitials. Click-triggered forms convert best (Wisepops 28.79%). Trade-offs: The benchmarks are vendor data, mostly from e-commerce; A/B test magnet titles rather than chasing the averages. Frequency caps cost some captures but protect the page experience. Steps: Inline: one email field after the first proof block on each landing page and again at the foot, with type=email, inputmode=email and autocomplete=email for a one-tap keyboard in Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp in-app browsers.
