# Website research: Phones, speed, light/dark and UX

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

Design for Nigeria's 75th-percentile mobile user, not the median. Alex Russell's November 2025 estimate puts Nigeria's P75 connection at 3.1 Mbps down, 1 Mbps up and 190 ms round trip (RTT), the slowest of the countries he lists. Ookla's 44.14 Mbps median for December 2025 overstates what most visitors get. The audience is 75% mobile and 84% Android (StatCounter, Sept 2026): Tecno, Samsung, Infinix, Xiaomi and Itel phones, Opera at about 18% of mobile browsing, and about 43% of screens 360 px wide. Google's field data (CrUX, which feeds PageSpeed Insights) excludes Android WebViews (the Instagram and TikTok in-app browsers) and Opera. So Samuel's real audience is invisible in PageSpeed Insights, and the site must measure itself with the small web-vitals library. At 190 ms RTT, each new third-party origin costs roughly 570 ms just to connect (my estimate: about 3 round trips). Hitting LCP under 2.5 s therefore needs a critical path of about 170 KB or less and zero third-party origins before the first tap. A full page under about 400 KB is about one fifth of the 2025 median mobile home page (2,164 KB). A concrete font finding: Google Fonts' 'latin' subset does not include the naira sign (U+20A6), so every price shown as '₦5,000' also downloads the latin-ext files. With Google Fonts that is about 76 KB from a second origin; a self-hosted custom subset with ₦, ₵ and ₹ is about 30 KB (estimated). The VSL must sit behind a click-to-load cover image (about 3 KB, against 540 KB for YouTube's player, per Chrome's docs). It must never be hosted on Netlify: on the credit plans bandwidth costs 20 credits per GB, and the Free plan has 300 credits a month. Each production deploy also costs 15 credits, so 20 agent pushes use up a Free month. Light and dark mode: use colour tokens, set the theme with a tiny inline script in the head before first paint, and include the color-scheme meta tag, which Android WebView needs before it honours the page's dark styles. Gold text on light backgrounds must be bronze #8A5E17 (5.68:1 on white); champagne on white is 1.31:1, and bronze on navy fails at 2.86:1. Checkout must be a plain same-tab form POST to a Netlify Function that redirects to Flutterwave Standard, with fulfilment by webhook, server-side verification and an emailed status link. It must not rely on the Inline popup, window.open or localStorage, all of which break in Opera Mini and are unreliable in in-app browsers. On the stack, the firm recommendation is Astro 6 with static output on Netlify. It gives one shared layout and a single offers data file, hashed assets that can be cached long-term, build-time AVIF/WebP and srcset, a built-in self-hosting Fonts API and zero JavaScript by default, while existing Netlify Functions stay as they are. Eleventy is mid-rename to 'Build Awesome' with v4 still in alpha. Plain HTML would copy prices and the header into about 20 files.

- **(a) Opera Mini constraints:** In Opera Mini's Extreme mode (the Android default), JavaScript runs on Opera's servers with a limit of about 5 seconds. localStorage, sessionStorage, window.open/close and native HTML5 form validation are not supported, and AVIF is unsupported in every Opera Mini version, while WebP is supported. From Opera's help page (written 2015, still published): every interaction needs a server round trip; keyboard, touch, scroll and mousemove events do not fire; setTimeout over 5000 ms will not run without a user action; validation must happen in JavaScript or on the server. caniuse: AVIF 96.35% global support, none in Opera Mini; WebP 97.18% global, supported in all Opera Mini versions. Opera holds about 18% of Nigerian mobile browsing, but the Mini share within that is unknown. So every call to action must be a real link or form POST, and anything kept only in localStorage (theme choice, transaction reference) will fail there. *(medium, as of Opera doc 2015; caniuse 2026-10)* Sources: https://help.opera.com/en/opera-mini-and-javascript/ · https://caniuse.com/avif · https://caniuse.com/webp

- **(a) Speed vs conversion evidence:** Faster pages convert better, consistently across studies. A 0.1 s mobile speed gain raised retail conversions 8.4%, and B2C sites converted at 3.05% at 1 s versus 1.12% at 3 s. Deloitte / 55 for Google, 'Milliseconds Make Millions' (2020), 37 brands and 30M+ sessions: a 0.1 s improvement gave retail conversions +8.4%, travel +10.1% and retail average order value +9.2%. Portent (2022-04-20), about 100M page views across 20 sites: B2C e-commerce 3.05% at 1 s, 1.68% at 2 s, 1.12% at 3 s; B2B lead generation almost 40% at 1 s, 34% at 2 s, 29% at 3 s; B2B is 3x better at 1 s than at 5 s. Akamai/SOASTA (2017-04-18), about 10B visits: a 100 ms delay cut conversion about 7% (MediaPost's breakdown says 7.1% on smartphones), and a 2 s delay raised bounce 103%. Vodafone A/B test (web.dev, 2021): 31% better LCP gave 8% more sales. Google Flights: fetchpriority took LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s. All of these are Western retail or lead-gen studies from 2017 to 2022. The direction is robust; the exact multiples will not transfer to Nigeria. *(high, as of 2017-2022 studies)* Sources: https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions · https://www.deloitte.com/ie/en/services/consulting/research/milliseconds-make-millions.html · https://www.portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyone.htm · https://www.akamai.com/fr/newsroom/press-release/akamai-releases-spring-2017-state-of-online-retail-performance-report · https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/306009/three-seconds-and-youre-out.html · https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone · https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority
- **(a) Page-weight benchmarks and a budget model for Nigeria P75:** The 2025 median mobile home page is 2,164 KB. At Nigeria's P75 (3.1 Mbps, 190 ms), only about 170 KB of critical-path bytes reliably arrive in time for LCP under 2.5 s, and each extra origin costs about 570 ms just to connect. HTTP Archive Web Almanac 2025: median mobile home page 2,164 KB, with 98.1% of pages requesting JavaScript. Russell's 2026 budgets are set for a global P75 of 9 Mbps and 100 ms: a 3 s, JS-light page gets 2.0 MiB total with 0.3 MiB JS. Nigeria has about a third of that bandwidth and about twice the RTT, so those budgets are far too generous here. My derived model: DNS, TCP and TLS 1.3 take about 3 RTT (about 570 ms) before the request. TCP slow start then delivers about 14, 28 and 56 KB in the first three RTTs, then about 74 KB per RTT (the bandwidth-delay product). So about 170 KB lands about 1.5 to 1.8 s after navigation starts, leaving CPU headroom on a Helio-class phone. A Google Fonts setup adds a second origin, and 4 connections instead of 2 shrinks Russell's budgets further, by about 350 KiB at the 3 s target. This model is an engineering estimate, not a published figure. *(medium, as of 2025-11)* Sources: https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight · https://infrequently.org/2025/11/performance-inequality-gap-2026/
- **(a) Core Web Vitals thresholds:** Core Web Vitals 'good' thresholds are unchanged in 2026: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1, judged at the 75th percentile of page views. 'Poor' starts above 4 s, 500 ms and 0.25. No 2026 revision found. Lighthouse 13 removed the 'lazy load third-party resources with facades' audit, though the pattern is still recommended. *(high, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds · https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades
- **(a) Fonts: measured sizes and the naira-sign trap:** Measured Google Fonts WOFF2 sizes: Gloock latin 17.6 KB plus latin-ext 9.0 KB; Plus Jakarta Sans variable (200-800) latin 27.3 KB plus latin-ext 21.7 KB; the italic adds 29.5 KB. Google's latin subset does not include ₦ (U+20A6), so any page showing a naira price also downloads the latin-ext files, about 76 KB for both fonts. I fetched the Google Fonts CSS2 response on 2026-10-26 with an Android Chrome user agent and measured the files. The latin unicode-range covers U+0000-00FF, U+2000-206F and U+20AC (euro), but not U+20A0-20AB, which is in latin-ext. ₦ (U+20A6), ₵ (U+20B5) and ₹ (U+20B9) all fall in latin-ext. I checked the cmaps of both source TTFs (google/fonts repo): both contain ₦, ₵ and ₹. Gloock is one static weight (400, no variable axes); Plus Jakarta Sans is a single variable file covering 200-800. Estimate from in-memory subsetting with zlib compression: a tight subset (ASCII plus ₦ ₵ ₹ € £, curly quotes, dashes, bullet, arrow) is about 33% smaller than the Google latin cut. That suggests about 12 KB (Gloock) plus about 18 KB (Plus Jakarta Sans) as WOFF2. web.dev: self-hosting only wins with a CDN and HTTP/2 (Netlify has both) and needs subsetting plus WOFF2; preload ignores unicode-range; font-display swap gives the least delay but can shift layout, optional avoids late swaps; size-adjust reduces CLS. Chrome has partitioned its HTTP cache since version 86, so Google Fonts files cached on other sites give no benefit. *(high, as of 2026-10-26 (measured))* Sources: https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Gloock&family=Plus+Jakarta+Sans:ital,wght@0,200..800;1,200..800&display=swap · https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/gloock · https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/plusjakartasans · https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices · https://developer.chrome.com/blog/http-cache-partitioning
- **(a) Netlify caching defaults:** By default Netlify sends browsers 'Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, must-revalidate' for static assets, so every repeat page view re-checks CSS, JS and fonts. Long-lived 'immutable' caching is only safe for fingerprinted or versioned filenames. Netlify's edge caches static assets for up to a year and clears the cache on each deploy, but browsers re-check every file. On a 190 ms connection that costs time on every navigation. The fix is a _headers rule such as '/fonts/* Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable' or '/_astro/* ...', applied only to files whose names change when their content changes. Plain HTML without a build step has no automatic fingerprinting. *(high, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://docs.netlify.com/platform/caching/ · https://docs.netlify.com/manage/routing/headers/
- **(a) Netlify credit pricing: page weight and deploys cost money:** Netlify's credit plans give 300 credits a month on Free ($0), 1,000 on Personal ($9) and 3,000 on Pro ($20). Every production deploy costs 15 credits, bandwidth 20 credits per GB, compute 10 credits per GB-hour, and web requests 2 credits per 10,000. Implications (my arithmetic): 20 production deploys use a whole Free month. At 400 KB a page, 10,000 page views is 4 GB, or 80 credits; at the 2.1 MB median it would be about 420 credits. A VSL served from Netlify (1,000 plays × 75 MB = 75 GB, or 1,500 credits) is impossible on Free or Personal. I could not confirm whether Samuel's existing account is on a credit plan or a legacy plan, or whether deploy previews and Image CDN transforms use credits. *(medium, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://www.netlify.com/pricing/
- **(a) Images:** Netlify Image CDN resizes and converts images at request time for plain HTML sites, with no build step. The LCP image should carry fetchpriority="high" (Chrome 102+, Firefox 132+, Safari 17.2+) and must never be lazy-loaded. URL form: /.netlify/images?url=/img/x.jpg&w=480&fm=avif&q=60. Accepted formats are avif, webp, jpg, png, gif and blurhash, with q defaulting to 75. If fm is omitted it picks WebP first, then AVIF, based on the Accept header, so set fm explicitly in <picture> sources if you want AVIF. Results are cached at the edge and re-run when the source image changes in a deploy. The docs page does not state plan limits or credit cost. AVIF has 96.35% global support (not Opera Mini), so ship AVIF, then WebP, then JPEG. *(high, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://docs.netlify.com/build/image-cdn/overview/ · https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority · https://caniuse.com/avif

- **(a) Prefetching wastes Nigerian data on mobile:** Chrome's speculation rules now fire on mobile from viewport heuristics. 'moderate' does so since August 2025, 500 ms after scrolling stops; 'eager' since January 2026, 50 ms after a link enters view. That can download pages the visitor never opens. 'conservative' triggers only on touch or pointer down. Chrome blocks speculation under Save-Data, low memory, or Energy Saver on low battery. Supported in Chrome and Edge 121+; not in Firefox; behind a flag in Safari. *(high, as of 2026-01)* Sources: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/prerender-pages
- **(b) Dark mode mechanics, including Android WebView:** Android WebView (the Instagram and TikTok in-app browser on Android) reports prefers-color-scheme from the host app's theme. A page that ships no dark styles can be auto-darkened by the app. Under the default dark-mode setting, WebView needs <meta name="color-scheme" content="dark light"> before it honours the page's dark styles. Android docs: for apps targeting API 33+, automatic darkening applies only if the page doesn't use prefers-color-scheme and the author hasn't disabled it. Under the default 'prefer web theme' setting, WebView requires the color-scheme meta tag. web.dev recommends color-scheme: light dark on :root, colour variables named by role, theme-color meta tags with media attributes, and <picture> sources with prefers-color-scheme media for theme-specific images; it also says to let users override the system setting and remember the choice. light-dark() has been Baseline since May 2024 and needs color-scheme. One catch (from how media queries work): <picture> media queries and theme-color media follow the operating-system setting, not a manual toggle. Logos and images therefore need to be inline SVG or swapped by CSS keyed on [data-theme], and the toggle script must rewrite theme-color itself. *(high, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/dark-theme · https://web.dev/articles/prefers-color-scheme · https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/light-dark

- **(c) Checkout friction (Baymard):** Average cart abandonment is 70.22% (50 studies, updated 2025-09-22). Extra costs (40%), not trusting the site with card details (19%), forced accounts (18%), a long or complicated checkout (17%) and site errors (17%) lead the reasons. The ideal checkout has 7-8 form fields, against an average of 14.88. Other reasons: the total cost couldn't be seen upfront (12%), card declined (10%), too few payment methods (9%). The average US checkout shows 23.48 form elements, against an ideal of 12-14. Baymard estimates a 35.26% conversion gain from fixing checkout usability. Inline validation (2024-01-09): 31% of sites have none, 4% get it wrong; validate when the user leaves the field, not too early, clear errors once fixed, and confirm correct entries. Perceived security: visually set the payment area apart, put 1-2 icons or seals inside it, use padlock icons and reassuring microcopy; in Baymard's 2025 survey, 19% of 1,026 respondents abandoned a checkout over card trust. Mobile forms: avoid labels inside the fields (placeholder-style). These are US/EU retail numbers; the card fields themselves sit on Flutterwave's hosted page, so Samuel controls the step before the redirect and the trust block. *(high, as of 2025-09-22)* Sources: https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate · https://baymard.com/research-articles/inline-form-validation · https://baymard.com/research-articles/perceived-security-of-payment-form · https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-forms-avoid-inline-labels
- **(c) Payment flows inside in-app browsers:** Payment checkouts inside WebViews break when redirects are blocked or when they depend on window.close or popups. Paystack's own WebView guide says redirects must be allowed for bank and 3DS authorisation, and that window.close() doesn't work there. Flutterwave's Inline checkout is a modal driven by a third-party script; its Standard checkout is a server-created hosted link that redirects back with status, tx_ref and transaction_id. Paystack: after 3DS, customers are sent to https://standard.paystack.co/close, which can't close itself in a WebView; use the charge.success webhook to deliver what was bought and the Verify API to confirm. Flutterwave Inline: loads https://checkout.flutterwave.com/v3.js; redirect_url takes precedence over the callback; closing the modal mid-payment marks it cancelled, but webhooks still fire if it later succeeds; session_duration can be up to 1440 minutes; always verify on the server. Instagram on Android reportedly does not launch intent:// URLs and leaves users on a white screen (Meta developer forum, no official fix). On iOS, link services use an x-safari-https:// prefix to open Safari, which neither Apple nor Meta documents. Instagram/Facebook and TikTok in-app browsers inject JavaScript that tracks taps and form inputs (Krause, Aug 2022). PDFs render inconsistently in Instagram's in-app browser, and Drive or Dropbox preview links fare worst (vendor source). WhatsApp: a business-link in-app browser was in testing in May 2025 (WABetaInfo); its scope is unconfirmed. No source tests Flutterwave inside the Instagram or TikTok in-app browsers, so this needs testing on a real phone. *(medium, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://docs-v2.paystack.com/docs/guides/using_the_paystack_checkout_in_a_mobile_webview/ · https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/inline · https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/flutterwave-standard-1 · https://developers.secure.facebook.com/community/threads/470205278761649 · https://docs.short.io/articles/useful-articles/how-to-use-mobile-targeting-to-escape-redirecting-links-to-in-app-browser · https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2022/08/spying-on-the-spies-see-what-javascript-commands-get-injected-by-an-in-app-browser · https://link.boo/help/pdf-not-opening-from-instagram · https://jang.com.pk/en/37510-whatsapp-releases-in-app-browser-for-business-website-links-news

The library suggests adding a query string to escape links so detection can be skipped on return. *(medium, as of 2026-10)* Sources: https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/inapp-spy@5.0.10/README.md · https://dev.library.kiwix.org/content/stackoverflow_en_nopic_2021-08/questions/31569518/how-to-detect-facebook-in-app-browser
- **(c) Navigation:** Hiding navigation behind a menu lowers how often people find and use it. In NN/g's mobile test, a site with visible plus hidden navigation got 89% navigation use, against 44% and 56% for two sites with hidden-only navigation. Old study (2016-07-24) but still the main source. The effect was smaller on mobile than on desktop. *(medium, as of 2016-07-24)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/
- **(d) WCAG 2.2 AA:** WCAG 2.2 (W3C Recommendation, 2023-10-05) adds six A/AA criteria that bite on this site: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (sticky header and bottom bar), 2.5.8 Target Size ≥ 24×24 CSS px, 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry and 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication. 4.1.1 Parsing was removed. Target-size exceptions: spacing (24 px circles don't overlap), an equivalent control elsewhere, links inside sentences, browser-native controls, or essential presentation. Redundant Entry: details already entered (name, email) must be filled in or selectable later in the same process. Accessible Authentication: no memory or puzzle test without an alternative; password managers and paste count as help. *(high, as of 2023-10-05)* Sources: https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/
- **(e) Static site generator landscape:** Astro 6 shipped on 2026-03-10: Node 22+, a built-in Fonts API that self-hosts fonts with fallbacks and preload, a stable CSP API and live content collections. Cloudflare acquired the Astro company on 2026-01-16 and says Astro stays open source and works on any host. Eleventy was renamed 'Build Awesome' on 2026-03-03, with 3.1.x stable, v4 in alpha since July 2025, and a paid Pro tier funded by Kickstarter. Astro's static output needs no hosting adapter. Netlify is named as a partner in the Astro Ecosystem Fund. Build Awesome says the Pro tier won't be required and Eleventy plugins and commands keep working. *(high, as of 2026-03)* Sources: https://astro.build/blog/astro-6/ · https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260116386991/en/Cloudflare-Acquires-Astro-to-Accelerate-the-Future-of-High-Performance-Web-Development · https://www.11ty.dev/blog/build-awesome/ · https://www.11ty.dev/blog/review-2025/

## Recommendations

- **Adopt hard per-page performance budgets for every landing page (/webinar, /community, /academy, /mentorship, /resources), measured as compressed mobile transfer sizes, and fail the build when a page breaks them.** Why: Nigeria's P75 user (3.1 Mbps, 190 ms, Helio-class Android) only gets LCP under 2.5 s with about 170 KB on the critical path and no extra origins. The speed studies show that each 0.1 to 1 s is worth real conversion. Trade-offs: Rules out heavy hero videos, carousels, chat widgets, and analytics or pixel tags loaded before interaction. Those must load after the first tap or move server-side. Steps: BUDGETS (mobile, compressed). HTML document including inline critical CSS: ≤ 30 KB, and the first 14 KB must hold the header, hero headline, price and main button. CSS: inline all of it if ≤ 15 KB, otherwise one hashed file ≤ 25 KB. Fonts: 2 WOFF2 files, ≤ 40 KB total (target about 30 KB), and 0 third-party font requests. JavaScript before interaction: ≤ 15 KB (hard cap 30 KB), no framework runtime. LCP image: ≤ 50 KB AVIF (≤ 70 KB WebP) at 480 w. Above-the-fold bytes: ≤ 170 KB. Whole page before any video tap: ≤ 400 KB (hard cap 600 KB). Requests before LCP: ≤ 5. Third-party origins before the first interaction: 0. Video: cover image ≤ 30 KB plus ≤ 3 KB script. FIELD TARGETS (p75, from your own RUM including in-app browsers): LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms (aim ≤ 100), CLS ≤ 0.1 (aim ≤ 0.05), TTFB ≤ 0.8 s. LAB GATE: Lighthouse mobile default (Slow 4G, 4x CPU) with Performance ≥ 90 and LCP ≤ 2.0 s. ENFORCE: a post-build script that sums each page's HTML + CSS + JS + fonts + LCP image from dist/ and exits non-zero over budget, so Netlify keeps the previous deploy live. TEST: monthly on a real Tecno or Infinix phone, inside the Instagram and TikTok in-app browsers, on MTN mobile data.
- **Self-host Gloock and Plus Jakarta Sans as custom-subset WOFF2 files that include ₦, ₵ and ₹; never load the Google Fonts CSS.** Why: Google's latin subset lacks ₦, so every naira price pulls in the latin-ext files as well (about 76 KB across both fonts), all from a second origin costing about 570 ms to connect at 190 ms RTT. A tight subset is about 30 KB total (estimate), served from the same origin. Trade-offs: Someone must re-run the subsetter if a new script or symbol is needed (Yoruba diacritics, other currencies). Dropping italics means emphasis uses weight, not slant. Steps: Subset with fontTools pyftsubset or glyphhanger. Codepoints: U+0020-007E, U+00A0, U+00A3, U+00A9, U+00D7, U+00E9, U+2013-2014, U+2018-2019, U+201C-201D, U+2022, U+2026, U+20A6, U+20AC, U+20B5, U+20B9, U+2192, U+2713. Keep the kern, liga and calt features and drop hinting. Plus Jakarta Sans: one variable file, optionally limited to weights 400-800. Gloock: one static 400 file. No italic. Use versioned names (gloock-400-v1.woff2) and a _headers rule '/fonts/* Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable'. Put @font-face in the inline <style> in the head with font-display: swap. Preload both files (<link rel=preload as=font type=font/woff2 crossorigin>), as only 2 files fit within budget. Add fallback faces with size-adjust and ascent-override (Georgia for Gloock, Arial for Plus Jakarta Sans) to keep CLS under 0.05. On Astro 6, test whether its Fonts API accepts this custom unicode-range before relying on it.
- **Deliver every image through <picture> as AVIF, then WebP, then JPEG, with srcset sized for 360 px phones and laptops. Prefer inline SVG for logos and icons.** Why: About 43% of Nigerian phones are 360 px wide. Opera Mini lacks AVIF but supports WebP. The LCP image decides LCP, and fetchpriority cut Google Flights' LCP by 0.7 s. Trade-offs: More image files per photo (fixed by Astro's <Picture> at build time, or Netlify Image CDN at request time on plain HTML). Steps: Hero (LCP): srcset 360w, 480w, 720w, 960w, 1440w; sizes="(min-width: 1024px) 560px, calc(100vw - 32px)"; explicit width and height; fetchpriority="high"; never loading=lazy. AVIF quality about 50-60 targeting ≤ 50 KB at 480 w. Everything below the fold: loading="lazy" decoding="async"; testimonial thumbnails ≤ 15 KB each. Logos and icons: inline SVG with fill: currentColor or CSS variables, so one file serves both themes (≤ 3 KB each). With Netlify Image CDN, always set fm=avif or fm=webp per <source>, because when left to choose it picks WebP first. Photos of Samuel in dark mode: optional filter: brightness(.95).
- **Ship the VSL as a click-to-load cover image, host it on YouTube (nocookie) or a streaming service with adaptive bitrate (Bunny Stream or Cloudflare Stream), never on Netlify. Show the length and an approximate data cost, and place the key points in text right under it.** Why: YouTube's player is about 540 KB before anyone taps play, against a 3 KB cover. A 10-minute VSL costs a Nigerian viewer roughly NGN 28 to NGN 62 of data, and serving it from Netlify would burn about 20 credits per GB. Trade-offs: YouTube is free and handles adaptive quality, but shows its branding and related videos (rel=0 only limits them to your channel). Bunny or Cloudflare Stream look cleaner but cost money and need their own player script, loaded only on tap. Their Africa pricing and Nigerian edge presence are unverified. Steps: <lite-youtube videoid=… params="rel=0&modestbranding=1&cc_load_policy=1" nocookie> or a custom equivalent. Poster image: AVIF ≤ 30 KB showing Samuel's face plus a one-line hook and a play button at least 64 px. Label it, e.g., '6 min · about 35 MB'. Never autoplay. Captions on by default, since most visitors arrive muted from Instagram or TikTok. Start adaptive streaming at 360p or 480p. Skip loading the poster in high resolution when navigator.connection.saveData is on. Below the video: 3 to 5 bullet takeaways and the call to action, so non-watchers can still convert. Keep the VSL under about 8 minutes and test a short (90 s) cut against a long one.
- **Build light and dark themes from colour tokens, with a no-flash inline script, a remembered toggle, the color-scheme meta tag for Android WebView, and theme-aware logos.** Why: Android in-app browsers report the host app's theme and may auto-darken pages without dark styles, which would mangle the navy and champagne palette. Champagne fails on white (1.31:1) and bronze fails on navy (2.86:1), so each theme needs its own gold. Trade-offs: Opera Mini has no localStorage, so the toggle won't persist there and the OS theme applies. <picture> media queries can't follow a manual toggle, so photos that differ by theme must be avoided or swapped by CSS. Steps: <head>, in this order: <meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">; two <meta name="theme-color"> tags (#FFFFFF with media=(prefers-color-scheme: light), #071226 with media=(prefers-color-scheme: dark)); the inline script <script>try{var t=localStorage.getItem('cte-theme');if(t==='light'||t==='dark')document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme',t)}catch(e){}</script>, placed before any CSS. LIGHT tokens on :root: --bg #FFFFFF, --surface #FAF6EE, --text #0A1F44, --muted #5B6475, --gold-text #8A5E17, --border #8C94A3, --btn-bg #0A1F44, --btn-text #FFFFFF, --focus #0A1F44, --error #B42318, --success #067647, color-scheme: light. DARK tokens in @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { :root:not([data-theme=light]) {…} } and repeated in :root[data-theme=dark]: --bg #071226, --surface #0A1F44, --text #FFFFFF, --muted #C9D2E3, --gold-text #FCDD9E, --border #5A6E94, --btn-bg #FCDD9E, --btn-text #071226, --focus #FCDD9E, --error #F97066, --success #47CD89, color-scheme: dark. Give body an explicit background: var(--bg). Brand rule: in light mode keep the hero as a navy band (champagne allowed only on navy) with white or cream sections below; a primary button on white is navy with white text (16.25:1). Toggle: a <button> with an accessible name; it flips data-theme, saves to localStorage inside try/catch, and rewrites the theme-color content. Focus ring: 2 px solid var(--focus) with a 2 px offset.
- **Mobile layout: a compact sticky header, a bottom call-to-action bar that appears only once the hero button scrolls away, and visible offer links on the hub pages; offer landing pages strip navigation down to logo plus button.** Why: Hidden-only navigation is found far less (89% vs 44-56% in NN/g's test). Each offer URL must work as a standalone landing page where leaving through the menu is a loss. WCAG 2.4.11 requires that sticky bars never cover the focused element. Trade-offs: Stripped landing pages make cross-selling harder (put it after the call to action and in the thank-you page instead). Evidence that a bottom bar lifts conversion is vendor-level only, so A/B test it. Steps: Header: height ≤ 56 px, logo plus one button ('Get a seat · ₦5,000'). Desktop shows Webinar, Community, Academy, Mentorship, Resources plus the button. Mobile hub pages (/ and /resources): a horizontally scrolling row of offer links under the header instead of a menu-only nav. Bottom bar: 64 px plus env(safe-area-inset-bottom); shows the price and one button; driven by IntersectionObserver (shown after the hero button leaves view, hidden while the pricing or form section is visible); without JS it simply doesn't appear. CSS: html{scroll-padding-top:72px;scroll-padding-bottom:88px}. Design at 360 px width with 16 px gutters (328 px content), using 100dvh, never 100vh. Main buttons ≥ 48 px tall and full width on mobile. FAQ: native <details>/<summary>, 5 to 8 questions, including 'Can I pay by bank transfer?', 'I'm outside Nigeria, how do I pay?' and 'What happens after I pay?'. Put a WhatsApp help link in the same spot on every page, in the footer and the checkout step (WCAG 3.2.6).
- **Forms: the email-only capture should work without JavaScript, use correct input types and autocomplete tokens, validate when the user leaves the field, and suggest fixes for email typos.** Why: Every visitor should leave an email. Baymard's ideal checkout is 7-8 fields, and 31% of sites lack inline validation. Opera Mini has no native HTML5 validation, so the server must validate too. Trade-offs: Server-side validation plus redirect pages are slightly more function code. Steps: Resource capture: one field <input type=email name=email autocomplete=email inputmode=email autocapitalize=off spellcheck=false enterkeyhint=send required>, label above the field (never placeholder-only), button text naming the outcome ('Send me the outreach guide'). It POSTs to a Netlify Function that validates, writes to the Apps Script CRM and returns 303 to /resources/<guide>/thanks. The thanks page shows an HTML version of the guide plus a direct PDF link served as application/pdf (not Google Drive), and the email carries the same link. Optional WhatsApp field only where reminders matter: type=tel autocomplete="mobile tel", accepting 080… or +234…, normalised on the server. JavaScript enhancement: validate on blur, clear errors when fixed, show a 'Did you mean gmail.com?' hint for gmail.con, gmial.com and yahoo.con, and announce success with role=status (WCAG 4.1.3). No CAPTCHA (use a honeypot field plus rate-limiting in the function).
- **Make checkout robust inside in-app browsers and Opera Mini: a plain same-tab form POST to a Netlify Function that creates a Flutterwave Standard payment link and redirects with a 303, with webhook fulfilment, a re-enterable status page and an emailed recovery link.** Why: Popups, window.open, window.close and third-party modal scripts are what break in WebViews and Opera Mini. Bank-transfer payers leave for their bank app and may lose the in-app browser page. Collecting the email first enables recovery and meets WCAG 3.3.7 (no re-entering details). Trade-offs: Flutterwave's hosted page is less on-brand than the Inline modal. Bank transfers confirm asynchronously, so the success page needs a 'pending' state. That the in-app page gets killed when the bank app opens is my inference; test it on a low-RAM phone. Steps: 1) Offer page: button 'Pay ₦10,000 · get the recording' goes to /checkout/<offer>. 2) Step 1: name (autocomplete=name), email, optional WhatsApp; 3 fields at most. 3) A normal <form method=post action=/api/checkout> (redirect rule to the function). The function writes a pending order to Netlify Blobs plus a CRM row (abandoned-checkout capture), creates the Flutterwave Standard payment with a unique tx_ref, the customer details, redirect_url=https://calledtoedit.com/paid?ref=<tx_ref> and payment_options for the buyer's country, then returns 303 to the hosted link. 4) /paid verifies on the server by transaction_id or tx_ref. If successful, show next steps (email, Discord invite, calendar). If pending, show 'Confirming your transfer' with <meta http-equiv=refresh content=10> for about 2 minutes plus 'We'll email you'. 5) The webhook is the only thing that delivers what was bought, and handles repeat calls for the same tx_ref safely. 6) After 30 minutes still pending, send one 'Finish your payment' email with a fresh link. 7) Trust block right above the Pay button: '₦10,000 total' (say 'no extra fees' only if true), 'Secured by Flutterwave · Card · Bank transfer · USSD', 'Instant access by email', a WhatsApp help link, one proof line ('78 editors paid for the first webinar'), all inside a visually separate box with a padlock. 8) Never keep the transaction reference only in localStorage; never open payment in a new tab.
- **Handle in-app browsers gently: detect them, but only on the checkout step show a small notice with 'Open in Chrome' or 'Open in Safari' steps and a Copy-link button. Never auto-redirect out of the in-app browser.** Why: intent:// escapes reportedly fail in Instagram on Android, and the iOS x-safari trick is undocumented. Forcing visitors out of the in-app browser on the landing page loses people. The checkout step is where in-app browser failures cost money. Trade-offs: User-agent sniffing is fragile and needs a yearly check against a maintained list. Steps: A small inline check: /Instagram|FBAN|FBAV|FB_IAB|FB4A|BytedanceWebview|musical_ly|TikTok/i.test(navigator.userAgent), or inapp-spy if the extra bytes fit the budget. Notice text: 'Paying inside Instagram? If the payment page stalls, tap ⋮ → Open in Chrome' (iOS: 'tap ··· → Open in Safari'), plus a navigator.clipboard copy button with a select-text fallback. Log the in-app browser type with each lead and order in the CRM so Samuel learns his real mix. Test the intent:// and x-safari-https:// escapes once on real devices before offering them as buttons.
- **Add your own real-user Core Web Vitals monitoring, because Google's data (CrUX/PageSpeed Insights) cannot see Android in-app browsers or Opera.** Why: Most of the audience arrives through Instagram or TikTok in-app browsers or uses Opera, all excluded from CrUX. Without your own data, the site may pass PageSpeed Insights while being slow for actual buyers. Trade-offs: About 3 KB of JavaScript (loaded after the page loads), plus a small function and storage. Steps: Load web-vitals@5 (standard build) after the page has loaded. Report LCP, INP, CLS and TTFB through navigator.sendBeacon to /api/rum with page path, in-app browser type and theme. Store in Netlify Blobs (one JSON row per day per page) and review the p75 per page and per in-app browser in a monthly check.
- **Meet WCAG 2.2 AA with a short checklist, built into the component library once, not page by page.** Why: Most violations on small sites come from shared components (buttons, header, forms). Fix them once and every page inherits the fix. Trade-offs: Minor design limits: no thin gold text on white, visible focus rings, borders at least 3:1. Steps: Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, large text and UI ≥ 3:1 (use the token values above; bronze only on light, champagne only on dark). Focus visible and never hidden under sticky bars (scroll-padding). Targets ≥ 24 px (use 44-48 px for main buttons and the toggle). <html lang=en>, a skip link, one <h1> per page. Labels tied to inputs, errors in text tied with aria-describedby, and a suggested fix (3.3.1/3.3.3). No re-entering details already given in checkout (3.3.7). Help link in the same spot everywhere (3.2.6). Community login via Discord OAuth or an email magic link, never a puzzle (3.3.8). Captions and a transcript for the VSL and testimonials. Reflow at 320 px with no sideways scrolling. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Test with TalkBack on Android and a keyboard on desktop.
- **Firm stack decision: move calledtoedit.com to Astro 6 with static output on Netlify, no UI framework integrations. Keep all existing Netlify Functions, Blobs, scheduled functions, Flutterwave, Apps Script and the Discord bot exactly as they are.** Why: With about 15-20 pages, plain HTML means about 20 copies of the header, footer and prices; one missed edit shows a wrong price on a landing page. It also has no asset fingerprinting (so no long-term caching) and needs hand-made srcsets. Astro offers a shared layout, one offers data file, hashed /_astro/ assets, build-time AVIF/WebP and srcset, a self-hosting Fonts API, a CSP API, Markdown blogging, and zero client JavaScript by default. The output is plain HTML in dist/, so nothing is locked in. Eleventy would also work, but it is mid-rename with v4 in alpha and has no built-in asset fingerprinting. Trade-offs: Adds a Node 22+ toolchain and npm dependencies, and builds can fail (Netlify's all-or-nothing deploys keep the old site live). Yearly major versions (5 in Dec 2024, 6 in Mar 2026) need a deliberate upgrade. Cloudflare now owns Astro; static output needs no adapter, so Netlify stays fully supported. Each production deploy still costs 15 credits whatever the stack. Steps: Folders: src/layouts/Base.astro (head with theme script, fonts and meta; Header; Footer), src/components/ (Header, Footer, OfferHero, PayBlock, EmailCapture, VideoFacade, FAQ, Testimonial), src/data/offers.ts (one object per offer: slug, name, priceNGN, ctaLabel, status open|waitlist|soldout, nextDate, checkoutPath, payment options), src/pages/ (index, webinar, community, academy/index, academy/[cohort], mentorship, resources/index, resources/[slug], paid, 404), src/content/blog/*.md for later, public/_headers ('/_astro/* Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable' and the /fonts/* rule), public/_redirects (/api/* → /.netlify/functions/:splat 200), netlify/functions/*.mjs unchanged. netlify.toml: build command 'npm run build', publish 'dist', NODE_VERSION = '22'. Pin exact versions in package.json, keep automatic dependency updates off, and run one planned upgrade a year. Add the budget-check script to the build. On the old netlify.app sites, add 301 redirects to the matching calledtoedit.com paths once the new pages are live.
- **Treat Netlify deploys and bandwidth as a budget: batch AI-agent changes into a few production deploys a week, and keep video and large downloads off Netlify.** Why: On credit plans, every production deploy costs 15 credits (20 deploys = a whole Free month) and bandwidth costs 20 credits per GB, so heavy pages and iterating by pushing to production directly cost money. Trade-offs: Slower ship cadence unless branch or preview deploys turn out to be cheap or free (not verified). Steps: Work on a branch, review on its deploy preview, and merge to main in batches. Check the account's plan type and credit use in the Netlify dashboard each month. Host the VSL on YouTube, Bunny or Cloudflare Stream and the PDFs on the site only if each is ≤ 2 MB (compress them); otherwise use a separate storage bucket or CDN.
- **Do not prefetch or prerender pages on mobile beyond touch-down; use speculation rules with eagerness 'conservative' only, or none.** Why: Since August 2025 (moderate) and January 2026 (eager), Chrome on mobile prefetches links that are merely on screen, which spends visitors' paid data on pages they may never open. Trade-offs: Gives up a few hundred milliseconds on desktop navigations; 'moderate' could be limited to desktop with a media-scoped rule if wanted. Steps: <script type="speculationrules">{"prerender":[{"where":{"href_matches":"/*"},"eagerness":"conservative"}]}</script>, excluding /api/* and /paid.
