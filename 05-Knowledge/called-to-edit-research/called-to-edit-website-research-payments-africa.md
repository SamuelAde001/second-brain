# Website research: Payments: Nigeria and the rest of Africa

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

Keep Flutterwave as the main rail, but your account is probably set up wrong for foreign buyers. Flutterwave treats any card issued outside your sign-up country as international. A Nigerian merchant has to ask for that access (Settings > Business Preferences > Payment Methods > request access, decision promised within 48 hours). So the Ghana and Zimbabwe buyers were most likely blocked at the merchant side, not just by their banks. Check the failed transactions in the dashboard to be sure. Flutterwave's official Nigeria rates are 2.0% local, with no cap shown, and 4.8% international (it was 3.8% until 2024-11-11), plus 7.5% VAT on fees. By default the customer pays the fee on top, which puts a total like NGN 5,107.50 in front of the buyer. Switch that off. Paystack Nigeria charges 1.5% + NGN 100 (the NGN 100 is waived under NGN 2,500), capped at NGN 2,000. International cards are 3.9% + NGN 100, and dedicated virtual accounts are 1% capped at NGN 300. Paystack also offers PalmPay, OPay and Google Pay. But Paystack's mobile money only works for businesses in Ghana, Kenya and Côte d'Ivoire, so it cannot solve the African cross-border problem. Flutterwave's checkout can show Ghana, Kenya and Uganda mobile money when the charge currency is GHS, KES or UGX. Whether a Nigerian Flutterwave account is allowed to collect in those currencies is NOT confirmed anywhere public: test it with a small live charge and ask support. Zimbabwe has no mobile money rail on any of these gateways. Treat it as a USD card country with a fallback. On cost, Flutterwave is cheaper for the NGN 5,000 and 10,000 items. Paystack is cheaper above about NGN 15,000-20,000, and far cheaper on the NGN 500,000 mentorship (about NGN 2,000 against about NGN 10,750). Add Paystack as a second rail for three things: high-ticket NGN sales, transfer-based community renewals through a dedicated account per member, and failover. Leave cross-border on Flutterwave. Add a hosted fallback (Selar) for digital products when a foreign card fails. Choose currency and payment methods by visitor country, which Netlify Functions already provide (context.geo.country.code), and let the buyer override the country. Register Called to Edit with CAC: Paystack Starter accounts have a lifetime cap of NGN 8,000,000 and USD 5,000, and Flutterwave lists missing documents as a top reason for refusing international access. Caveat: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Flutterwave virtual-account behaviour, Paystack dedicated-account eligibility, Paystack chargeback rules, Bumpa, Paystack Storefront, OPay merchant checkout and Indian recurring-card rules could not be checked and are listed as open questions.

## Findings

- **Flutterwave: international cards need a request:** A Nigerian Flutterwave merchant must request international card acceptance. The path is Settings > Business Preferences > Payment Methods > 'request access'. You get a ticket ID by email and a decision 'within 48 hours', though some requests take longer. Listed refusal reasons: missing documents, high-risk activity, non-compliance, regional restrictions. Flutterwave defines international card payments as 'payments made by your customers using their debit or credit cards from outside your Flutterwave sign-up country'. A Ghanaian or Zimbabwean card is therefore international on a Nigerian account, and these failures match the access not being enabled. The page doesn't list which documents are needed; it links to the onboarding requirements (see the onboarding finding). What the buyer sees when access is off could not be confirmed. Pull the decline message for the two failed buyers from the dashboard. *(high, as of Page marked 'Updated 29 months ago' (about mid-2024); read 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://flutterwave.com/ng/support/payment-methods/how-to-accept-international-cards-on-your-flutterwave-for-business-account · https://flutterwave.com/gh/support/pricing/international-card-transaction-fee-increase
- **Flutterwave Nigeria fees:** Official Nigeria pricing: 2% per local transaction (shown as 1.4% transaction fee + 0.6% platform fee) for cards, bank transfer and USSD. International cards (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover) are 4.8%. Fees carry 7.5% VAT. No fee cap is published. By default the CUSTOMER pays the fee (changeable in the dashboard). Local payments settle next day. International cards are charged and settled in local currency by default. The international rate went from 3.8% to 4.8% on 2024-11-11, blamed on higher card-scheme interchange. Several 2026 third-party blogs still quote '1.4% + NGN 100 capped at NGN 2,000'. That conflicts with the official page; ask Flutterwave whether any cap applies to your account. Example costs from the official rates, with VAT: NGN 5,000 sale about NGN 107.50; NGN 50,000 about NGN 1,075; NGN 500,000 about NGN 10,750 (no cap shown). Also on Flutterwave's Nigeria receiving-payments page: any payment in a currency other than your local one 'is regarded as an international payment for settlement & pricing', and Naira cards always settle in Naira 'irrespective of the currency you are purchasing in'. *(high, as of Pricing page read 2026-10-26; receiving-payments page 'Updated 16 months ago')* Sources: https://flutterwave.com/ng/pricing · https://flutterwave.com/ng/support/pricing/pricing-for-receiving-payment · https://flutterwave.com/gh/support/pricing/international-card-transaction-fee-increase · https://www.lilachbullock.com/flutterwave-in-nigeria/

- **Why foreign African and Indian cards fail:** There are four failure points. (1) Merchant side: international cards are off on the Nigerian Flutterwave account until the request is approved. (2) Issuer side: banks in Ghana and elsewhere often ship cards with international or online use off, so the buyer must turn it on in the bank app; gh-link domestic cards do not work with foreign merchants. (3) 3DS or address checks failing, and vague 'Do not honor' declines. (4) Zimbabwe: payment providers' sanctions screening. An Australian government body reported Zimbabwean-issued credit cards being declined because of how its payment provider applied sanctions rules, while other countries' cards worked. EcoCash's physical Mastercard reportedly could not be used online; EcoCash offers a virtual card for online shopping. India: RBI circular RBI/2019-20/142 (2020-01-15) requires new cards to be enabled only for in-person use within India. Online and international use must be switched on by the cardholder. Put a one-line note for IN visitors: 'Enable international online payments in your bank app first.' ## Recommendations

- **Fix the existing Flutterwave account first, this week, before any new rail.** Why: The Ghana and Zimbabwe failures most likely come from international cards not being enabled, which Flutterwave requires a Nigerian merchant to request. Customer-pays-fee is on by default and puts an odd total in front of the buyer, which hurts conversion. Trade-offs: Approval can take more than 48 hours and isn't guaranteed. International cards cost 4.8% plus VAT against 2% local. Steps: 1) Dashboard > Transactions: open the two failed Ghana and Zimbabwe attempts and note the exact decline message and code. 2) Settings > Business Preferences > Payment Methods > 'request access' on international cards; keep the ticket ID. 3) If the account is Starter, register 'Called to Edit' as a CAC business name and upgrade the account. Missing documents is the first listed refusal reason, and it also lifts Starter limits. 4) Switch the fee bearer to merchant, so buyers see NGN 5,000, not NGN 5,000 plus fee. 5) Untick 'Enable Dashboard Payment Options' so the code can set payment_options per checkout. 6) Ask support in writing: (a) is any cap on the 2% local fee? (b) can this NG account collect in USD, GHS, KES and UGX, at what fee, settled in which currency? (c) can USD be held or paid out in USD? (d) the per-transaction and monthly limits on the account. 7) Once approved, run live tests: a USD card charge from a non-Nigerian card, and GHS and KES mobile money charges at the minimum amount.
- **Pick currency and payment methods by visitor country in the existing checkout function, and charge foreign buyers in fixed USD prices.** Why: Nigerians convert on transfer and wallets, Ghana, Kenya and Uganda on mobile money, and everyone else on cards. Showing NGN to a Ghanaian or Zimbabwean buyer adds currency friction and issuer FX checks. Trade-offs: Multi-currency charges on a Nigerian account count as international for Flutterwave pricing and settlement. Settlement-currency behaviour for GHS and KES is unconfirmed until support answers. Steps: In the checkout .mjs read context.geo?.country?.code and let a country switcher override it. NG: currency NGN, payment_options 'banktransfer, card, ussd, opay'. GH: GHS with Ghana mobile money and card, if the test passes; otherwise USD card. KE: KES 'mpesa, card'. UG: UGX 'mobilemoneyuganda, card'. ZW, ZA, IN, US, UK and the rest: USD 'card'. Keep a fixed, rounded USD price per offer in one config file; don't convert at a live rate. Show 'Pay in USD by card' plus a short note for IN, GH and ZW: 'Turn on international online payments in your bank app first.' Log country, currency, method and status to the Sheet for each attempt so conversion by country can be measured. Webhook: verify each charge by re-querying the transaction and checking amount, currency and tx_ref before granting access, and make the handler idempotent on tx_ref.
- **Add a no-build fallback for buyers whose card or rail fails: a Selar listing for each digital product (recordings, e-book, mini-webinars), linked from the failed-payment screen.** Why: Selar already handles M-Pesa, Ghana mobile money, PayPal, Stripe and Klarna for Nigerian sellers. It catches the Zimbabwe, India and odd-card cases that no gateway here fixes, and costs nothing until it sells. Trade-offs: Higher fees (up to 10% on USD and GHS per Selar's 2023 blog). Buyer data and email land in Selar first. Not suitable for community auto-renew or the 1:1 mentorship. Steps: Create matching Selar products at the same USD prices. On the Flutterwave redirect with status 'cancelled' or 'failed' for non-NG visitors, show 'Card didn't work? Pay another way' linking to the Selar product. Deliver from Selar's own delivery, or have the agent mark the buyer in the Sheet from Selar's sale email. Check Selar's current fee and payout terms in the dashboard before going live (the 4%-10% figures are from 2023).
- **Add Paystack as a second rail for Nigerian buyers only: the 1:1 hour, the 1-month mentorship, Academy cohorts, and dedicated virtual accounts for community renewals. Keep the NGN 5,000 and 10,000 items and all cross-border sales on Flutterwave.** Why: The fee comparison flips at about NGN 15,000-20,000: the NGN 500,000 mentorship costs about NGN 2,000 on Paystack against about NGN 10,750 on Flutterwave. Paystack adds PalmPay, OPay and Google Pay. Dedicated virtual accounts (1% capped at NGN 300) give transfer payers a permanent account number for monthly renewals, matched automatically by webhook. A second processor also guards against account freezes or downtime. Paystack does NOT help cross-border Africa: Nigerian Paystack accounts get no mobile money, and international cards are just cheaper there (3.9% + NGN 100 against 4.8%). Trade-offs: A second KYC, webhook and reconciliation path. Two settlement schedules. Paystack's VAT treatment and dedicated-account eligibility still need confirming. Steps: 1) Open a Paystack Nigeria account as the registered business; Starter accounts stop at NGN 8,000,000 and USD 5,000 lifetime. 2) Turn on international payments at sign-up or via Preferences, enable Google Pay, and check that OPay and PalmPay show at checkout. 3) Ask Paystack whether dedicated virtual accounts are on for your business and what customer data each account needs (BVN validation rules not verified). 4) Add a paystack-webhook.mjs next to the Flutterwave one, writing to the same Sheet with a 'rail' column. 5) Route by product price in the checkout function (NGN 20,000 and up to Paystack). If one gateway's create-checkout call errors, retry on the other.
- **Renewal path for community members who pay by transfer.** Why: Card tokenization covers card payers only. Most Nigerians pay by transfer, so without a renewal path those members lapse silently. Trade-offs: The email-link path still needs a buyer action each month. Dedicated virtual accounts depend on Paystack eligibility. Steps: Scheduled Netlify function daily: for members whose period ends in 3 days, email a prefilled one-tap checkout link (NGN, banktransfer first). Repeat on the due day and 3 days after, then remove the Discord role. If Paystack is added, give each transfer member a dedicated virtual account and treat any NGN 5,000 deposit into it as a renewal. Show the account number on the welcome email and in a Discord DM. Offer a discounted multi-month prepay to transfer payers to cut the monthly chase (price is Samuel's call).
- **Prepare for chargebacks.** Why: Flutterwave gives only 1 business day (local) or 2 (international) to respond, and charges USD 38 on international disputes. Trade-offs: A little more logging in each function. Steps: On every grant, write timestamped delivery evidence to the Sheet row keyed by tx_ref: access email sent, Discord role granted, recording link opened, live attendance. Keep a one-page evidence template the agent can fill on the same day. Put a clear refund policy line on each landing page and checkout.

## Fact-check

Recommendation stands: yes.

**Changes:** The core plan stands: fix Flutterwave first, route by country, add Paystack for high-ticket NGN sales, add a fallback. The checks force these changes.

1) CAC registration becomes a requirement, not an option. Paystack dedicated virtual accounts are 'only available to Nigeria-based registered businesses' (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2124866). The transfer-renewal plan through those accounts doesn't work on a Starter account. Register 'Called to Edit' before opening Paystack, or drop the virtual-account renewal and use only the emailed checkout link.

2) Plan for Paystack's 16-hour chargeback window, not Flutterwave's 1-2 days. Paystack gives Nigerian businesses 16 business hours, and a missed deadline means automatic acceptance and a full refund (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2127234). The mentorship and Academy sales would sit on Paystack, so the proof-of-delivery log (access email, Discord role, call attendance, keyed to the transaction reference) must be written automatically at grant time. The disputes email set in the Paystack dashboard must reach Samuel's phone. Build the evidence logging before taking the first NGN 500,000 sale on Paystack.

3) Prepare Paystack's international request for digital products. It wants a 'Valid website or social media page showing where the ebooks or digital products are sold' and full compliance documents (https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2127682). The calledtoedit.com product pages, refund policy and contact details should be live before applying.
