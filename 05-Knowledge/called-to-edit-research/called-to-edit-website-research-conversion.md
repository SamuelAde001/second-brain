# Website research: Conversion: what makes a creator-education site sell

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

The evidence points to one clear homepage design: one primary free offer above the fold, then a short "where are you?" router with three paths. A store-style grid of six offers is the wrong approach. The top creator-educator homepages checked today (Justin Welsh, Amy Porterfield, Marie Forleo) all lead with a free guide, training or video and put paid offers lower down. NN/g's eyetracking found 57% of viewing time goes to the first screen and 74% to the first two. Choice-overload research says too many options hurt most when the buyer is unsure what they want, which describes beginner editors. Unbounce's 2024 data for education pages favours short, plain copy: about 363 words at the median-converting page, and reading level at or below 12th grade converting 54% to over 2x better. About 85% of that traffic was mobile, and the top Nigerian phone width is 360 px (StatCounter, July 2026). Show every price in NGN, including the NGN 500,000. Hiding prices reads as evasive (NN/g), and Stripe reports 76% of buyers pick their local currency when offered. Put the strongest proof next to the most expensive offers, because reviews lift higher-priced items more (Spiegel: 380% vs 190%). Urgency should only ever be real. A 2022 meta-analysis (131 studies) found time limits work best for high-involvement purchases like a cohort, and the UK has fined fake timers under the DMCC Act since April 2025. No credible waitlist-to-buyer benchmark exists. Vendor guides say 3-7% for passive lists and 15-25% for engaged ones; a real 442-person cohort waitlist converted 2%. The best signal is engagement before launch: Maven buyers who attended 5+ free lessons were 15x more likely to buy. The case for a VSL is weak. Unbounce's study of about 35,000 pages found pages with embedded video converted lower than pages without, on mobile and desktop. Engagement falls with length (Wistia 2025: 50% under 1 min, 21% over 30 min). A VSL should therefore be an optional click-to-play facade with a face poster, captions and the length shown, and the page must sell without it. For the NGN 500,000 mentorship, the B2B data supports a short application with the calendar shown right after it. No independent study compares application forms with direct booking for coaching. With thin proof, show specific, consented proof (paid attendance, verbatim quotes, the Route Rise video) rather than small raw counts, which NN/g found can backfire. My web search budget ran out before I could check anchoring meta-analyses, Nigeria's FCCPC rules on urgency, and Flutterwave's multi-currency display.

## Findings

- **Homepage: first screen:** Most attention goes to the first screen: 57% of viewing time, and 74% for the first two screens. NN/g's 2024 homepage principles say to state plainly what you do, give top tasks the most visual weight, use specific CTA labels, and avoid autoplay and popups. EVIDENCE: NN/g eyetracking (article 2018-04-15): 57% of viewing time above the fold, 74% in the first two screens, down from 80% in 2010. NN/g 'Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles' (2024-03-15): a tagline that says what you do, no generic welcome, no 'Learn more' or 'Click here' labels, CTAs grouped by audience where useful, emphasis on top tasks, no autoplaying video, no popups unless legally required, avoid 'false floors'. It cites Google data that bounce likelihood rises 32% as load time goes from 1 s to 3 s. OPINION: on a phone the first screen should hold only the logo, the promise line, one sub-line naming who it is for, one primary CTA, Samuel's real face, and one micro-proof line. *(high, as of 2018 study; 2024-03-15 guidelines)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/
- **Phone viewport in Nigeria:** Design at 360 CSS px width. 360x800 and 360x806 are the two most common mobile resolutions in Nigeria, about 17% each. EVIDENCE: StatCounter Nigeria mobile, July 2026: 360x800 17.15%, 360x806 17.02%, 414x896 8.4%, 360x820 7.73%. Across all devices (June 2026), 360x800 leads at 18.27% and 1366x768 is the top laptop size at 4.89%. OPINION: Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp in-app browsers add their own toolbars, so the usable first screen is well under 800 px tall. Keep the hero, including the CTA button, inside about 600 px of height and check it in each in-app browser. *(high, as of 2026-07)* Sources: https://gs.statcounter.com/screen-resolution-stats/mobile/nigeria · https://gs.statcounter.com/screen-resolution-stats/desktop-mobile/nigeria
- **Copy length and reading level:** Education landing pages convert best with short, plain copy. The median-converting page has about 363 words, the lowest of any industry, and copy at or below 12th-grade reading level converts 54% to over 2x better than college-level copy. EVIDENCE (Unbounce 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report, education page): median conversion 8.4% vs 6.6% for all industries. Online course pages 18.3%, general course pages 13%. Mobile is about 85% of traffic; mobile converts 10.2% vs desktop 12%. Email traffic converts 14.1%, paid search 7.3%, paid social 5.1%. These are correlations across customer pages, not controlled tests. OPINION: this fits Samuel's 'few words' rule. Each standalone offer page should land near 300-450 words above the FAQ, and email should be his main conversion channel, since social traffic converts lowest. *(medium, as of 2024 report)* Sources: https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/education-conversion-rate/
- **What leading creator-educator homepages do:** Large creator-educators lead their homepage with a free offer that captures an email (guide, training or video). They do not lead with paid offers, and none of the three checked uses a 'choose your path' router. OBSERVED PRACTICE (fetched 2026-10-26; not conversion data): Justin Welsh: hero is a weekly essay with 'Read by over 200,000', and the only action is a free 30-page guide. No prices on the homepage, three bestselling-author testimonials, a short founder block, and a final email CTA. Amy Porterfield: a top banner and hero push a free training registration, followed by founder credibility ($130M revenue, 100,000+ students), a podcast ('60 Million +' downloads) and a testimonial carousel. Marie Forleo: hero offers a free audio/video, then founder intro, press logos, three paid offers each with an outcome-worded CTA ('Show Me the Profit System') and a 'Success Stories' link, then testimonials and email capture. CAVEAT: all three have huge proof that Samuel does not have yet, so copy their structure, not their numbers-led proof. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://www.justinwelsh.me/ · https://www.amyporterfield.com/ · https://www.marieforleo.com/
- **Choice overload:** Many options do not reliably hurt sales in general. They do hurt when the decision is hard and buyers are unsure what they want, which describes beginner editors facing six offers. EVIDENCE: Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd (2010) meta-analysis: mean effect near zero, with large variance between studies. Chernev, Böckenholt and Goodman (2015, Journal of Consumer Psychology) meta-analysis: the effect is reliable when task difficulty, choice-set complexity and preference uncertainty are high, and when the buyer wants to minimise effort. OPINION: beginners and stuck editors have high preference uncertainty. Show three stage-based paths with one recommended offer each, and keep the full six-item ladder below them for people who already know what they want. *(medium, as of 2010 / 2015 meta-analyses)* Sources: https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual/ · https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf
- **Routing visitors by stage:** Self-select routing works only when the groups are mutually exclusive, in plain language, labelled 'for …', and easy to switch between. Quiz-style routers that end in an email form capture about 8-11% of visitors. EVIDENCE: NN/g 'Audience-Based Navigation: 5 Reasons to Avoid It' (2015): users fit several groups or none, can't tell whether a section is 'about' or 'for' them, pay an extra step, and fear missing better content. It works when categories are mutually exclusive, use plain language and the word 'For', show their contents early, and let users switch. NN/g 2024 still endorses grouping CTAs by audience. ConvertFlow benchmark (September 2026, 1,063 campaigns): quiz landing pages capture a lead from 11.28% of visitors, embedded quizzes 10.45%, all formats 7.85%. Interact's '40%+ opt-in' figure is reported only second-hand and is unverified. OPINION: use symptom-based labels editors recognise, e.g. 'I'm still learning Resolve', 'I can edit but I can't get clients', 'I want Samuel 1:1'. Each card shows its own contents and price, and the cards are links, not a gate. *(medium, as of 2015 / 2024 / 2026-09)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/ · https://www.convertflow.com/benchmark-reports/lead-generation-quiz-funnel-benchmark-september-2026
- **Email capture mechanics:** Signup popups average about 4.8% conversion. Click-triggered (two-step) forms convert far better, at 54% of people who click. Full-page mobile overlays go against Google's December 2025 guidance. EVIDENCE: Wisepops (labelled 2026, published 2025-09-30, about 1B displays): average 4.82%; mobile 4.98% vs desktop 3.67%. By trigger: click 54.42%, scroll 5.37%, on landing 4.72%, exit intent 3.94%. By delay: immediate 4.16%, 1-5 s 5.93%, 6-10 s 5.98%, 11-15 s 6.45%, 16-20 s 2.95%, 21-30 s 1.53%. Google Search Central (updated 2025-12-10): don't cover the whole page with interstitials; small banners are acceptable. NN/g 2024: avoid popups. CAVEAT: click-trigger rates are high because those visitors already chose to click. OPINION: use inline email forms and buttons that open a small form. Optionally add a bottom slide-in banner after about 11-15 s or 50% scroll, never a full-screen modal. *(medium, as of 2025-09-30 / 2025-12-10)* Sources: https://wisepops.com/blog/popup-stats · https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/avoid-intrusive-interstitials · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/
- **Show every price, including NGN 500,000:** Hiding prices behind 'apply' or 'contact' makes a seller look evasive. Even a rough or 'from' price helps visitors judge fit, and UK law now penalises fees revealed late. EVIDENCE: NN/g (2013, B2B usability tests): participants got frustrated and left sites without prices, saw those companies as evasive, and the bad impression spread to the whole brand. Price ranges or sample scenarios help even when exact pricing is impractical. UK DMCC Act consumer provisions in force 2025-04-06: mandatory charges must be in the headline price, and the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover (matters for UK visitors). OPINION: list the mentorship as 'NGN 500,000 for 1 month, by application' rather than 'apply to see price'. Any card or processing fee the buyer pays should be in the shown price. *(medium, as of 2013 study / 2025 law)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/ · https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/winter-2025/cma-launches-first-dmcca-enforcement-cases-and-finalises-price-transparency-guidance/
- **Second currency:** Buyers prefer their own currency. Stripe reports 76% choose local currency when offered, and its local-currency pricing lifts cross-border revenue 17.8% and subscription conversion 4.7%. EVIDENCE (Stripe blog, 2026-06-04, vendor data, no method published): 76% choose local currency when offered; Adaptive Pricing gives a 17.8% cross-border revenue lift, a 5% higher authorisation rate, and 4.7% conversion uplift with 5.4% higher LTV per session for subscriptions. NOT VERIFIED: whether Samuel's Flutterwave account can display or charge GHS, KES, ZAR or INR. OPINION: show NGN as the price everywhere. Add one small grey line for visitors outside Nigeria (e.g. '≈ USD X · charged in NGN, your bank converts'), fed from one exchange rate he updates himself, and show it only by locale or IP. Never show two equal-weight prices, which doubles the reading on a 360 px card. *(medium, as of 2026-06-04)* Sources: https://stripe.com/blog/new-ways-to-turn-global-demand-into-revenue · https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/custom-checkout/adaptive-pricing
- **Anchoring and price order:** Showing the most expensive option first can raise sales of the top option, but the only direct test evidence is weak. Proof matters more at high prices. EVIDENCE: GoodUI Evidence #70 (2017-01-02, uptowork): ordering plans most expensive first gave a possible +14% in premium sales and -1.1% overall sales, with a net revenue gain. No sample size or significance reported. Spiegel Research Center (page updated 2025-12-04, research from 2017): reviews raised conversion 380% for higher-priced items vs 190% for lower-priced ones. NOT VERIFIED (search budget ran out): anchoring and compromise-effect meta-analyses. OPINION: on the homepage, order offers by stage, not price. On /mentorship, show NGN 500,000 first with 'Not ready for a month? Start with one 1-hour call: NGN 50,000' as the step down. On /academy, frame NGN 50,000 against the NGN 500,000 1:1 and against the price of one client edit. *(low, as of 2017 / 2025-12-04)* Sources: https://www.goodui.org/blog/evidence-70-least-or-most-expensive-plans-first/ · https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales
- **Which kind of scarcity works:** A 131-study meta-analysis found time limits work best for high-involvement purchases like a NGN 50,000 cohort. Combined 'limited time and limited quantity' messages did not reliably raise purchase intent in a 2023 meta-analysis. EVIDENCE: Barton, Zlatevska and Oppewal (Journal of Retailing 98(4), 2022), 416 effect sizes from 131 studies: demand-based scarcity works best for utilitarian products, supply-based for experiences, time-based for high-involvement products. Ladeira et al. (Psychology & Marketing, 2023), from the abstract via search summary, full text not read: scarcity from excess demand beat restricted supply, with no comparable advantage for 'urgency' scarcity (limited quantity plus limited time). OPINION: lead with the real enrollment deadline, the real early-bird end date and the cohort start date. Use 'X editors already enrolled' (demand-based) only once the number is real and not embarrassing. Use a seat cap only if feedback capacity really limits it. *(medium, as of 2022 / 2023)* Sources: https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/scarcity-tactics-in-marketing-a-meta-analysis-of-product-scarcity/ · https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/items/609e47cf-0367-4af7-82ac-9ab7154e2a2c/1
- **Fake urgency: what backfires:** Fake timers are common, documented and now enforced against: 393 countdown timers on 361 of about 11K shopping sites, some where the offer outlived the timer. The UK has fined misleading timers since April 2025. EVIDENCE: Princeton 'Dark Patterns at Scale' (ACM CSCW 2019): 1,818 dark-pattern instances on 1,254 sites, including 393 countdown timers on 361 sites, with examples where the deal stayed live after the timer expired. FTC report 'Bringing Dark Patterns to Light' (September 2022). UK DMCC Act from 2025-04-06: urgency claims must reflect a genuine time limit. The CMA opened its first 8 investigations on 2025-11-18 and sent advisory letters to 100 firms. No peer-reviewed study measuring the trust damage of fake timers was found; vendor claims of 9-40% timer lifts are unsourced. NOT VERIFIED: Nigeria FCCPC rules on fake urgency. OPINION: Samuel's buyers talk to each other in WhatsApp groups. One reset timer seen by two people sharing a link is worse than no timer. *(high, as of 2019 / 2022 / 2025-11)* Sources: https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns/ · https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light · https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/11/uk--competition-and-markets-authority-opens-investigations-into-online-pricing-practices · https://www.growthsuite.net/blog/real-urgency-vs-fake-urgency-why-your-countdown-timers-are-not-working
- **When buyers actually buy:** Course purchases cluster near the deadline and the start date. Creator reports put 40-75% of launch revenue in the final days, though that is anecdotal. EVIDENCE: CourseHorse enrollment analysis (school courses): 23% enrolled within 3 days of start, 59% within 14 days, only 5% more than 8 weeks ahead. Single-creator and affiliate reports: 40% of sales in the last 2 days; 74.8% of tracked revenue from deadline, extension and final-hours emails; 40-60% on the last day (Matt McWilliams, 2016). OPINION: build the deadline (early-bird end, enrollment close) into each cohort page and email plan. Expect the waitlist page to sit quiet and the last 48 hours to carry the launch. *(low, as of 2016-2026, mixed)* Sources: https://chblog.nobledesktop.com/blog/coursehorse-analytics-can-schools-anticipate-when-students-will-enroll · https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/how-close-sales-final-day-launch/ · https://createandgo.com/?p=34796
- **Waitlist-to-buyer rates:** No rigorous benchmark exists. Vendor guides say 3-7% for passive waitlists and 15-25% for engaged, incentivised ones. A documented 2025 cohort launch converted 9 of 442 waitlisters (about 2%). EVIDENCE: LaunchList, Learnybox and similar guides (2026): 3-7% unprimed, 15-25% engaged; a 20-35% claim is unsourced. Preston Chin (2025-06-29): 442 on the waitlist, 9 buyers at USD 797-997, about 2%. He says the list was inflated by auto-adding people through a link trigger; waitlist emails averaged 45% opens. Chaotic Creator: email subscribers converted better than Instagram viewers, comments predicted sales better than saves or shares, and the most viral cohort had the worst view-to-sale rate. Maven (2024-12-12, correlational): students who joined 5+ free Lightning Lessons were 15x more likely to buy. OPINION: count only people who opted in deliberately and answered a qualifying question. Run free or NGN 5,000 mini-sessions for the waitlist before launch, because engagement before launch is the best predictor available. *(low, as of 2024-12 / 2025-06 / 2026)* Sources: https://prestonchin.substack.com/p/my-failed-cohort-launch-made-7773 · https://chaotic-creator.beehiiv.com/p/40k-course-launch-exposing-my-conversion-rate-data · https://blog.getlaunchlist.com/blog/convert-waitlist-to-paying-customers · https://maven.com/resources/q4-2024-product-update
- **Launch email cadence:** Maven's 3-email cohort launch sequence matched the old 6-email sequence on conversion, with 60% higher clicks and 70% fewer unsubscribes. EVIDENCE (Maven help centre, updated 2025-09-05; company-reported, no sample size): for windows of 4+ weeks, emails go out 4 weeks before the enrollment deadline, 3 days before, and 24 hours before. For windows under 4 weeks, the first email goes 2 hours after opening. Copy pulls the syllabus and testimonials from the landing page. OPINION: for Samuel, send the same three to everyone. Add one 'waitlist-only early access' email 48 hours before public open, plus a WhatsApp broadcast, since Nigerian buyers live in WhatsApp. *(medium, as of 2025-09-05)* Sources: https://help.maven.com/en/articles/11071093-maven-cohort-launch-emails
- **Early-bird pricing:** No measured data was found on early-bird share or the best discount size for cohorts. Available guidance is practitioner opinion. OPINION ONLY: Guroo Academy suggests early-bird about 20% below standard and capped at no more than 25% of cohort capacity. CourseHorse timing data (59% enroll within 14 days of start) suggests early-bird windows mostly pull forward buyers who would have bought anyway. A real benefit, such as a bonus live session or a priority review slot, holds price better than a deep discount. *(low, as of 2026)* Sources: https://www.guroo.academy/pricing-plans · https://chblog.nobledesktop.com/blog/coursehorse-analytics-can-schools-anticipate-when-students-will-enroll
- **Webinar registration and show-up:** About half of webinar registrants actually attend: 57% across live and on-demand in ON24's 2024 data, 47.7% live in Livestorm's 2025 data. Smaller operators report 21-34%. Registration-page benchmarks are mostly blog numbers. EVIDENCE: ON24 2025 report (2024 B2B data), via MarketingProfs: 57% of registrants attend. Livestorm 2025: 47.7% live show-up across 33,786 sessions (second-hand). Ainora critique: 21-34% for small operators, because definitions differ. Webtonic (2026; mixes Unbounce and CommuniPass data, rows not attributed, UNVERIFIED): free webinar registration converts 8-14% cold and 18-28% warm. Unbounce 2024: online course pages median 18.3%. OPINION: Samuel's webinars are paid (NGN 5,000), and paying usually raises attendance, but no benchmark was found. Track his own registrant-to-attendee rate from the 78-seat event as the baseline. *(medium, as of 2024-2026)* Sources: https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/52917/b2b-webinar-benchmarks-conversion-attendance-personalization · https://ainora.lt/blog/webinar-attendance-statistics-every-study-2026 · https://www.webtonic.io/blog/education-coaching-landing-page-statistics
- **High-ticket: application vs direct booking:** No independent study compares application forms with direct booking for coaching. Large B2B data (2.9M form submissions, mid-2025 to mid-2026) shows a calendar right after a qualifying form gets 62% of qualified people to book, and wider availability raises that sharply. EVIDENCE: Chili Piper benchmark, July 2025 to June 2026, 2,931,118 form submissions, B2B SaaS buyers so transfer with care. 36% of all submissions and 62% of qualified ones book. Showing combined availability books 76% vs 18% for one rep's calendar. 40% of meetings are booked after hours, 14% on weekends. About 50% of meetings happen same or next day. Instant call vs scheduling-only: 38 vs 35 meetings per 100 forms (observational). PRACTITIONER, UNVERIFIED: Webtonic puts high-ticket ($2K+) application pages at 1-3% cold and 3-5% warm, and free discovery-call pages at 5-9% cold and 12-22% warm. Vendor playbooks claim applications filter tire-kickers above about USD 2,000, without data. OPINION: NGN 500,000 is high-consideration here, so use a short application then an instant calendar for a 20-30 min fit call. The NGN 50,000 one-hour call is cheap enough to sell as pay-then-book with no application. *(medium, as of 2025-07 to 2026-06)* Sources: https://www.chilipiper.com/reports/mid-market-conversion-benchmark · https://www.webtonic.io/blog/education-coaching-landing-page-statistics · https://membership.io/blog/high-ticket-coaching-funnel-the-call-first-playbook
- **No-shows:** Automated reminders alone are a weak fix: a US government randomised trial found no significant drop from SMS reminders. Vendor claims run to 28% fewer no-shows. EVIDENCE: US GSA Office of Evaluation Sciences RCT: no-shows 16.2% with SMS reminders vs 17.8% control, not statistically significant. Calendly (vendor): sales users cut no-shows 28% with automated reminders, no baseline or method published. OPINION: payment up front is the strongest show-up lever for the NGN 50,000 call. For free mentorship fit calls, book them within 48 hours and send a personal WhatsApp confirmation. *(medium, as of GSA study (date not shown) / Calendly 2025)* Sources: https://oes.gsa.gov/assets/abstracts/2313-abstract.pdf · https://calendly.com/blog/reduce-no-show-rates-sales
- **Does video lift landing-page conversion?:** Across about 35,000 Unbounce pages per period, pages with embedded video had LOWER median conversion than pages without, on mobile and desktop. The '86% lift' statistic comes from one old case study. EVIDENCE: Unbounce analysis (2021-08-26; three periods late 2020 to early 2021, about 35,000 customer pages each). Embedded-video pages had lower medians, with the gap worse on click-through pages. Background video was marginally higher on form pages. Video was neutral or negative across 10 industries. This is correlational: weaker pages may add video. VENDOR, CONFOUNDED: Vidalytics reports +49% conversion from 'Smart Autoplay' vs click-to-play on one VSL page (98% Bayesian probability). An earlier mobile autoplay variant with pausing disabled lost, 15% vs 30% engagement. OPINION: a VSL is a bet, not a default. Treat it as optional and make the text carry the sale. *(medium, as of 2021-08-26)* Sources: https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages · https://www.vidalytics.com/case-studies/autoplay-increases-conversions-split-test
- **Video length and engagement:** Engagement falls as videos get longer (Wistia 2025: 50% under 1 min, 38% at 5-30 min, 21% over 30 min). The viewers who stay through long videos act on CTAs more. EVIDENCE: Wistia 2025 State of Video (via Martech Cube and Chief Marketer, original not opened): average engagement 50% under 1 min, 46% at 1-3 min, 45% at 3-5 min, 38% at 5-30 min, 21% over 30 min. Overall engagement was at a four-year low. CTA action: 17% for videos over 30 min, 5% at 3-5 min, 2% for shorter (self-selection). Vidyard (2022 data, 1.78M videos): completion 66% under 1 min, 56% at 1-2 min, 50% at 2-10 min, 39% at 10-20 min, 22% over 20 min. OPINION by price (no direct evidence ties length to price): about 60-90 s for free and NGN 5,000 offers; 2-4 min for the community; 5-12 min or a recorded info session for the NGN 50,000 cohort; 3-6 min 'is this for you' for the NGN 500,000 mentorship, with the real selling on the call. *(medium, as of 2025 (Wistia) / 2022 data (Vidyard))* Sources: https://www.martechcube.com/?p=249151 · https://chiefmarketer.com/wistia-state-of-video-report-engagement-down-content-under-promoted · https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/49949/business-video-benchmarks-retention-rates-by-length
- **CTAs and email forms inside video:** Video CTAs convert about 16% on average (Wistia, 36,000+ CTAs, 2024). Email forms placed in a video are filled by about 1 in 5 viewers, but only about 5% when asked too early. EVIDENCE: Wistia (2024 CTA data, 2025 SoV charts): average CTA conversion about 16%; best CTA placement by length is first quarter under 1 min, last quarter at 1-3 min, midpoint at 3-5 min, end at 5-30 min. Wistia blog (2026-04-22, citing 2026 SoV): email forms about 20% overall; original series 30%, on-demand webinars 25%, sales videos 20%, testimonials and explainers 17%; asking too early gets about 5%. OPINION: inside the 3.5 h masterclass replay and free webinar replays, put an email or offer form at the end or a natural pause, not at the start. *(medium, as of 2024-2026)* Sources: https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/using-video-ctas · https://wistia.com/blog/collect-more-emails-with-video
- **Autoplay vs click-to-play:** Browsers allow muted autoplay. Usability research says don't autoplay: users mute or pause it, and moving content over 5 s needs a pause control. The only evidence for autoplay is one confounded vendor test. EVIDENCE: Chrome autoplay policy (2017-09-13, still current): muted autoplay always allowed; sound only after the user interacts with the domain (or, on mobile, a home-screen install). NN/g 'Video Usability' (2014) and homepage principles (2024): don't autoplay, show video length, give play, pause and mute controls. WCAG 2.1 (via NN/g): autoplaying motion over 5 s must be pausable. Vidalytics vendor test: +49% for muted autoplay with click-to-unmute-and-restart. OPINION: for Nigerian visitors on paid data, autoplay spends their bytes before they agree. Default to click-to-play with a poster. If Samuel wants to test autoplay, do it only on desktop or Wi-Fi visitors (navigator.connection where available) and only on one sales page. *(medium, as of 2014-2024)* Sources: https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/ · https://www.vidalytics.com/case-studies/autoplay-increases-conversions-split-test
- **Captions:** 69% of people watch video with the sound off in public, and 80% say they are more likely to finish a video that has captions. EVIDENCE: Verizon Media and Publicis Media survey, April 2019, 5,616 US adults aged 18-54: 69% watch sound-off in public, 25% even in private, 80% more likely to watch to the end with captions available. That is 80% of respondents, not '80% more likely'. US data, not African. OPINION: burn short captions into the VSL, which survives every in-app player, and also ship a WebVTT track plus a text transcript below for skimmers and search. *(high, as of 2019)* Sources: https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/ · https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx
- **Filming a face-to-camera VSL:** Talking-head video loses attention fast. Changing angles and expressions, plus on-screen text that matches what is being said, holds it: users looked at matching overlays 82% of the time they appeared. EVIDENCE: NN/g 'Talking-Head Video 2.0' (2017, single case study, sample size not given): users look at smiling faces looking at camera; attention drifts to the background or other links when visuals are static. Overlays matching the audio were looked at 82% of the time they appeared. Tell viewers the topic and length. NN/g 'Video Usability' (2014): keep the intro to 5 s or less, use a representative poster frame (can be mid-video), provide a transcript, and put essential information on the page as text because some users won't watch. OPINION: Samuel is an editor, so the VSL is itself proof of skill. Use jump cuts, punch-ins, B-roll of real client work (with permission) and text overlays. *(medium, as of 2014 / 2017)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/talking-head-video/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/
- **Keeping video light on mobile data:** A facade shows a static poster and loads the real player only when tapped, so no player code or video data loads until the visitor asks. That matters when 1GB costs NGN 500-800 on Nigerian networks. EVIDENCE: Chrome Lighthouse 'third-party facades' doc (2020-12-01; the audit was removed in Lighthouse 13) recommends paulirish/lite-youtube-embed, justinribeiro/lite-youtube, luwes/lite-vimeo-embed and slightlyoff/lite-vimeo. Trade-off: autoplay after lazy load may not work consistently. lite-youtube-embed claims about 224x faster rendering than the standard iframe and uses youtube-nocookie.com. TechCabal (2026-02-09): average NGN 575/GB in 2025 vs NGN 287.5 in 2024; 1GB costs NGN 500 on MTN and NGN 800 on Airtel. OPINION: for a self-hosted MP4 or HLS, use preload='none' plus a compressed poster image, start adaptive streaming at low resolution, and show the length on the poster (e.g. '2:40 · captions on'). *(high, as of 2020 doc / 2025 data prices)* Sources: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades · https://github.com/paulirish/lite-youtube-embed · https://techcabal.com/2026/02/09/how-nigerians-spent-%E2%82%A620-87billion-daily-on-data-in-2025/
- **VSL script structure:** Practitioner guides agree on one order: hook, problem, stakes, solution or mechanism, proof, offer, a single CTA. The hook has to land in the first 10-20 s. PRACTITIONER OPINION (no controlled evidence): Vidpros, Shootsta and similar 2025-26 guides give hook, problem, stakes, solution, proof, offer, one CTA. Longer variants add the mechanism, value stack, objection handling and close. Text-on-slides with voiceover can work as well as face-to-camera. EVIDENCE that supports a fast open: NN/g says keep the intro to 5 s or less. OPINION, Samuel's version: open with the gap between good editing and getting paid; state who it's for and who it isn't; name the 2-3 real reasons editors stay unpaid (no niche, no outreach system, underpricing); give Samuel's own proof (US B2B client, the 78 paid seats); explain the method; show exactly what the offer includes, its price in NGN and the real deadline; end with one action. *(low, as of 2025-2026)* Sources: https://vidpros.com/how-to-make-a-vsl/ · https://shootsta.com/guides/video-sales-letter-script · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/
- **Social proof when it's thin:** Small raw counts can backfire, and proof from familiar, similar people persuades most. The first few genuine reviews do most of the work: 5 reviews raise purchase likelihood 270% vs none. A perfect 5.0 rating is trusted less than 4.0-4.7. EVIDENCE: NN/g 'Social Proof in the User Experience' (2014): a participant judged an article weak because it showed only about 1,000 shares; the main risk is the impression that few people approve; approval from familiar people reduces uncertainty. Spiegel Research Center (2017 research, page updated 2025-12-04): 5 reviews give 270% higher purchase likelihood with diminishing returns after that; 380% lift for higher-priced vs 190% for lower-priced items; purchase likelihood peaks at 4.0-4.7 stars and falls toward 5.0; a 'verified buyer' badge adds 15%. NN/g 'Photos as Web Content' (2010): users study photos of real people and ignore stock images. OPINION: '78 editors paid NGN 5,000 to sit through 3.5 hours live' is strong because of the payment and the time, not the count. Don't show the community member count yet. Use 5-8 named, faced, specific quotes instead of a wall of anonymous praise. *(medium, as of 2010-2025)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/ · https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/
- **Consent and honesty rules for testimonials:** FTC rules: a testimonial showing above-typical results must say what people generally achieve, and fake or bought reviews can bring civil penalties (from 2024-10-21). Nigerian data-protection commentary says commercial use of a person's image needs express consent. EVIDENCE: FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ (guides revised 2023): ads featuring exceptional results must state the generally expected results; material connections (paid, free access, relatives, staff) must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials rule, finalised 2024-08-14, effective 2024-10-21: bans fake reviews (including AI-generated) and sentiment-conditioned incentives; up to USD 51,744 per violation at the time, adjusted for inflation. Nigeria: NDPA 2023 and the GAID (issued 2025-03-20, operational 2025-09-19). Commentary says express consent is needed for images used commercially and for direct marketing. Testimonials are not addressed directly, so applying it to them is inference. OPINION: collect consent with a checkbox on the feedback form ('Samuel can use my name, photo and this quote on calledtoedit.com and social'). Blur other people's names and phone numbers in WhatsApp or Discord screenshots. Give a free community month for a testimonial only without conditioning it on a positive review, and disclose it. *(medium, as of 2023-2025)* Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking · https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20241009-ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-testimonials-and-touts-new-rule-at-open-commission-meeting · https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1627428/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc · https://blog.lawpavilion.com/ndp-act-2023-gaid-2025-a-comprehensive-guide-to-nigerias-new-data-protection-landscape
- **About page that converts:** About-page visitors want a scannable summary (who, where, why, history, contact), plain language and real photos. They check the site's own testimonials against outside sources. EVIDENCE: NN/g 'About Us information on websites' and 'About Us summaries' research: users look for purpose, location, mission, values, history and contact; prefer plain language and real photos over stock and hollow phrases; compare on-site testimonials with third-party sources. OPINION: for Samuel, a 4-line summary at the top (Abuja-based editor; edits for US B2B brand Route Rise; coaches editors to get paid; founder of Called to Edit), then a short story, then links to proof that can be checked (Instagram @SamuelSignals, the Route Rise video, the webinar recording trailer), then the same 3-path router. *(medium, as of 2023 article; older studies)* Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/ · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-summaries/
- **Cohort vs self-paced outcomes (Academy page):** On Ruzuku's coaching courses, scheduled cohorts finished at 77.0% vs 38.5% for self-paced, and courses with discussions at 68.0% vs 45.3% without. EVIDENCE: Ruzuku Course Success Index, coaching (April 2026; 706 courses on 70 sites, 11,778 enrollments): scheduled cohort 77.0%, drip 62.1%, self-paced 38.5%; with discussions 68.0% vs 45.3%; with assessments 69.5% vs 53.3%. Not a random sample, and some cohort-size bands are tiny. OPINION: on /academy, use this to justify NGN 50,000 over the NGN 10,000 recorded masterclasses: you finish because of the schedule and the feedback. *(medium, as of 2026-04)* Sources: https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/course-success-index/coaching
- **Nigerian payment habits on the page:** Bank transfer is now the main way Nigerians pay online: 58% of Paystack's Nigerian transactions in 2023 (cards 36%), and 51.77% of Nigerian payments in Flutterwave's 2024 report. EVIDENCE: TechCabal (2024-05-14): Paystack transfers rose from 28% (2022) to 58% (2023) of Nigerian transactions; cards 36%. Techpoint: Flutterwave 2024, transfers 51.77% of payments in Nigeria. Sources cite low trust as a driver, since buyers want confirmation before completing a sale. No 2025 share found. OPINION: put 'Pay by bank transfer or card' under every price button. The card-only failures in Ghana and Zimbabwe show the payment step is a conversion problem as well as a technical one, and the page should name the fallback (e.g. 'Card not working? Pay another way'). *(high, as of 2023-2024 data)* Sources: https://techcabal.com/2024/05/14/paystack-bank-transfers/ · https://techpoint.africa/2024/05/16/transfers-might-displace-cards-soon/
- **In-app browsers (Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp):** Checkout inside in-app browsers converts worse. One vendor's randomised split showed 3.21% checkout conversion when traffic was pushed out to the real browser vs 1.24% staying in-app. Google does not support Sign in with Google inside embedded webviews. EVIDENCE (vendor, commercial interest): Escapehatch 50/50 splits on Instagram traffic: home-fragrance store 3.21% escaped vs 1.24% control; supplements +37%. Other vendors claim 30-50% lower conversion without sources. Mechanisms cited: autofill, saved cards, wallets and cookies break in webviews. Google's support guidance says embedded webviews are not supported for Sign in with Google. A Shopify merchant reported a fall from 7.8% to 0.86% on iOS webviews (anecdote). No TikTok-specific data. OPINION: no Google sign-in anywhere in the funnel; one-field email forms; detect the in-app user agent and, at the pay step only, show 'Having trouble? Open in Chrome' with a copy-link button. Test Flutterwave checkout in the IG, TikTok and WhatsApp webviews on a 360 px Android. *(low, as of 2025-2026)* Sources: https://getescapehatch.com/case-studies/home-fragrance · https://www.alphonsolabs.com/in-app-browser-checkout-test-checklist/ · https://community.shopify.com/t/conversion-rate-issue-in-mobile-safari-ui-wkwebview/380512

## Recommendations

- **Homepage order (calledtoedit.com): 1) Hero: 'I coach video editors to get paid.', one sub-line, an inline email field with the button 'Send me the free outreach guide', a real photo of Samuel, and one proof line ('78 editors paid to attend the first live webinar · I edit for US B2B brand Route Rise'). 2) Route Rise video testimonial as a facade, plus 3 consented quote cards. 3) 'Where are you right now?' with three stage cards. 4) The full ladder with NGN prices, each item linking to its own page. 5) A 4-line founder block linking to /about. 6) Next dates: next mini webinar, Academy waitlist. 7) FAQ: payment methods, international buyers, refunds, replays. 8) Final email capture.** Why: 57% of attention goes to the first screen (NN/g). Leading creator homepages put a free email offer first. Unbounce data favours about 363 words and plain language. Email is the channel that converts best (14.1%) once you have the address. Trade-offs: A free hero CTA means fewer people go straight to paid offers. That is acceptable, because paid offers are sold through email and the stage cards, and someone who arrives ready to pay still sees the ladder in one scroll. Steps: Build at 360 px first and keep the hero within about 600 px of height. Make the CTA label say the outcome, never 'Learn more'. Add a sticky bottom bar on mobile with the same primary CTA, appearing after the hero scrolls out. Check it in the Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp in-app browsers before launch.
- **Route by stage with three plain-language cards, not six offers: 'For editors still learning DaVinci Resolve' (Academy waitlist; fallback: free guide), 'For editors who can edit but can't get clients' (next NGN 5,000 mini webinar or the NGN 10,000 masterclass, plus the NGN 5,000/month community), and 'For editors who want Samuel 1:1' (NGN 500,000 mentorship; step down: NGN 50,000 one-hour call). Each card shows one recommended offer, its price, and one link.** Why: Chernev et al. (2015) found choice overload bites when buyers are unsure what they want, which fits beginners. NN/g says audience routing works only with mutually exclusive, plain 'For …' labels, visible contents and easy switching. Trade-offs: Some visitors fit two cards. Let every card link to /resources and the full ladder so nobody feels locked out, which is NN/g's 'missing content' worry. A 2-question quiz could capture more emails (ConvertFlow: about 11% lead capture) but adds a step. Test it later, not at launch. Steps: Name each card by a pain editors recognise, not by product. Tag clicks with ?path=learn|clients|1to1 so the email list is segmented from the first click.
- **Pricing display rules: NGN is the only large number. Add a small '≈ USD X · charged in NGN' line only for non-Nigerian visitors (by IP or locale). Show monthly price plus 'cancel anytime' for the community. Show the NGN 500,000 openly with 'by application'. Put 'Pay by bank transfer or card' under every pay button. Include any fees the buyer pays in the shown price.** Why: NN/g: hidden prices read as evasive. Stripe: 76% prefer their own currency when offered. Bank transfer is the majority method in Nigeria (58% of Paystack transactions in 2023). The UK DMCC Act penalises fees revealed late. Trade-offs: An approximate USD figure goes stale as the naira moves, so keep one rate in a config file and say '≈'. Two equal-weight currencies on a 360 px card hurt readability. Steps: Store the prices and the one exchange rate in a single JSON that every page reads. Geolocate in a Netlify Edge Function or from the Accept-Language header. Confirm with Flutterwave which currencies his account can present and charge (open question).
- **/mentorship as a two-tier page. NGN 500,000 one-month 1:1 at the top: who it's for and not for, the weekly structure, what success looks like, the 3 strongest named testimonials plus the Route Rise video, then 'Apply (2 minutes)'. The application has 5-6 questions (current level, niche, portfolio link, monthly income goal, biggest blocker, start month). Submitting it shows an embedded calendar for a 20-30 min fit call within the next 48 hours. Below that, the step down: 'Not ready for a month? Book one 1-hour call: NGN 50,000', which is pay first, then pick a slot, then a 3-question intake. Optionally credit the NGN 50,000 against the mentorship within 30 days.** Why: No independent evidence favours applications over booking for coaching. B2B data shows a calendar right after qualification books 62%, and wider availability books 76% vs 18%. Up-front payment is the strongest no-show defence; SMS reminders alone showed no significant effect in a government trial. Proof matters more at high prices (Spiegel: 380% vs 190%). Trade-offs: An application adds friction and some good buyers will skip it. The NGN 50,000 call gives them a direct path. Crediting the call fee lowers revenue per call but raises the chance of a NGN 500,000 close. That is opinion; test it. Steps: Application posts to the Apps Script CRM sheet. On success, show the Calendly or Google Calendar booking page embedded, with evening and weekend slots open (40% of B2B bookings happen after hours). Send a WhatsApp confirmation by hand for NGN 500,000 leads.
- **/academy as a waitlist page per cohort, e.g. 'DaVinci Resolve: Beginner to Fusion Nodes'. Above the fold: the outcome, the cohort month (or 'Next cohort: dates announced to the waitlist first'), 'NGN 50,000 · waitlist gets the early-bird price', and a form with email, first name, optional WhatsApp, and two dropdowns (current Resolve level; main goal). Promise exactly three things: 48-hour early access before public sale, the early-bird price, and one free live pre-lesson for the waitlist. Then: syllabus in 5 lines, cohort vs self-paced (Ruzuku: 77% vs 38.5% completion), Samuel's credential, FAQ.** Why: No credible waitlist benchmark exists. Passive lists convert poorly (a 442-person list converted 2%); engaged ones do better. Pre-launch engagement is the strongest predictor found (Maven: 5+ free lessons gave 15x purchase likelihood). Trade-offs: Qualifying dropdowns cut sign-ups slightly but make the count honest and let launch emails be personalised. Only ask what will actually be used. Steps: Launch sequence: pre-lesson invite, then a waitlist-only open email (48 h early), then public open. After that, Maven's three-touch cadence (open; 3 days before the enrollment deadline; 24 h before), each with a WhatsApp broadcast twin. Track waitlist-to-buyer % per cohort in the CRM sheet as the real benchmark.
- **Honest urgency system: every deadline is a fixed server-side date. When it passes, the price or availability really changes, and the page switches to 'Enrollment closed: join the waitlist for the next cohort'. Show 'X seats left' only if X is computed live from paid enrollments against a real cap set by Samuel's feedback capacity. No evergreen or reset timers anywhere.** Why: The 2022 meta-analysis found time limits work best for high-involvement buys like a cohort. Combined quantity-and-time urgency showed no reliable advantage (2023). Fake timers are documented at scale and now fined in the UK (DMCC Act, April 2025). His buyers compare notes in WhatsApp. Trade-offs: Real deadlines mean saying no to late buyers, unless he publicly offers one 24-hour extension, which creators report as a high-sales window. Decide that rule before launch. Steps: Keep the deadline in the same prices JSON. A Netlify Function returns server time and seat count. The countdown renders from server time, not the visitor's clock. The webhook updates the seat count from Flutterwave payments.
- **/webinar (paid NGN 5,000 mini webinars): one screen with title as outcome, date and time in WAT plus auto-converted local time, length, price, and a pay button. Then 3 bullets on what you'll leave with, who it's for and not for, 3 quotes from the 2026-10-23 attendees, and a FAQ (replay? refund? which app?). After payment: an add-to-calendar file, the Meet link by email and WhatsApp, and reminders at 24 h and 1 h.** Why: Online-course pages have the highest education median in Unbounce's data (18.3%). Roughly half of registrants attend (ON24 57%, Livestorm 47.7%), so show-up mechanics decide how many reach the upsell. Trade-offs: Promising a replay can lower live attendance. Opinion; decide per event. Live is where the community upsell happens. Steps: Log registrant-to-attendee % per event in the CRM sheet. The first event's real attendance is the baseline to beat.
- **/community (NGN 5,000/month): hero says what you get every week (live call day and time WAT, feedback turnaround, accountability check-ins, the AI bot trained on Samuel's teachings), the price with auto-renew stated plainly, 'cancel anytime', and the join button. Then a screenshot of a real feedback thread, a 20-30 s screen capture of the bot answering an editor question, the first-week onboarding, and 3 member quotes. Don't show the member count until it helps.** Why: NN/g found small raw numbers can backfire. Concrete weekly deliverables and real screenshots are specific proof. Plain auto-renew wording avoids drip and subscription complaints. Trade-offs: Not showing the member count gives up a social-proof cue. Revisit once the number is persuasive. Steps: Get consent from members whose messages appear. Blur names and handles of anyone who didn't consent.
- **VSL plan: don't put a VSL in the homepage hero. Put a 60-90 s 'who I am and who this is for' video below the hero on the homepage, a 2-4 min video on /community, a 5-12 min video (or a recorded info session) on /academy at launch, and a 3-6 min 'is this for you' video on /mentorship. Use preload='none', low-resolution adaptive start, burned-in captions plus a WebVTT track, and a text transcript below. Every page must sell fully without the video.** Why: Unbounce's study of about 35k pages found embedded video correlated with lower conversion. Engagement falls with length (Wistia 2025). NN/g and WCAG argue against autoplay. 69% watch sound-off in public. Nigerian data costs NGN 500-800 per GB. Trade-offs: Click-to-play gives up the one vendor-reported autoplay lift (+49%, confounded). Self-hosted HLS costs more setup than a YouTube facade. Show an in-video CTA or email form at the end or at the midpoint for 3-5 min videos (Wistia placement data). Track play rate and the next click per page.
- **VSL script (structure only): 0-10 s hook naming the gap ('You can edit. So why aren't you getting paid?'). Who it's for and not for. The 2-3 real reasons editors stay unpaid (no niche, no outreach system, underpricing, invisible personal brand). Samuel's proof (Abuja editor working for a US B2B client; 78 paid seats; one student or attendee quote). The method he teaches. Exactly what this offer includes, its NGN price and the real deadline. One CTA. Film it like an editor: punch-ins, angle changes, B-roll, text overlays that match the words.** Why: Practitioners agree on hook, problem, stakes, mechanism, proof, offer, CTA. NN/g says intros of 5 s or less and changing visuals hold attention, and matching overlays were looked at 82% of the time. Trade-offs: This is opinion-level evidence. Steps: Each product gets its own URL (/resources/outreach-guide etc.) that works as a standalone landing page with the same card plus 3 bullets and one quote.
