# Website research: Payments: India and the rest of the world

Part of [[05-Knowledge/called-to-edit-research/called-to-edit-website-research|Called to Edit website research]] (2026-10-26).

This setup needs three rails, and the cheapest working version needs no US company. (1) Nigerians: keep Flutterwave (NGN bank transfer and cards, with the card-saving auto-renew already built). Add Paystack only for big NGN tickets: its local fee is capped at NGN 2,000 and its dedicated-virtual-account transfers at NGN 300. Flutterwave charges a flat 2% plus 7.5% VAT on the fee, and its page shows no cap, so a NGN 500,000 mentorship costs about NGN 10,750 there. (2) Other Africans: still Flutterwave, but charge in the buyer's own currency (GHS, KES, ZAR, UGX, RWF, TZS) so Ghana mobile money and M-Pesa appear. Flutterwave hides those methods when you charge in NGN, which is probably part of why the Ghana buyer failed. Flutterwave has no Zimbabwe method, so Zimbabwe goes to the USD rail. (3) Rest of world: Polar.sh as merchant of record for the e-book, recorded masterclasses and the USD community. Polar pays out to Nigerian bank accounts through Stripe, handles VAT/GST, auto-grants Discord roles, and has clean webhooks. Polar, Lemon Squeezy, Paddle, Dodo and Stripe Managed Payments all ban 1:1 coaching, so foreign mentorship buyers pay by Flutterwave or Paystack international card. Lemon Squeezy also pays to Nigeria, but it says it is moving users to Stripe Managed Payments, which does not accept Nigeria-based businesses or coaching. Paddle is built for software and bans coaching and community access. Dodo Payments no longer onboards Nigerians. dLocal Go accepts Nigerian merchants but has no Indian buyers. Gumroad's Stripe payout list leaves out Nigeria. UPI for India: of the options checked, only Stripe through a US company (Stripe Atlas) clearly allows a Nigerian-owned business to take it (1.8% + 30c, plus 1.5% international and 1% FX; AutoPay up to INR 15,000). That costs USD 500 at setup, then USD 100 a year for the registered agent, USD 400 a year Delaware LLC tax (Delaware's own figure; Atlas's page still says 300), and a yearly Form 5472 that carries a USD 25,000 penalty if missed. Stripe direct is only about 1.1% + 20c cheaper per sale than Polar, so the LLC pays off through UPI and through selling coaching to foreigners, not through lower fees. Until then, sell Indian buyers one-time multi-month passes, not auto-renew: RBI rules make recurring charges on Indian cards fragile (24-hour pre-debit notice, extra check above INR 15,000, cards domestic-only by default). Routing: Netlify geolocation (context.geo.country.code) picks the default currency and rail on each offer page. A visible 'Paying from Nigeria / elsewhere' switch overrides it. Never auto-redirect the offer URLs. Take the email before checkout and pass it in. The web search budget ran out partway through, so later checks used official pages fetched directly. Selar, Mainstack, Whop's Nigeria payouts, PayPal-through-Paga merchant receiving, Stan, Teachable and Thinkific payouts stay partly unverified.

## Findings

- **Polar.sh (merchant of record):** Polar pays out to Nigeria (and Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, India) through Stripe Connect Express cross-border transfers. Organisations created after 2026-05-27 pay 5% + 50c on the free plan, plus 1.5% on non-US cards. Plans: Starter free at 5% + 50c; Pro USD 20/mo at 3.8% + 40c; Growth USD 100/mo at 3.6% + 35c; Scale USD 400/mo at 3.4% + 30c. The 4% + 40c 'Early Member' rate covers only organisations created before 2026-05-27. Stripe's payout fees, passed on with no Polar markup: USD 2 per month with active payouts, 0.25% + 25c per payout, and a 1% cross-border fee outside the EU. Disputes cost USD 15. Refunds do not return fees. Polar does not sell to US-sanctioned countries (Cuba, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Syria), so Zimbabwean buyers are allowed. Stripe's docs say Nigeria is 'only available for cross-border payouts accounts' (NUBAN + SWIFT). Whether the money lands as NGN or USD is not documented. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/supported-countries · https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees · https://docs.stripe.com/connect/payouts-bank-accounts
- **Polar.sh: what it allows and what it can do:** Polar lists courses, eBooks and 'Discord server access' as acceptable. It prohibits 'human services' (so 1:1 coaching), lists 'community access' as prohibited, and bans '"Get Rich" schemes or content' (policy effective 2026-10-01). The Discord-access and community-access lines contradict each other, so ask Polar to approve the community before relying on it. Discord benefit: Polar sends invites and grants roles to subscribers automatically. Webhooks: order.paid, order.refunded, subscription.active, subscription.canceled, subscription.revoked, subscription.past_due, among others. Checkout: hosted, or embedded on your own site (domain must be validated first). Apple Pay and Google Pay work on hosted checkout without setup; on embedded checkout you must email support to turn them on. A product can carry several currencies, and Polar picks country and currency from the buyer's IP, so fixed per-currency (PPP-style) prices are possible. Polar's docs do not mention PayPal or UPI, and do not list which currencies are supported. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/acceptable-use · https://polar.sh/docs/features/benefits/introduction · https://polar.sh/docs/integrate/webhooks/events · https://polar.sh/docs/features/checkout/embed · https://polar.sh/docs/features/checkout/session
- **Lemon Squeezy (Stripe-owned):** Lemon Squeezy pays out to Nigerian banks today. Its 2026-01-28 post says the goal is to move users to Stripe Managed Payments, and points businesses in countries Stripe doesn't support to Stripe Atlas. Fees: 5% + 50c; +1.5% outside the US; +1.5% for PayPal; +0.5% on subscriptions. Payouts: 1% to non-US banks; 3% (capped at USD 30) to non-US PayPal. USD 50 minimum payout, 13-day hold, paid twice a month. Payments always settle in USD, even though prices show in 130 currencies. Buyers can pay with cards, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Alipay, WeChat Pay and Cash App. Subscriptions take cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal only. No UPI. A custom domain works for checkout links (single A record). It has a lemon.js overlay. It bans 'Services of any kind (including ... consulting ...)' and lists get-rich-quick schemes among regulated products. Webhooks include order_created, subscription_created/updated/cancelled/expired and subscription_payment_success/failed/recovered. The 2026 post gives no shutdown date and does not say whether non-Stripe countries can stay. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/getting-paid · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/checkout/payment-methods · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/currencies · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/domains/adding-a-custom-domain · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products · https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/webhooks/event-types · https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/2026-update
- **Stripe Managed Payments (where Lemon Squeezy is heading):** Managed Payments accepts only businesses based in the US, Canada, most of Europe, Australia, Hong Kong, Japan and Singapore, so not Nigeria. It sells only fully automated digital products and excludes live 1:1 coaching and professional services. Online courses, e-books and digital video are eligible. 'If your service involves human intervention (such as live 1-1 coaching), it doesn't qualify.' It costs 3.5% on top of Stripe's normal payment fees. Buyers from most countries can pay; restricted buyer countries include China, Cuba, Iran, Russia, Syria and North Korea. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility · https://stripe.com/pricing
- **Paddle:** Paddle is a poor fit. It is 'built to serve software companies' and bans coaching ('pure consulting or advisory services, including ... coaching') and standalone 'community access'. It added domestic UPI in INR, including UPI Autopay for subscriptions, on 2026-06-17. Price 5% + 50c. Nigeria is not on its list of unsupported seller countries; Zimbabwe is. UPI limits: INR 100,000 per transaction, INR 15,000 for any checkout with a recurring item, and a pre-debit notice at least 24 hours before each renewal. The changelog calls UPI both 'early access' and 'Released'. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle · https://www.paddle.com/support/countries-supported · https://developer.paddle.com/changelog/2026/upi-autopay/ · https://www.paddle.com/pricing
- **Dodo Payments (merchant of record with UPI and RuPay):** Dodo is ruled out. Nigeria is not on its accepted merchant-country list, only in a 'grandfathered' note for existing merchants, and eligibility also follows the ID country of every director. It also bans coaching and consulting. Fees: 4% + 40c; 4% + 15c for Indian domestic payments; +1.5% international; +0.5% subscriptions; USD 5 fee on payouts under USD 1,000; USD 25 for SWIFT USD payouts to non-US businesses. Ghana, South Africa and Zimbabwe are on its accepted list. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/accepted-countries-and-territories.md · https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/merchant-acceptance.md · https://dodopayments.com/pricing
- **Gumroad:** Gumroad is the merchant of record (it handles VAT/GST) and charges 10% + 50c on direct sales and 30% on Discover sales. Nigeria is not on its Stripe Connect payout list (41 countries), which leaves PayPal as the only payout route for Nigerians. Whether a Nigerian PayPal account linked to Paga can receive Gumroad payouts has not been tested. The country list comes from a mirror of Gumroad's open-source docs, not Gumroad's help centre. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://gumroad.com/pricing · https://mintlify.wiki/antiwork/gumroad/payments/stripe
- **PayPal in Nigeria (2026):** PayPal and Paga announced on 2026-01-27 that Nigerians can link PayPal to a Paga wallet, receive money from 200+ countries and withdraw in naira or USD. Paga's own page still shows 'Coming soon' near 'Accept payments from anywhere', and publishes no fees or limits. No hands-on 2026 user reports or fee schedule were found. Unconfirmed: whether a Paga-linked account can take PayPal checkout payments, invoices, or payouts from platforms such as Gumroad or Lemon Squeezy. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://www.dabafinance.com/en/news/paypal-paga-nigeria-inbound-payments · https://www.mypaga.com/home/pagapaypal · https://allafrica.com/stories/202601270689.html
- **Paystack (Nigeria):** Nigeria fees: local channels 1.5% + NGN 100 (the NGN 100 is waived under NGN 2,500), capped at NGN 2,000. International cards 3.9% + NGN 100; Amex 4.5%. USD transactions 3.9%. Dedicated virtual accounts 1%, capped at NGN 300. Since 2026-02-18 there is a NGN 50 stamp duty on Paystack transfers of NGN 10,000 or more. Paystack announced Apple Pay for Nigerian and Ghanaian businesses (the blog date could not be fetched). A third-party report says USD use is limited to Nigerian and Kenyan merchants and that a CAC registration is effectively required for international payments; Paystack's own pages blocked fetching (403), so that is unconfirmed. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2130306 · https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/7573314 · https://paystack.com/blog/product-engineering/apple-pay · https://techpoint.africa/news/paystack-expands-apple-pay-cote-divoire-south-africa
- **Flutterwave (Nigeria) international and African collections:** Flutterwave Nigeria charges 2% on local payments (1.4% + 0.6% platform fee) and 4.8% on international cards, plus 7.5% VAT on the fee. Its pricing page shows no cap. African mobile money only appears when you charge in that country's currency. Methods by currency: GHS = card + Ghana mobile money; KES = card + M-Pesa; ZAR = card, account, 1Voucher; UGX, RWF and TZS = card + mobile money; XOF/XAF = card + francophone mobile money. Nothing for Zimbabwe. 'We'll ignore any [payment options] that don't apply to the current currency.' International cards are 'charged and settled in your local currency by default.' Which non-NGN currencies a Nigerian merchant account can collect is set at account approval, so ask support. Webhooks: charge.completed and subscription.cancelled, checked with the verif-hash header, retried 3 times at 30-minute intervals. Payouts cost NGN 10, 25 or 50 depending on size. No official explanation was found for the Ghana and Zimbabwe card failures. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://flutterwave.com/ng/pricing · https://developer.flutterwave.com/v3.0/docs/payment-methods · https://developer.flutterwave.com/v3.0/docs/mobile-money-1 · https://developer.flutterwave.com/v3.0/docs/webhooks
- **Stripe via a US LLC (Stripe Atlas):** Atlas costs USD 500 one-time (includes state fees and the first year of registered agent), then USD 100 a year for the agent. Delaware's own site puts the annual LLC tax at USD 400, due June 1, with a USD 200 penalty plus 1.5% interest a month if late; Atlas's docs still say USD 300. A foreign-owned single-member LLC must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, and the penalty for not filing is USD 25,000. Without a US SSN, the EIN takes 10-35 business days. US card payments can start before the EIN arrives; other payment methods only after. Atlas cannot guarantee Stripe will approve the business and checks restricted categories. Its pages do not say whether Nigerian founders are eligible. Stripe US pricing: 2.9% + 30c; +1.5% international cards; +1% currency conversion; Billing 0.7%; Tax 0.5% (no-code); Managed Payments +3.5%. A US Stripe account can also accept Naira cards and bank transfers with USD settlement through a Nigerian merchant-of-record partner (invite-only preview; those disputes can't be contested). *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://stripe.com/atlas · https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup · https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes · https://corp.delaware.gov/alt-entitytaxinstructions/ · https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472 · https://stripe.com/pricing · https://docs.stripe.com/payments/countries/nigeria
- **UPI for a Nigerian seller:** Stripe accounts in the US, UK, EEA, Switzerland, Canada, Australia and Singapore can accept UPI in INR, one-time and recurring (UPI AutoPay). Pricing is 1.8% + 30c, plus 1.5% international and 1% FX. UPI payments run from INR 1 to INR 100,000, with recurring capped at INR 15,000. Of the options checked, this is the only route clearly open to a Nigerian owner, and only through a US entity. Paddle has UPI but bans coaching and community. Dodo has UPI but excludes Nigeria. Lemon Squeezy lists no UPI. dLocal Go accepts Nigeria-registered merchants but its buyer countries leave out India. Razorpay offers 'foreign (non-Indian) businesses' cards, UPI and netbanking from Indian buyers, settled abroad, by request form; whether a Nigerian entity qualifies is not stated. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.stripe.com/payments/upi · https://stripe.com/pricing · https://helpcenter.dlocalgo.com/en/articles/7240022-where-does-my-company-have-to-be-registered-if-i-want-to-open-a-dlocal-go-account · https://helpcenter.dlocalgo.com/en/articles/7229140-in-which-countries-can-i-sell-with-dlocal-go · https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/
- **RBI rules affecting Indian buyers:** Since 2020-03-16, Indian cards are issued for domestic in-person use only; buyers must switch on online and international payments themselves. Recurring or off-session charges on Indian cards or UPI need a registered e-mandate, a pre-debit notice at least 24 hours before each charge, and extra authentication on every charge above INR 15,000. Stripe says these rules apply to non-Indian merchants too: 'Without a mandate for an off-session payment, the payment will be declined.' Stripe holds card renewals for about 26 hours. For a non-INR subscription, a mandate is only registered if an Indian card is attached when the subscription is created. A processor without e-mandate support will see Indian auto-renewals fail. *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11788 · https://docs.stripe.com/india-recurring-payments
- **Netlify geolocation for routing buyers:** Both Edge Functions and Netlify Functions get context.geo.country.code (ISO 3166). Netlify does not add geo headers. Redirect rules can match Country=xx, and an nf_country cookie can override it. Country redirects run on Netlify's CDN using GeoIP. The docs say nothing on how accurate it is. In Netlify Functions, geo is also available through getContext(). *(high, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.netlify.com/edge-functions/api/ · https://docs.netlify.com/build/functions/api/ · https://docs.netlify.com/routing/redirects/redirect-options/
- **Chariow (African creator platform):** Chariow charges 15% per sale (10% after USD 5,000 in sales) with no monthly fee. It pays out about 72 hours after a sale to local banks in 30+ African countries, mobile money or international banks. Buyers pay by M-Pesa, Orange Money, MTN MoMo or cards. Its changelog says naira payments from Nigerian buyers work, but local naira withdrawals for Nigerian creators are 'coming soon'. Mostly francophone. These figures come from Chariow's own llms.txt summary; the pricing page wasn't fetched. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://chariow.com/llms.txt · https://roadmap.chariow.com/en/changelogs/vos-produits-sont-desormais-accessibles-depuis-le-niger
- **Selar (Nigerian, multi-currency):** Selar lets one page show USD to international buyers and NGN or GHS to locals, with USD virtual accounts or local payouts (April 2026 post). The latest fees found are old: USD/GBP 7% + 50c (May 2023) and NGN 4% + NGN 50 (June 2022). In 2023 Selar said international buyers pay through Stripe (verified creators) and PayPal. In 2022, USD/GBP sales reached the wallet after 10 days. No current fee table, no USD subscription details, and nothing on VAT handling for foreign buyers was found. *(low, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://selar.com/blog/how-to-earn-in-dollars-in-africa-without-a-us-bank-account/ · https://selar.com/blog/?p=5392 · https://selar.com/blog/how-to-sell-online · https://selar.com/blog/selar-vs-gumroad/
- **Mainstack:** Mainstack takes USD or GBP from buyers anywhere and either converts to naira or holds USD on its own MSB-licensed infrastructure. A subscription tier is rolling out. No official fee schedule could be retrieved. Its pages render with JavaScript, so fees, membership features and webhooks were not verified. *(low, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://www.launchafrica.vc/mainstack
- **Whop:** Whop charges 2.7% + 30c on domestic cards, +1.5% international, +1% FX, plus an optional 2% tax-remittance add-on. It pays out to '200+ countries', including crypto (5% + USD 1). Whether it pays out to Nigerian banks, how it verifies Nigerian sellers, and whether it is the merchant of record were not confirmed. *(medium, as of 2026-10-26)* Sources: https://docs.whop.com/fees

## Recommendations

- **Rail 1, Nigeria: keep Flutterwave as the main NGN processor (bank transfer, USSD, cards, saved-card auto-renew for the community). Use Paystack, or a manual bank-transfer invoice, for NGN 50,000+ tickets.** Why: Flutterwave's 2% + VAT is cheapest at NGN 5,000. At NGN 500,000, Paystack's NGN 2,000 cap, or 1% capped at NGN 300 on dedicated virtual accounts, beats about NGN 10,750 on Flutterwave (its page shows no cap). Trade-offs: A second processor means a second webhook and reconciliation. A third-party page claims Flutterwave has a NGN 2,000 cap; if it does, Paystack adds little and can be skipped. Steps: Open Paystack (CAC registration is likely needed for international). Make one Paystack payment page for mentorship. Point charge.success to the same Netlify Function that handles Flutterwave.
- **Rail 2, other Africa: charge Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, Rwanda, Tanzania and francophone visitors in their own currency through Flutterwave, so mobile money appears. Send Zimbabwe and unsupported countries to the USD rail.** Why: Flutterwave shows Ghana MoMo and M-Pesa only when you charge in GHS or KES; an NGN charge forces a foreign card payment, which issuers often decline. Flutterwave has no Zimbabwe method. Trade-offs: The Nigerian account must be approved for each collection currency. Local prices need FX upkeep. Settlement may come back in NGN by default. Steps: Ask Flutterwave support to enable GHS, KES, ZAR, UGX, RWF, TZS and XOF collection and mobile money. Pass currency and payment_options by country. Pull the decline codes on the failed Ghana and Zimbabwe attempts from the dashboard.
- **Rail 3, rest of world (now, no company): Polar for the e-book, recorded masterclasses, Academy cohorts and a USD community tier. Use Flutterwave or Paystack USD card links for foreign mentorship buyers.** Why: Polar is a merchant of record that pays to Nigerian banks, handles VAT/GST, offers fixed per-currency prices picked by IP, grants Discord roles, and sends clean webhooks. Lemon Squeezy is heading to Stripe Managed Payments, which accepts neither Nigeria-based businesses nor coaching. Paddle and Dodo are ruled out. Trade-offs: New orgs pay 5% + 50c + 1.5%, about 11.5% on a USD 10 item, plus 1% cross-border and 0.25% + 25c per payout. Polar bans human services and lists 'community access' as prohibited, so the community and live cohorts need written approval. No UPI or PayPal confirmed. Steps: Before building, email Polar describing the products as video-editing and client-acquisition education with no income claims, and get written approval for the community and live cohorts. Set USD prices plus any local-currency prices, choose Discord benefit or own bot, subscribe to order.paid, subscription.active, subscription.revoked and subscription.past_due, and send a small first payout to confirm NGN or USD.
- **Indian buyers: until a US company exists, sell them one-time 3-, 6- or 12-month community passes through Polar, not auto-renew. Say on the page that the card must be enabled for international online payments.** Why: RBI rules (domestic-only cards by default, e-mandate, 24-hour pre-debit notice, check above INR 15,000) make recurring charges on Indian cards fail when the processor can't register mandates. Trade-offs: More manual renewals. No UPI means some Indians won't pay at all. Steps: Detect IN, show an INR price if Polar supports INR, offer only fixed-term products, and email a renewal link before expiry.
- **Phase 2 (only when India or foreign-coaching demand is proven): form a Delaware LLC with Stripe Atlas and run Stripe Checkout for India (UPI incl. AutoPay ≤ INR 15,000) and for foreign coaching. Keep Polar or use Managed Payments for eligible digital goods.** Why: It is the only verified way for a Nigerian-owned business to take UPI, and Stripe allows coaching where merchant-of-record platforms don't. Direct Stripe (about 5.4% + 30c for a foreign card with FX) saves only about 1.1% + 20c per sale against Polar, so do it for the payment methods, not the fees. Trade-offs: Fixed cost about USD 500 to start, then about USD 500 a year (agent + Delaware tax) plus an accountant for Form 5472 / pro forma 1120 (USD 25,000 penalty if missed). Without Managed Payments you own VAT/GST. Nigerian tax on LLC income needs advice. Stripe approval isn't guaranteed. Steps: Check Atlas eligibility for a Nigerian founder and the restricted categories. Open a US bank account (Stripe Treasury or an Atlas partner). Enable UPI, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Link. Ask for the Naira preview only if useful.
- **Routing: Netlify geolocation sets the default; a visible switch decides. Run a tiny Edge Function on /webinar, /community, /academy, /mentorship and /resources that writes context.geo.country.code into the HTML (e.g. data-cc). It picks the price and checkout button (NG → Flutterwave NGN; GH/KE/ZA/UG/RW/TZ/XOF → Flutterwave local currency; everyone else → Polar USD). Never redirect the offer URLs.** Why: Each offer link must work and share as a standalone page. Geolocation is server-side, so it works inside Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp browsers. Writing the country at the edge saves Nigerian phones an extra request. Trade-offs: IP geolocation misreads VPN and proxy-browser users and Nigerians abroad who pay from Nigerian accounts. The switch fixes that but adds one choice. Edge Functions run on every request, so keep them small. Steps: Use one Edge Function with config.path set to the offer pages. Add a 'Paying from Nigeria? / Paying from elsewhere?' link near the price and store the choice in a cookie that wins over geolocation. Skip _redirects Country rules for these pages.
- **Take the email before any checkout and pass it into every rail. Normalise all payment webhooks in one Netlify Function that writes to the Google Sheet and grants Discord roles.** Why: Samuel wants every visitor to leave an email, and this enables abandoned-checkout follow-up across rails. One handler keeps the CRM and Discord roles consistent whichever processor took the money. Trade-offs: Polar's built-in Discord benefit is easier but splits role management across two systems. Steps: Verify each webhook signature (Flutterwave verif-hash; Polar and Lemon Squeezy signatures). Skip duplicate events using Netlify Blobs. Map paid → grant role and revoked/cancelled → remove role.
- **Keep income claims off product titles, descriptions and checkout text on any merchant-of-record platform. Describe products as video-editing and career skills education.** Why: Polar bans 'Get Rich' schemes, Lemon Squeezy lists get-rich-quick as regulated, and Dodo restricts income promises. A brand line like 'get paid' can trip review and freeze payouts. Trade-offs: Sales copy on the landing page can stay bolder than the product descriptions the platform reviews. Steps: Write a separate plain product description for Polar, and keep proof (testimonials) on the site rather than in checkout metadata.

## Fact-check

Recommendation stands: yes.

**Changes:** The three-rail design stands: Flutterwave as the NGN core, local-currency Flutterwave for other African countries, a merchant of record for the rest of the world, and geo-default plus a manual switch with no redirects. Rail 3 is weaker than the findings present it, and five things should change.

1) Narrow Polar to what its policy clearly allows: recorded masterclasses sold as courses. Do NOT plan the community, live Academy cohorts, early-bird deposits/waitlists or paid webinar seats on Polar. Polar prohibits 'community access' and 'human services' and restricts 'Ticket sales', 'Pre-orders and paid waitlists' and 'eBooks'. The e-book can go to Lemon Squeezy (eBooks explicitly acceptable; pays out to Nigeria; no shutdown date announced) or to Polar after a written review.

2) Foreign buyers of the community, cohorts and mentorship default to Flutterwave international card (4.8% + 7.5% VAT on the fee) or Paystack international (3.9% + NGN 100). Sell fixed-term passes, not renewals, until a mandate-capable processor exists.

3) In the Flutterwave dashboard, check the fee-bearer setting. The default makes customers pay the fee on top of the price. Decide deliberately, and only then compare against Paystack's NGN 2,000 cap.

4) Before paying for Atlas, submit Razorpay's free foreign-business form for UPI, cards and netbanking from Indian buyers, settled abroad. Atlas is only worth its cost if Razorpay refuses. The cost case for Atlas is weaker than stated: a foreign-card subscription on Stripe direct with Billing (0.7%) and Tax (0.5%) costs about 6.6% + 30c, against Polar's 6.5% + 50c. Fees are a wash, so Atlas is justified only by UPI and coaching. Fixed costs are about USD 500 a year plus an accountant, and the USD 25,000 Form 5472 penalty can double if it runs past 90 days after IRS notice.

5) Use Polar's documented currencies. INR, KES, ZAR and NGN prices are possible (GHS is not), and checkouts created server-side must pass customer_ip_address or the currency will be wrong.

Also, before launch, confirm the Netlify plan. On the credit-based Free plan (300 credits, hard limit, 15 credits per production deploy), running out pauses every site, including checkout and webhook functions. Move to a paid credit plan with auto-recharge, or confirm the account is on a legacy plan.

**Missed:** 1) Polar's restricted list (eBooks, ticket sales, pre-orders and paid waitlists) and its right to refund at its own discretion for 60 days after purchase, keeping the original fee. 2) Flutterwave's default of passing fees to the customer. 3) Netlify's credit hard limit pausing all sites, including payment webhooks. 4) Polar's Nigeria payouts sit outside Stripe's documented self-serve cross-border regions (US, UK, EEA, CA, CH; everything else is 'Contact sales').
