## Script

One line per breath, the way he writes in Notion. Seated, straight to camera. Visual notes in braces.

**HOOK**
You don't choose what to learn in video editing. {On screen: "YOU DON'T CHOOSE WHAT TO LEARN"}
What you learn is chosen for you.
And if you don't get this, you can spend one whole year learning things you'll never even use.

**WHY ME**
When I started, I wanted to learn everything. {punch in}
Every animation I saw, I wanted to know how to do it.
Omo, it was overwhelming.

**THE MEAT**
Now don't get me wrong. {back out}
As a beginner, you need to learn two things.
The software, and the skill of editing.
And those two are not the same thing, by the way. But that's a topic for another day.

But the mistake a lot of beginners make is this.
They try to learn every skill, thinking every one of them will be needed.
Instead, start from the result you want to get. {On screen, one by one: CLIENTS · VIDEOS · PLATFORM · TOOLS}
What kind of clients do you want to edit for?
What kind of videos?
Which platform?
And what tools do you have?
Those things will decide the skills you need to learn.

**EXAMPLE**
So let's say your goal is to earn $500 per video. {punch in. On screen: "$500 PER VIDEO"}
You should know that a short-form video might not easily get you there.
That means long-form.
And you can't be doing long-form videos on CapCut mobile, right?
Then you ask, which niche can actually pay that money? {back out}
Maybe you find out it's business videos, or finance videos.
Now you know what to learn.
You need a tool like DaVinci Resolve, and skills like animated charts and screen recordings.

**TAKEAWAY**
So yes, learn the basics first. Cutting, pacing, sound design. {slower}
Then find out what it takes to get the result you want, and learn that.
Don't focus on the skill. Focus on the result. {On screen: "FOCUS ON THE RESULT"}
Because a lot of us spend time learning things we never use, and never need, in our journey.

**CTA** (see question 2)
