1. "Learning an editing software doesn't mean you can edit." *(used)*

## Script

**HOOK**
Learning an editing software doesn't mean you can edit. {On screen: "SOFTWARE ≠ EDITING"}
You can know every button in CapCut and still not know how to edit a video.

**WHY ME**
And trust me, I know software. {punch in}
There's nothing you can tell me about DaVinci Resolve that I don't know.
But that's not what my clients pay me for.

**THE MEAT**
You see, these are two different things. {back out}
First, the software. That's your tool. CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve. {On screen: TOOL}
And then the skill. That's the editing itself. Where to cut. How to pace it. What sound goes where. {On screen: SKILL}
And this is the mistake a lot of beginners make.
They learn the software, and they think that's editing.
They know how to add a transition, but they don't know why the transition is there.
Every cut, every animation you do needs a reason to be in the video. {punch in}
It either helps the viewer understand, or it helps the viewer feel something.
If it does neither, it doesn't belong there.

**EXAMPLE**
Let's say your client is talking about his YouTube views. {back out}
Someone who only knows the software throws in a fancy transition.
Someone who knows the skill shows the YouTube views. {On screen: a YouTube views counter}

**TAKEAWAY**
So yes, master your software first. That part you can't skip.
And try to master it in 30 days, not 60.
Then learn the skill, on full videos, start to finish. Not little nuggets.
The software is the tool. Editing is the skill. You need both. {On screen: "YOU NEED BOTH"}

**CTA**
Follow if you want to make money through video editing.
