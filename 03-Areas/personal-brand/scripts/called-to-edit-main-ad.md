# Called to Edit: main video / ad script (draft 1, 2026-10-07)

Video 3 on the [[03-Areas/personal-brand/webinar-video-list-2026-10|webinar video list]]: the main, heavily edited piece that also runs as the paid ad. Most people who see it **won't know him**, so it sells the webinar to a stranger, not to a follower. The intro/story video ([[03-Areas/personal-brand/intro-story-video-2026-10-05|intro video plan]]) carries the full backstory; this one uses it only as proof. Project: [[04-Projects/called-to-edit-webinar-and-academy|Called to Edit]]. Back to [[03-Areas/personal-brand/personal-brand|Personal brand]].

Written by the Brand manager at Samuel's ask (2026-10-07): *"the best script that hits well with the target audience, doesn't sound like AI, sounds like me, funny, interesting, engaging and would make people want to sign up"*, using what worked in proven ads.

**Status: draft.** Lines marked **(mine)** are the Brand manager's jokes, not his words. He keeps, rewrites or kills each one. Every fact is from his own words (sources at the end).

## What the proven ads taught, and where it sits in the script

| Proven ad / method | What it did | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| Meta's Reels data | Most people decide in the first 3 seconds; no "Hi, I'm…" openings; most Feed viewers have sound off, so captions carry it; a spoken + on-screen CTA lifts intent | Hook is the first line, no intro. Bold captions on every big line. CTA said and shown |
| Hormozi, *$100M Leads* | Hook calls out exactly who it's for; give a **reason why** for scarcity; a **damaging admission** builds trust; simple words | Callout in the pain beat. "100 seats, because that's all my Google Meet can hold." "Not because I'm the best editor in Nigeria." |
| Harmon Brothers (Squatty Potty, Purple, Poo-Pourri: USD 350M+ in sales) | Pain and a **sticky metaphor** first, product later; make the awkward truth funny; at least **two** CTAs | Jollof metaphor; the offer comes after 0:50; link said twice |
| Dollar Shave Club (founder-led, one take of deadpan Q&A) | Founder answers the objections himself, deadpan, fast | The two-Samuels objection round |
| Russell Brunson, *Perfect Webinar* | Break the false belief about the **vehicle** ("tutorials will get me paid"), then the internal one ("I'm not ready") and the external one ("my setup") | Reframe: editing is only half the job. Beginner / phone / MacBook objections. Aboki corner proof |

## The 30-second ad cut

Same shoot, no new recording. For paid placement, where a stranger gives it 30 seconds at most.

> "Stop watching video editing tutorials. You've watched enough. You can edit, but nobody's paying you, because nobody taught you the other half: getting clients, knowing what to charge, looking like a pro. I learned it the hard way. Two years ago I couldn't make ₦50,000 from editing. In the last 19 months I've made over $20,000, for international clients. On Friday, October 23rd, I'm teaching all of it, live. Called to Edit. ₦5,000. Only 100 seats. Tap the link and get yours."

## Hook test pack

Shoot all four in the same session, about 4 seconds each. Run the ad with each opening and keep the one that holds people longest. Each one flows straight into *"You can do the smooth zoom transition…"*

1. **Pattern break (default):** "Stop watching video editing tutorials. You've watched enough."
2. **Callout:** "If you can edit, but nobody has paid you yet, watch this."
3. **Before and after:** "I used to charge my laptop at the Aboki corner. Now I edit for a billion-dollar company." (If this one runs, drop the Aboki line from the proof beat.)
4. **His own proof hook:** "Two years ago, I couldn't make ₦50,000 from editing. In the last 19 months, I've made over $20,000." (Already the intro video's hook. Use for people who haven't seen that one.)

## How the edit treats it

- The skits (PART 47, the jollof, the Aboki corner, the two Samuels) are **literal on purpose**: they're the jokes. Everything else follows his B-roll rule: ordinary work-from-home life, matched to the feeling, never the words ([[03-Areas/personal-brand/brand-context|brand context]]).
- Bold captions on the hook, the "one naira" line, the money line and the CTA. Normal captions elsewhere. Brand under a quarter of the screen time; the title card shows once, the link twice.

## Questions before shooting

1. **19, 20 or 21 months?** The host bio (2026-10-07) says 19; his intro script said 20; his draft said 21. The script uses 19.
2. **Whop logo on screen**, or "a billion-dollar company" only? Named on the site and bio already.
3. **Every (mine) line:** keep, rewrite, or kill. Especially "Omo" and the data joke.
4. **100 seats:** if he upgrades Meet past 100, the reason-why line changes.
5. **CTA wording:** "Tap the link" fits the paid ad and a link sticker. For the organic post, "link in bio", or the intro video's "comment EDIT"?

## 2026-10-07: Samuel's draft 2 and the review

He rejected the "stop watching tutorials" hook. Samuel: *"I want hit on a desire, the desire to be a video editor that gets paid in dollars"*. His draft 2 keeps the structure and changes:

- **Hook, his words:** *"Is it possible to learn video editing as a beginner in 3 months and get clients that pay you Nothing less than $500 per video? Yes, cause I have done it"*
- **Pain:** *"A lot of video editors spend years learning video editing from youtube and yet they don't have clients that can pay them properly for their skills"*, then "Omo".
- **Picture:** guitar nobody can hear, marked by him as a placeholder.
- **Proof:** *"5 years ago I learned video editing as a soldier, practice all the video editing techniques but couldn't make it work"*, then the ₦50,000 / 500 videos / $20,000 in 19 months lines.
- **Offer:** *"All my years of experience. Live. It's titled Called to Edit."*
- **Doubts:** expensive computer, phone, *"I gat you"* for beginners, ₦5,000 vs data.
- **Close:** *"there are only 100seats, and people are signnup very fast so Comment Edit to reserve your seat"*.

**Review, the Brand manager:**
1. **The hook claims what the Brain says isn't true.** His main rate is USD 333.33 per video (Route Rise, [[03-Areas/video-editing/clients/alex|client note]]); the first international client paid USD 20; two years of learning came before ₦50,000. "3 months as a beginner" and "$500 per video" break non-negotiable 2, contradict his own proof beat 20 seconds later, and are the kind of income claim paid-ad review tends to reject. Keep the dollar desire; state a true number ("over $300 per video") unless he names a client paying $500+.
2. **The question hook is ~30 words (~10 s).** Cut to one breath, and show "$20,000+ in 19 months" on screen in the first second.
3. **Pain in the third person** ("a lot of video editors") lets the viewer off. Say "you".
4. **"People are signing up very fast"** isn't true (18 sign-ups, 3 paid on 2026-10-06). Use the real reason: the Meet holds 100.
5. **"Comment EDIT to reserve your seat"**: a comment doesn't reserve a seat; payment does. Organic: "comment EDIT and I'll send you the link", only if someone answers every comment. Paid ad: "tap Sign up".
6. "5 years ago" vs "4 years ago" (intro draft): one has to go.
7. Picture: generator with no fuel offered in place of the guitar.
