# The Life of a Video Editor, Ep 4: How I stay productive

**Stage:** words final and B-roll planned 2026-10-14; shoot next. Notion holds the live version. Was an outline 2026-09-23. (Samuel, 2026-09-24: moved up from Ep 6 — *"I don't feel like doing another video similar to the last, let's mix things up"*.)
**Series:** [[03-Areas/personal-brand/series/life-of-a-video-editor|The Life of a Video Editor]]. On screen at the start, in bold: *The Life of a Video Editor, Episode 4*. Target 1:30, up to 2:00.
**Bank:** #26, #28, #22 ([[03-Areas/personal-brand/personal-brand-ideas|ideas]]). Told as the struggle, not as tips. No guru tone.

## Hooks (he picks one)

1. "People ask me how I'm so productive. Truth is, I'm not."
2. "They give me five days for a job. I use three of them on my phone."
3. "I don't eat until 1pm. That's been my biggest productivity hack."

## Outline

**Context**
- He posts day-in-the-life videos of himself working. The comments keep asking how he's so productive.
- *"I am not the best productive person at all, and I'm not going to admit that I am."*

**Conflict**
- When he looks at the amount of work, he gets overwhelmed and feels down. Then he procrastinates, scrolls, isn't in the mood to edit.
- A recent week: he assumed a project wouldn't take long, and it took longer. The first three days were slow, YouTube and TikTok. The last two were 12-hour days, with his wrist and hand in pain.

**Pivot: what he does now**
- Phone on silent, out of the room, in the morning.
- The hardest work goes first: the mind is clear and there are no notifications yet.
- Editing only, about 6:30am to 1pm, nonstop.
- No food until 1pm. Coffee, maybe a snack. Eating makes him bloated and sluggish. *"That has been my major productivity hack."*

**Resolution**
- He still struggles. The overwhelm hasn't gone, and it's worst with a tight deadline. *"It's not easy. Video editing is not easy. It's stressful."*
- What he's trying now: one job at a time.
- The line: *"It has to be done."*

### Draft v3 (Brand manager, 2026-10-14)

Samuel rejected v1 as *"very very very bare"* and asked for a script *"just like me"*, engaging, *"with bit of comedy and great value"*, using everything the Brain knows. Written in the voice of his finished scripts (*I almost quit my Job*, *Davinci resolve*, *Doom Scrolling*). About 354 spoken words, roughly 2:00. Comedy lines are the Brand manager's, on his request.

Facts and where they come from: five days / three on the phone (bank #26, his hook); freeze instead of lock in (#28); "holding my neck" ([[02-Me/daily-routine|daily routine]], energy); 19 hours ending 1:42am and the 8-day job closed in about 8 hours once planned in timed chunks ([[02-Me/patterns|patterns]] P2, P5, legacy engine data he confirmed as patterns 2026-09-22); "when my morning is messed up" (patterns, his words); wrist (#22); phone out, hardest first, no food till 1pm, "it has to be done" (#43-#45); lofi, 1pm meal with social media, 2pm nap (daily routine).

Open before recording: 6:30 or 7:00 start (interview vs routine); the 19-hour and 8-day lines are engine records, his yes needed to say them on camera.

```
HOOK
People keep asking me how I'm so productive {scrolling the "how are you so productive" comments on my phone}
The truth? {phone drops onto the bed}
My client gives me five days to edit a video {wide: me walking into the room, PC on, timeline loaded}
and I spend three of them on my phone. {top-down: me on the bed scrolling, the PC glowing untouched behind me}

CONTEXT
I post videos of myself working {clip from an old day-in-the-life}
and the comments keep asking, "Samuel, how are you this productive?" {comments popping up one by one}
And I'm just there looking at my screen like... {slow push-in on a blank face, 2 seconds}
if only you knew.

STRUGGLE
See, when the work gets heavy, I'm supposed to lock in. {wide: me at the desk, a long timeline on screen}
I do the opposite. {spin the chair away from the desk}
Day one: "I have plenty time." {lying on the bed, scrolling, smiling}
Day two: "I still have plenty time." {same angle, now on the floor}
Day three: YouTube. TikTok. YouTube again. {same angle, upside down on the bed}
Day four: omo. {sit up fast, panic face, rush to the PC}
And the whole time I'm scrolling, that pending job is holding my neck. {slow-mo: hand on the back of my neck}
So the last two days, I edit twelve hours a day. {time-lapse on one shot, daylight to night}
One time, I worked nineteen hours and finished at almost 2am. {wall clock at 1:42}
My wrist was paining me. {close-up: rubbing my wrist}
Nobody warns you that this job comes for your body.

PIVOT
Then I noticed something. {sitting on the edge of the bed, quiet}
When my morning is messed up, my whole day is finished. {curtains opening, morning light}
So I fixed my mornings. {stand up, walk to the PC}
One. My phone goes on silent, and it leaves the room. {drop the phone in the drawer, close it}
Two. The hardest work goes first, while my mind is clear and nobody has messaged me yet. {hands on the keyboard, morning light on the desk}
Three. From 6:30 till 1pm, I only edit. {wall clock, then time-lapse of me working}
Lofi on, Resolve open, nothing else. {Resolve loading page}
Four, the weird one. {close-up}
I don't eat until 1pm. Just coffee. {pouring coffee}
Because the moment I eat breakfast, my body says, "Samuel, let's go and sleep." {lying on the couch, hand on belly}
So 1pm, food and social media. 2pm, nap. {eating with phone in hand, then asleep}
Don't judge me.
And five. I plan the job in hours, not days. {writing time blocks on paper}
One job dragged for eight days. {calendar pages flipping}
The day I finally planned it hour by hour, I finished the rest in eight hours. {closing the laptop, leaning back}
A job doesn't take days. It takes hours.

RESOLUTION
Am I fixed? No. {sitting on the floor, back against the wall}
The overwhelm is still there. Tight deadlines still get me.
Video editing is not easy. It's stressful. {close-up}
Now I take one job at a time. {closing every tab until only Resolve is left}
And when I don't feel like working, I tell myself one thing. {stand, put on headphones}
"Samuel, it has to be done." {same top-down angle as the hook, but now I'm at the desk, working}

CTA
Which rule are you stealing? Tell me in the comments. {point at the camera}
And follow along, I'm sharing more of my life as a video editor. {walk out of frame}
```

### Final words (Samuel's corrections to polished v2, 2026-10-14)

Samuel preferred v2 over v3 (*"I prefer the other last script before this one, let's perfect that one"*), then corrected the polished v2 line by line: TikTok to Instagram throughout, "Watching YouTube", "they gave me 5 days to deliver" (confirms five days), numbering replaced by *First / And then / And lastly*, *"My body starts to feel slow and sleepy"*, *"It makes me go insane"*, *"reduce distractions"*, CTA *"I'd love to know how you deal with procrastination in the comments"*. Put in the Notion page under Script the same day. About 300 words, roughly 1:45.

```
HOOK
People keep asking me how I'm so productive.
Truth is... I'm not.
But there's one weird thing I do that actually works, and it has nothing to do with editing.

CONTEXT
I post videos of me working, and the comments keep asking, "Samuel, how are you so productive?"
And I'm just looking at my phone like... if only you knew.
Because I am not the best productive person at all, and I'm not going to lie to you that I am.

STRUGGLE
See, when I look at how much work I have, I get overwhelmed. I feel down.
And what do I do when I feel down?
I open Instagram.
There is this project I had recently. They gave me 5 days to deliver.
I told myself, "This one no go take long."
Day one: watching YouTube.
Day two: Instagram.
Day three... YouTube and Instagram.
Day four: omo.
The last two days, I edited 12 hours a day.
By the end, my wrist and my hand were begging me to stop.

PIVOT
That week humbled me.
But I finally figured out what to do better.
First, in the morning, my phone goes on silent, and it leaves the room.
Not my table. Not my pocket. The room.
And then, I do the hardest work first, because my mind is clear and nobody has messaged me yet.
From 6:30am to 1pm, I edit. Only edit. Nonstop.
And lastly, the weird one I told you about.
I don't eat until 1pm. Just coffee, maybe a small snack.
Because the moment I eat a heavy breakfast, my body starts to feel slow and sleepy.
Honestly, that has been my major productivity hack.

RESOLUTION
But I won't lie to you, I still struggle.
The overwhelm hasn't gone. When the deadline is tight, it makes me go insane.
It's not easy. Video editing is not easy. It's stressful.
But what I am learning now... is to take one job at a time.
You don't need to feel productive. You just need to reduce distractions.
And on the days I don't feel like it, I tell myself:
"Samuel, it has to be done."

CTA
I'd love to know how you deal with procrastination in the comments.
```

Read as he wrote it: "But what I am learning now...." taken as the lead-in to "is to take one job at a time" (asked him). 6:30am kept; he didn't change it.
