# Can I build a career in video editing with just my phone? Talking head (draft 1, 2026-10-14)

A talking head from Samuel's Notion card of 2026-10-13. Every point below comes from his voice dump of 2026-10-14. His line from the card, *"I didn't start editing from my phone but I have mentored people who started editing from their phone"*, keeps the hook honest: the proof is what he has seen in others, not his own start. It ends with the Called to Edit CTA because he tied the video to the webinar himself (*"there are many things I'm going to be sharing concerning building a career in video editing, whether you are using your phone or not, in my upcoming webinar"*). Story bank: none used. Back to [[03-Areas/personal-brand/personal-brand|Personal brand]].

**Status: draft 1.** The Brand manager tightened the wording for Samuel to say in his own way. About 410 spoken words, roughly 2:30 at a normal talking pace. Trimming lines are under Notes.

## Hooks (he picks one)

1. "Can you build a career in video editing with just your phone? Yes, you can. But there's a right way to do it." *(recommended: it answers the title, so the people searching this question stay)*
2. "Your phone is not what's stopping you. The way you present yourself is."
3. "If you edit with your phone, stop telling clients you edit with your phone."

## Script

**Hook** {close-up, straight to camera}
"Can you build a career in video editing with just your phone?
Yes, you can. But there's a right way to do it."

**Context** {medium shot}
"I've mentored people who started editing on their phones. And I've seen a lot of phone editors succeed.
Not because of the phone. Because of how they presented themselves."

**Conflict** {B-roll: you at your desk, scrolling slowly, unimpressed. Not a phone being edited on}
"Here's where most phone editors get it wrong.
They see someone like me doing crazy motion graphics and animations on my clients' videos, and they try to compete with that. On a phone.
You are competing on the wrong ground.

Then they pitch to a client: 'I'm a mobile video editor.'
That will entirely spoil your game. Once a client hears that, they might feel you're not good enough."

**Pivot** {back to close-up, slower}
"But the gadget is not the limitation. The way you package and present yourself is.
Clients don't really care what you used to edit. They care about the result."

**Resolution: three things** {medium shot; the number and a short title on screen for each point}
"**One: position yourself based on what your phone can carry.**
The phone editors I see winning do event recaps. A church event. A gym session. A founder's speech cut into short clips for social media.
None of that needs crazy animations.

**Two: tell the story with the clips themselves.**
The way you cut. The sound design. Slowing things down to let the emotion breathe.
Add animations and typography on a phone, and it shows.
Tell the story with just the clips, and your phone is enough.

**Three: pitch the result, not the gadget.**
Don't say 'I'm a mobile editor.' Say: 'I can turn your event into a short clip people will remember.'
They don't need to know your phone did it. They just need to see the work, and feel the emotion in it."

**The honest part** {B-roll: an ordinary moment at home, calm. Cut back to camera on "you have to start from somewhere"}
"Me? I started on a laptop, because I was lucky. I had a salary, and I spent all of it on my first laptop.
And to be honest, the bigger money in video editing comes from a laptop.
But you have to start from somewhere.
A lot of the editors I know started on their phones, and now they've bought their first laptop.

So get good at the little thing your phone can do. Become the best at it. Save from it. Then buy your laptop.

Cut your coat according to your size."

**CTA** {straight to camera}
"I'm sharing a lot more on building a career in video editing, phone or laptop, at my webinar.
Called to Edit. Friday, October 23rd, 7pm. Comment EDIT, or check the link in my bio."

## Open questions for Samuel

1. **Talking heads.** He said phone editors succeed with *"simple talking head videos"*, then *"I will even tell you to avoid talking head videos"* because animations and typography on a phone show. Does he mean avoid talking heads altogether, or only the ones that need animated text? The draft leaves the line out. Point 2 covers the second reading.
2. **One real mentee.** The script has no number in it. One true example would make it land: how many of his mentees started on a phone, what kind of job the first one got paid for, how much, and how long until the laptop. Without one, it stays "I know a lot of editors".
