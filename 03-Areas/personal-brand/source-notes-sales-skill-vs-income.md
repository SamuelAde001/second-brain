# Source notes — "a skill isn't income until you can sell it"

Back to [[03-Areas/personal-brand/personal-brand|Personal brand]].

Samuel, 2026-10-11, wants this for a video he'll share soon: *skills are good but if you don't know how to sell it to someone and get someone to pay you, it's nothing.*

**Source:** Tom Scryleus, *I Asked 7 Top AI Models How to Escape the 9–5...*, YouTube, https://youtu.be/w-Mo1Ldg-qs. The sales part runs **~8:45–14:00**, with the ownership tail to ~16:20. Notes below are paraphrased from the auto-captions. The money figures are his, in USD, from a Western 9–5 frame. They're his claims, not checked facts.

## The core idea

- He asked seven AIs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity, Copilot, DeepSeek) how an ordinary person escapes the 9–5. Six gave essentially one answer: **learn a valuable skill, sell it to businesses as a service, then turn the service into a business.** DeepSeek leaned to classic saving and investing but still recommended freelancing.
- The principle under it all: **learn something that solves an expensive problem, then learn how to sell it.** The hot skill changes (today it's AI automation); the principle doesn't.
- **"Learn sales"** came up near the top for almost every model. He calls it the least sexy advice in the experiment, and admits it's the one he has a problem with himself.

## The line Samuel's video is built on

- People dreaming of escape think about *what they can make*: the business, the product, the course. None of it matters if nobody buys it.
- His own proof: 10+ years on YouTube, built a course on making YouTube videos, and **never managed to sell it to his own viewers.** Not because the course was bad: he doesn't know how to sell.
- Selling himself always felt uncomfortable, even dishonest, like in job interviews where you play a role to get hired. What he never learned was how to sell something he actually believes in.
- **He names video editors directly:** you can be the best video editor in the world, or understand AI better than almost anyone, but if you can't convince another person your work is worth paying for, you don't have a business. **You have a skill, and that's different.**
- **Sales turns the skill into income; income gives you something to invest.**

## Why sell to businesses, not individuals

- Consumer product at USD 20 (a book, clothes): you need about 250 sales a month.
- Solve a business problem for USD 1,000/month: you need about 5 clients.
- A business pays when the thing **makes it money, saves it money, or saves enough time to justify the cost.** You don't need a revolutionary idea, just a problem someone already has.
- Skills the AIs listed: email marketing, AI workflow automation, bookkeeping, paid ads, CRM setup, sales, copywriting, SEO, data analytics, web development, consulting.

## The roadmap (selling inside it)

- **Year 1:** keep the job, control expenses, emergency fund. Pick **one** skill, not five. 10–15 hours a week on it. **Don't spend a year preparing:** look for the first paying customer within the first months. Target ~USD 500/month side income by year end. Not enough to quit; enough to prove it works.
- **Year 2:** get better, more customers, **raise prices.** Stop selling hours: not "I charge USD 50 an hour" but "I'll solve this specific problem for USD 1,500." Same problem, same process, again and again.
- **Speed is yours when you sell outcomes.** In a job, getting faster earns you more work, not more money. Working for yourself, you own the efficiency: 10 hours down to 2, you keep the difference. His example is editing: about 20 hours per video once, under an hour now.
- **Years 3–5:** stop thinking like a freelancer. Document, automate, use AI or hire, build systems, maybe productise (software, subscription, digital product). Otherwise you've escaped a 9–5 to build another one, with every customer as your boss. The goal is **ownership**, not self-employment.

## What the video does *not* give

It argues **why** sales matters. It gives no **how**: no outreach method, no pitch, no objection handling, no pricing conversation. He admits he never learned it. If Samuel's video needs to teach selling, that part has to come from his own experience (how he lands Route Rise and other clients), not from this source.

- Turn Samuel's own story into proof: the first time someone paid him for an edit, and what he said or did that made them pay.
- The editor-specific line (best editor in the world, still no business) is the strongest single beat to borrow. Credit the source if it's quoted near-verbatim.
