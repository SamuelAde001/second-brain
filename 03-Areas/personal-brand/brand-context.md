## Who he is

He creates content to inspire people to create content. The brand covers content creation, video editing, the life of a self-employed entrepreneur, and his personal story — **from soldier in the Nigerian military to self-employed video editor, content creator, content coach**, and founder of the HighSignals community and the Scripnals app.

The goal is to inspire — **not as a guru**, but as someone relatable: raw, honest, open, valuable.

## Why he creates, in his own priority order

1. **Inspire people to create** — always comes first.
2. Let that inspiration lead people into the course, community, mentorship or Scripnals **naturally**.
3. Build his personal reputation — for opportunities, invitations, and becoming a recognised coach and leader.
4. **Let money follow value, not the other way around.** He works full-time as a video editor: it solves income, but it is his biggest time bottleneck.

That fourth point is the tension the whole system exists to manage.

## How he shows up

He is always the face of the content. Signature format: **cinematic, high-production storytelling — shot in his room, multiple camera angles, fully narrated, always on the DJI Osmo Pocket 4.** This is core to the visual identity.

## Content pillars

1. **Visibility & The Call to Create** — why putting yourself out there matters, and why Christians specifically should create more. Series: *Called to Create*.
2. **The Craft** — the actual skills behind good content: editing, shooting, camera angles, scripting. Working series: *Called to Edit*.
3. **Storytelling** — his signature format: cinematic, narrated, emotionally engaging personal storytelling.
4. **The Journey** — his personal story, the soldier-to-creator arc, struggles and wins.
5. **Build With Me** — Scripnals and HighSignals, framed as part of his story, not separate promo.

Series names for pillars 3, 4 and 5 are **not decided yet**.

## Audience

Ages **16–40**, mostly 20s–30s, both genders. Mostly Nigerians at home right now (the most relatable base), building toward a broad international audience of aspiring creators. They are aspiring creators, freelancers and entrepreneurs.

**What they are feeling** — this list is hook and idea fuel, kept whole:

Seeing others create and wanting to start · not knowing why they aren't getting opportunities · not understanding why visibility matters · scared to be visible · wanting to start but not knowing where · wanting to start but lacking tools · struggling with consistency · lacking ideas · content not converting · needing inspiration · wanting to quit their job · wanting to become a video editor · wanting to become influential · comparison · social media pressure · wanting new income · needing someone relatable, not a guru · wanting to feel seen in their struggles.

## Tone and voice

Storytelling over shouting — his journey, struggles, failures, wins. **Never a guru.** Funny yet inspirational. Emotional storytelling that actually touches people. Motivational. Always positive.

## Language

Mostly proper English, simple enough for a 15-year-old to follow — valuable but never overly complex. **Pidgin is a light sprinkle**: one or two phrases, mostly during narration, for comedic or emotional warmth. It is seasoning, not the main voice. No topics are off-limits beyond ordinary common sense.

## The storytelling formula

**Hook → Context → Conflict → Pivot → Resolution → Mantra**

- **Hook** — bold, high-stakes, slightly provocative. Never misleading. His own examples: *"God doesn't like your content ideas."* · *"Don't make Christian content."* · *"The worst mistake you can make is picking a niche."*
- **Context** — the situation or struggle, often with specific visual direction written into the script.
- **Conflict** — the turning point, where things come to a head.
- **Pivot** — the shift. Often the moment he goes to prayer, or realises what he was missing.
- **Resolution** — the lesson, stated plainly if the story hasn't already made it obvious.
- **Mantra** — series branding at the end, e.g. *"This is CALLED TO CREATE, Episode 5"*.

**Why it works, in his words:** higher stakes = bigger reward = people stay till the end. He raises the stakes hard in the hook without lying, then the story earns it in the resolution.

## What hits and what flops

- **Hits:** shocking, engaging, relatable, interesting, real value, real structure.
- **Flops:** a weak hook with no clear stake, pain or dream up front — or no clear benefit to the viewer. "These two kill a video faster than anything else."

## Length and pacing

60 seconds for tighter, punchier videos. 90+ seconds for calmer, narrative talking-head storytelling.

## Captions

Used heavily throughout. **Bold** for hooks and big statements, minimal/normal for regular narration.

## What success looks like

1. **Primary:** "wow" people through emotional storytelling so they check his profile and follow.
2. **Secondary:** provoke, inspire or make them laugh enough to comment.
3. App and community conversion happens more naturally through Stories and dedicated promo videos — **not** the main content.

## CTA

No fixed line yet. He wants one built around **visibility**.

## Inspiration

No specific creator. Drawn to strong storytelling and content craft in general.

## What he avoids

Guru energy. Anyone acting like they know it all or seem perfect. Any content that lies to or fools people.
