# SOP — script creation, from raw idea to finished script

**Trigger:** Samuel brings a raw idea for a Storytelling-pillar video, or any personal story.
**Owner:** the content agent (Phase 4).
**Source:** `Samuel's script creation process.md`, Content Creation project, 2026-07. Derived from the actual process behind his "100 Days" visibility script, turned into a repeatable method.

This is the long-form process. [[script-review-checklist]] is the short version for reviewing an existing draft.

---

## Part 1 — The interview, before writing anything

### Step 1: anchor the story (quick, easy questions)

Two or three short questions to find direction before going deep:

- What is the real moment this is built on? — offer a few likely angles rooted in his pillars and past struggles, plus "something else, let me explain".
- Where should this sit? — standalone / part of a named series / not sure yet.
- What tone or humour is he picturing? — self-deprecating / dramatised / ironic / "I'll figure it out as we talk".

**Never guess the story. Never invent details to fill gaps — ask.**

### Step 2: dig for the real details (open questions)

1. What was the actual situation? What did he really do, not do, or experience?
2. What was the specific turning point or realisation — something he saw, heard or experienced that flipped it?
3. What did it actually cost him, or what did he gain? **Push for concrete numbers, timeframes, feelings** — not vague statements.
4. When did this happen, and is there embarrassment or vulnerability attached? *This is usually where the best authentic lines come from.*
5. Are there phrases he already says to himself or his friends about this? **Capture his real language verbatim — these become the script's best lines.**

---

## Part 2 — Hook development

- Draft 2–3 hook options rooted in his actual answers, not generic marketing hooks.
- Every hook states a real stake, pain or curiosity gap in the first 1–2 lines. Bold, never dishonest or exaggerated.
- **If he says an option "isn't how I talk" — that is a correction to act on immediately, not a style preference to weigh.** Rebuild toward his literal cadence, not a softer version of the assistant's version.
- **Lock the hook before writing the rest of the script.** Don't build on a shaky hook.

---

## Part 3 — Writing the full script

- Use his real answers as the actual content — not raw material to be replaced with new phrasing.
- Preserve distinctive personal patterns: talking to himself by name, filler words like "broooo", repetition for rhythm ("kept saying… kept saying…"), his natural Nigerian/Pidgin cadence.
- **Do not add assistant-authored punchlines, jokes or one-liners** unless Samuel explicitly asks for that pass. His own material is the humour.
- Only fix objective errors — typos, grammar slips. Never rewrite phrasing into "proper" or corporate-sounding English.
- If a line is ambiguous, or could be a typo with more than one possible meaning, **ask**. Never guess and silently "fix" it into something he didn't say.
- Structure: **Hook → Context → Conflict (Struggle) → Pivot (Discovery) → Resolution (Value/Lesson) → Mantra.** Mantra only if it belongs to a named series; drop it for standalone pieces.

---

## Part 4 — Review checklist, run on any draft, his or the assistant's

- [ ] Hook states a real stake, pain or curiosity gap in the first line or two
- [ ] Hook is bold but never dishonest or exaggerated
- [ ] Follows Hook → Context → Conflict → Pivot → Resolution → (Mantra if series)
- [ ] Obvious benefit or takeaway for the viewer
- [ ] Lesson is clear — shown through the story, or stated plainly if it isn't
- [ ] Concrete details present (numbers, dates, names) instead of vague statements
- [ ] No corporate or generic phrasing has crept in — it still sounds like Samuel talking, not a script
- [ ] Typos and grammar fixed **without** changing voice
- [ ] Any ambiguous line flagged and confirmed with Samuel, not silently altered
- [ ] Length roughly fits: 60s tight / 90s+ calmer narrative
- [ ] Any product mention (Scripnals / Academy / community) woven naturally, not hard-sold — unless it is a dedicated promo video
- [ ] Feedback is honest — real weak points named, not just praise

---

## Part 5 — Standing rules for whoever is helping

1. Raw idea with no script → **ask before writing anything**. Never invent his story.
2. His own draft → sharpen and lightly correct only. Preserve his wording, rhythm and structure.
3. Never insert original punchlines or jokes unless explicitly requested — his own phrasing is the comedic engine.
4. Flag ambiguous phrases instead of guessing their meaning.
5. Give honest, specific feedback. Name real weaknesses; don't just validate.
6. Match hook options to his literal speaking cadence, especially after a correction like "that's not how I talk".
7. Keep the six-stage formula — or his own labels (Event hook / Context / Struggle / Discovery-Pivot / Value-Lesson) — as the working structure, dropping Mantra for standalone pieces.

## Done when

Samuel has a locked hook and a full script in his own voice, with visual direction in braces where it belongs, and he has not had to correct the same thing twice.
