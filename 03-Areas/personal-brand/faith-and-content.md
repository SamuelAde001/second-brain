# Faith and content — creating content the Biblical way

## What this is

From the "Creating content the Biblical way" chat (2026-07-27). Faith is an explicit part of Samuel's public brand, not a side note: the Visibility & The Call to Create pillar ([[brand-context]]) is partly about why Christians specifically should create more, and two of his standing hook examples are *"God doesn't like your content ideas"* and *"Don't make Christian content"* (already recorded in [[brand-context]]). This chat is where he actually worked one faith-and-content piece all the way from a raw list of points to a locked script — the thinking and the corrections below are his.

## The brief, in his own words

His opening ask, verbatim:

> "I want to do a video about, how to create content the Biblical way... This video would be more of a listicle but I want each point to hit hard so they take notes... I am not trying to be a guru, but teaching this makes me more authentic and relatable."

His original nine points, in the order he first listed them, each meant to carry a biblical reference and land as a memorable "punchline sentence":

- Pray first before you start ideating any video
- Get inspiration from other people's content
- Build your mind with scriptures so you don't speak about things that don't align with God
- Be authentic and real, don't create content that deceives
- Be a leader and take responsibility of what you share to your viewers
- Put in all your best hardwork in the scripting, filming and editing
- Be excellent
- Don't worry about the metrics and the results
- Be consistent

## How it evolved

- **Reordering logic:** prep (prayer, scripture) → creative process (inspiration) → character (authenticity, leadership) → execution (hard work, excellence) → aftermath (metrics, consistency).
- Two additions — stewardship (the parable of the talents) and a comparison point (Galatians 6:4) — were floated. Only stewardship survived, as the closer. **The comparison point was proposed in chat and never adopted** — Samuel cut it himself when trimming the list.
- **Samuel's own correction** (2026-07-27T09:40:55): the phrasing wasn't landing as an "executable action." He asked for every point rewritten as a direct action, the explanation after it, and the King James Version text in full — because he'd already decided the complete verse would display on screen during the edit, so paraphrasing it didn't matter.
- That produced 11 points. He then said "cut it down to the strongest" — the result was 7 points plus a closer. What got cut, and why: "Get inspiration from other people's content" had the weakest scriptural anchor and overlapped with "feed your mind scripture"; "hard work" and "excellence" were merged into one point since they were doing the same job.

## The hook — a real correction

Samuel rejected the first three hook options outright: *"I don't like the hooks you suggested, I want the hook to be easy and straight to the point."* This is a deliberate departure from his usual bold/provocative hook style (the "God doesn't like your content ideas" style) — **for this teaching/listicle format specifically, he wanted plain and direct instead of provocative.**

He tested his own line — *"Here are 7 steps to create content the biblical way"* — and asked point-blank whether it was a bad hook. The honest answer: by his own checklist it isn't really a hook at all, since it states no stake or pain — it's what his own storytelling-structures doc files under **Introduce**, not **Hook**. He kept the plain, declarative instinct anyway; what he locked in the final script below is exactly that register: **"Here is how I create content the Biblical way."**

## The "I" vs "you" decision — his own call

Samuel decided, unprompted, to narrate the video from his own perspective ("I pray before I start...") instead of giving commands ("You should pray..."), and asked directly for a reaction: *"I decided to go with narrating it from my perspective that telling them what to do, what do you think about that?"* His own reasoning, implicit in the question, is that testimony reads as authentic and invitational where commands read as guru — consistent with "never a guru" in [[brand-context]]. The script's closing line converts the testimony into an implicit directive ("...and surely, you should too") rather than switching voice mid-script.

## The locked script

Kept verbatim below. Samuel wrote the first full draft of this himself and asked for a structural/flow/redundancy pass; what follows is that draft with his own wording kept intact except for the specific fixes he asked to be checked against — consistent numbering (First → Lastly, not a mix of "Secondly" and "Next"), a "you can be careless with it" → "you can't afford to be careless with it" typo that flipped his meaning, merging a repeated line in point 2, trimming a near-synonym pair in point 5, and dropping a redundant "actually" at the close. Scripture is quoted King James Version, in full — he chose this deliberately since the full verse displays on screen during the edit, so paraphrasing wasn't necessary.

---

**HOOK**
Here is how I create content the Biblical way.

**First,** I pray before I start any video.
The truth is, without God, nothing we build actually stands.
*As the bible said — "Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it."*

**Second,** I feed my mind with scripture daily.
You can't give people genuinely good value from a rotten mind — what's inside you always shows up in what you create.
*Just like the bible said — "A good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth that which is good."*

**Third,** I make sure to be authentic and truthful.
Nothing is worse than leading people with lies and exaggeration just to get views.
*Even the bible said we should put away lying and speak truth to our neighbors.*

**Fourth,** I take full responsibility for what I share with my audience.
A lot of people take what they hear online seriously — that's a huge responsibility, and you can't afford to be careless with it.
*As the bible said — "Be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation."*

**Fifth,** I try to be excellent in my content.
A lot of us settle for mediocrity and half-hearted content, but we need to treat it as an act of worship to God, not just a random task.
*Even scripture said — "Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men."*

**Sixth,** I try not to worry about the metrics.
I've learned worry won't get you anywhere — your success was never fully in your hands.
*The bible said it's not about who plants or waters, but God who gives the increase.*

**Lastly,** I stay consistent even when I'm not seeing results — because the harvest always comes after the grind, not during it.
*Just like the bible says, don't be weary of doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we faint not.*

I am not perfect in this, but I seek to do content the right way — the God way.
And surely, you should too.
