# Module 04 — Color

Source's own module description: *"Three lessons. Color is where personal content most obviously separates from professional-looking content, and it's mostly four sliders used in the right order."*

## 20. Color Correction Fundamentals
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Correction is fixing what the camera got wrong. Grading is choosing how it should feel. Never do the second before the first

**2. Principle**
- **Correction vs grading** — and why every editor in every software does them in that order
- **Exposure:** recovering a dark clip, rescuing a blown-out one, and what's genuinely unrecoverable
- **White balance:** the orange or blue cast every phone clip has, and why fixing it changes everything downstream
- **Contrast:** where beginners overdo it, and what crushed blacks cost you
- Highlights, shadows, blacks and whites — what each one actually touches
- **Saturation vs vibrance** — why vibrance protects skin and saturation doesn't
- The working order: exposure → white balance → contrast → saturation. Always
- Judging your work: the phone-screen test, and why your monitor is lying to you

**3. Demo**
- Correct a dark clip and a blown-out clip start to finish, narrating each slider decision
- Fix an orange indoor cast on camera
- Push contrast and saturation deliberately too far so they can see the failure point
- Show a corrected clip next to the original

**4. The Trap**
- Grading before correcting — building a look on top of a broken image
- Judging color on one screen only

**5. Drill**
- Correct every clip in your current edit. No looks, no filters
- Play it back and notice how much more finished it already feels

## 21. Color Grading for a Look
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- A look is an emotional decision, not a filter you found

**2. Principle**
- What a look communicates: warm vs cool, lifted vs contrasty, saturated vs muted
- Matching the grade to the content — a warm nostalgic grade on an urgent piece fights the message
- Building a look with the tools you already used in Lesson 20, plus curves and color wheels
- **Filter strength:** why 100% always looks wrong, and where a filter should usually sit
- Using filters as a starting point rather than an answer
- **Skin tone as the guardrail** — whatever the grade, skin has to stay believable
- Adjustment layers: grading a whole sequence at once instead of clip by clip **[CHECK: adjustment layer workflow on CapCut PC]**
- Not chasing trends — a grade that dates is a video that dates

**3. Demo**
- Build a warm look and a cool look on the same clip, discuss what each says
- Apply a filter at 100%, then dial it to where it should be
- Grade with skin tone as the reference and show what happens when you ignore it
- Apply a grade across a whole sequence with one adjustment layer

**4. The Trap**
- Heavy filters that destroy skin tone
- A different look every video, so nothing accumulates into a style

**5. Drill**
- Build one look for your edit and write down what it's supposed to make the viewer feel
- Check skin tone before you commit

## 22. Consistency Across Clips and Videos
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Inconsistent color is the thing viewers can't name but always feel

**2. Principle**
- Why mismatched clips break immersion even when each one looks fine alone
- Matching across cameras, lighting conditions and time of day within one video
- Matching stock or downloaded B-roll to your own footage — usually the biggest offender
- The matching order: exposure first, then white balance, then contrast, then saturation. Same order, always
- Using a reference frame and checking A/B rather than grading from memory
- Saving and reusing a look so video 40 matches video 1 **[CHECK: preset save / copy-attributes workflow]**
- Building a color identity — what it does for recognition when someone scrolls past your content

**3. Demo**
- Take three mismatched clips and match them live, in order
- Match a stock clip to your own footage
- Save a look as a preset and apply it to a new project
- Show a sequence before and after matching

**4. The Trap**
- Grading each clip in isolation so nothing agrees with anything
- Saving a preset built from an uncorrected clip — it'll be wrong on everything else

**5. Drill**
- Match every clip in your edit to one reference frame
- Save your look as a preset and name it
