## 15. Effects Mindset
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- You're about to learn a lot of tools. The lesson that matters is which ones not to use

**2. Principle**
- The Module 00 filter applied to visuals: does this effect support the message or compete with it?
- Why beginner edits look like beginner edits — effects used as filler for a weak cut
- **The subtraction test:** remove it. Is the video actually worse?
- Effects that serve: direct attention, clarify information, control energy, create continuity
- Effects that don't: anything that exists to prove you know the software
- Trend effects and shelf life — the cost of dating your content
- Building a visual language: three or four things you do consistently beats twenty things you do once
- The ratio to aim for: learn every tool in this module, use about a tenth of them in any one video

**3. Demo**
- Take one 15-second clip and load it with effects — genuinely overdo it
- Strip back to two, then to one, then to none
- Land on the version that's actually best and say why

**4. The Trap**
- Adding an effect because the section feels boring. The section is boring because of the cut, and no effect will fix that
- Treating this module as a checklist to use rather than a menu to choose from

**5. Drill**
- Open your most recent edit and remove one effect
- Decide honestly whether the video got worse

## 16. Speed Ramping for Emphasis
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Speed is an emotional tool. It compresses boredom and stretches impact

**2. Principle**
- What speed changes do to feeling: acceleration builds tension, slow-motion grants importance
- Constant speed change vs a **ramp** — a ramp has an acceleration curve, and that's what makes it feel designed
- Where a ramp belongs: on one moment, not spread across the video
- The frame rate relationship: why slowing footage that wasn't shot fast looks choppy, and what to do about it
- Speed and audio: what happens to voice, and when to detach it
- Combining a speed change with a cut for a punch that neither achieves alone
- Speed as pacing control — the fix for a section that drags

**3. Demo**
- Apply a flat speed change, then build a proper curve ramp on the same clip **[CHECK: curve speed panel on CapCut PC]**
- Show the same moment at 0.5x, 1x and 2x back to back
- Detach audio and keep voice usable through a visual ramp
- Slow footage that wasn't shot for it, show the stutter, show the fix

**4. The Trap**
- Ramping everything. If every moment is emphasized, nothing is
- Slow motion on a moment that isn't worth the extra seconds

**5. Drill**
- Find the single most important moment in your edit and ramp into it
- Nothing else in the video gets a speed change

## 17. Basic Motion Graphics
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Motion graphics aren't decoration — they're information you can't get across any other way

**2. Principle**
- What graphics actually add: labeling, direction, emphasis, structure, data
- Shapes, arrows, icons, lines, counters — the small vocabulary that does 90% of the work
- Building elements rather than importing them, so they match your brand
- Animating with the keyframes from Lesson 11 — same skill, new application
- **Anchoring:** graphics attached to the subject vs pinned to the frame, and what each communicates
- Timing graphics to audio so they feel caused rather than added
- Consistency: a graphic style that repeats across videos becomes recognizable as yours
- Restraint: one or two graphic moments per short

**3. Demo**
- Build a simple arrow-and-label callout from shapes, animate it in and out
- Build a lower third from scratch using your type system
- Anchor a graphic to a moving subject
- Time a graphic pop to a sound effect

**4. The Trap**
- Graphics that say what the caption already says
- Motion graphics as proof of effort — the viewer doesn't grade you on effort

**5. Drill**
- Build one callout graphic for a moment in your edit that genuinely needs clarifying
- Reuse your type system fonts and colors

## 18. Masking, Blend Modes and Overlays
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- A mask is just "show part of this layer." Once that clicks, half of what looks like advanced editing becomes obvious

**2. Principle**
- What a mask actually does: reveals or hides a region of a layer
- Mask shapes, inversion, and **feathering** — why a hard mask edge always looks fake
- Blend modes and what they're solving: screen for light, multiply for shadow, the handful worth knowing
- Overlays — light leaks, texture, grain, dust — as atmosphere, not as content
- **Tracking:** attaching a mask to something that moves
- Composite thinking: any complex effect is layers plus masks plus timing
- Application example — the clone effect: one locked-off shot, two takes, one mask

**3. Demo**
- Build a basic shape mask, feather it, invert it
- Blend an overlay using screen mode, adjust opacity, show it at 100% so they see why that's wrong
- Build a mask reveal transition
- Build the clone effect start to finish
- Track a mask to a moving subject **[CHECK: tracking behavior in current CapCut PC build]**

**4. The Trap**
- Hard-edged masks that read as a cutout sticker
- Overlays at full strength — grain and light leaks should be felt, not seen

**5. Drill**
- Build one mask reveal in your current project
- Feather it until you can't tell where the mask edge is

## 19. Removing Backgrounds and Compositing
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Two different jobs, two different tools, and knowing which one the shot needs saves you an hour

**2. Principle**
- **Chroma key** (a green screen you shot) vs **auto cutout** (software finding the subject) — when each is the right call
- What makes a key clean: even lighting, separation from the background, no motion blur on the edges
- Where auto removal falls apart: hair, fast motion, low contrast, busy backgrounds
- Edge cleanup and why the edge is the only thing a viewer ever notices
- Matching the subject to the new background — scale, angle, color, and light direction
- Real uses in personal content: yourself over a graphic, reaction overlays, screenshots and screen recordings, product cutouts
- When compositing is genuinely worth the effort and when a plain cut says the same thing faster

**3. Demo**
- Key a green screen clip, clean the edges, place it over a background
- Auto-remove a background from a non-green clip, show exactly where it breaks
- Composite yourself over a graphic and match the color so it doesn't look pasted on
- Build a screenshot-over-shoulder shot

**4. The Trap**
- A key with a green fringe — one of the fastest ways to look amateur
- Compositing something that would have been clearer as a simple cutaway

**5. Drill**
- Composite one element into your edit
- Check the edges at 100% zoom before you move on

## 🔴 LIVE EDIT 03 — B-Roll Story Piece
**Shot type:** Live Edit

**Format:** new footage, no talking head. Cut and visuals only — no color or audio polish yet.

**What to hit:**
- Sequence from scratch on camera: build an order, then break it and rebuild
- Let music drive the structure and show yourself adjusting cut points to it
- Apply speed and motion using the restraint from Lesson 15 — say out loud what you're choosing not to do
- Build exactly one masked or composited moment, and justify it
- Play the result with no color grade and no sound design so the next module has something to add
