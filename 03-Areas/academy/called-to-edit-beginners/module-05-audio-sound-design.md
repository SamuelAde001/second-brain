# Module 05 — Audio & Sound Design

Source's own module description: *"Five lessons and a live edit. Sound is half the video and the half almost nobody edits."*

## 23. Sound Design Principles
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- Bad audio kills a good video. Good audio saves a mediocre one. Nobody's ratio is the other way round

**2. Principle**
- What sound communicates that image can't: emotion, scale, tension, time, presence
- **The three layers:** voice, music, effects — and the job each one does
- Music as emotional direction, not background filler — the track is a decision about how the viewer should feel
- Choosing music that matches pace *and* message, not just genre taste
- How music structure can carry edit structure — drops, builds, and cutting to them
- **Silence as a tool** — the moment before the payoff is often the most powerful sound in the edit
- What "just adding music" gets wrong: it fills space instead of shaping it
- Sound continuity: why sudden audio changes feel like errors even when the visuals cut cleanly

**3. Demo**
- Play a finished cut silent, with the wrong music, and with the right music
- Show one moment with sound removed entirely and how much weight silence adds

**4. The Trap**
- Picking a track because you like it rather than because it fits
- Music at the same level throughout, ignoring the arc

**5. Drill**
- Take your current edit and try three different tracks
- Write one line on what each one does to the feeling

## 24. Audio Hierarchy and Mixing
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Voice on top. Always. Everything else in the mix is negotiating for the space that's left

**2. Principle**
- The hierarchy: voice, then music, then effects — and what happens when it's violated
- Rough level targets you can actually work to, and why you mix by ear against the voice
- **Ducking:** pulling music down under speech, manually or automatically
- Fades: why every audio cut needs one, even a tiny one
- Audio transitions across cuts — keeping a sound bed running so the edit doesn't feel choppy
- Panning and stereo width, briefly — and why short-form stays mostly centered
- **Checking the mix where it'll actually be heard:** phone speaker, then earbuds. Never trust studio monitors alone

**3. Demo**
- Build a mix from scratch: place voice, add music, set the balance
- Duck music under speech manually with volume keyframes, then show any automatic option
- Show a hard audio cut and then the same cut with a 5-frame fade
- Play the mix on a phone speaker on camera

**4. The Trap**
- Music so loud that voice becomes work to follow
- Mixing on headphones only and shipping something unlistenable on a phone

**5. Drill**
- Mix your current edit: voice, music, fades on every audio cut
- Listen back on a phone speaker before you call it done

## 25. Using Sound Effects Without Cluttering
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Sound effects are punctuation. Punctuation you overuse stops meaning anything

**2. Principle**
- What SFX actually do: mark a transition, add impact, signal a change, direct attention
- The small vocabulary that covers most short-form: whoosh, impact, riser, click/pop, ambience
- **Frame-accurate timing** — an effect two frames late reads as a mistake even if the viewer can't say why
- Volume: an effect should be felt, not consciously heard
- Layering a whoosh and an impact for a transition that hits properly
- Ambience and room tone as a bed that makes a cut feel continuous
- The over-SFX trap and how it reads: cheap, busy, trying too hard

**3. Demo**
- Place a whoosh on a transition, first sloppily then frame-accurate, so they hear the difference
- Layer whoosh + impact on the same moment
- Add an ambience bed under a B-roll sequence
- Deliberately over-SFX a 10-second section, then strip it back to two

**4. The Trap**
- An effect on every cut
- Effects at full volume sitting on top of the voice

**5. Drill**
- Add a maximum of three sound effects to your entire edit
- Snap each one to the exact frame

## 26. Recording and Editing Voiceover
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- VO gives you total control of pacing. It's the fastest way to make a B-roll piece feel authored

**2. Principle**
- When VO beats a talking head: process content, storytelling over footage, anything where your face isn't the point
- Recording well without a studio: soft room, close mic distance, avoiding reflective surfaces
- Why room echo is the one thing you cannot fix later
- Delivery: speaking rather than reading, energy slightly above conversational, pacing with intent
- Recording in passes and picking the best take instead of chasing one perfect run
- Editing VO: cutting breaths, tightening gaps, removing the moments where you clearly restarted
- **Writing the cut around the VO** — VO should drive the edit, not be laid over a finished one

**3. Demo**
- Record a VO take live, including a bad one
- Show the room setup and what changing distance does to the sound
- Edit the take: breaths, gaps, restarts
- Lay it against footage and adjust the cut to the VO rather than the other way round

**4. The Trap**
- Reading a script in a flat voice — audible instantly
- Recording in a bare room and trying to fix the echo afterwards

**5. Drill**
- Record 30 seconds of VO for your B-roll piece
- Edit out every breath and restart, then cut the visuals to it

## 27. Audio Cleanup and Repair
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Some audio problems are fixable and some aren't. Knowing which is which saves you from wasting an hour on a lost cause

**2. Principle**
- What's fixable: consistent background noise, hum, mild harshness, uneven levels
- What isn't: heavy echo, clipping, wind distortion, a voice buried below the noise floor
- **Noise reduction and what it costs** — every reduction takes something out of the voice, so less is more
- EQ basics for voice: rolling off the rumble at the bottom, easing harshness in the upper mids
- Compression in plain terms: making loud and quiet parts closer together so voice sits steady
- Leveling across clips so the viewer never touches their volume
- Covering unfixable audio with music, ambience or a cutaway
- Why every one of these is a repair, and fixing it at the source always wins

**3. Demo**
- Apply noise reduction at a sane level, then at a heavy level so they hear the artifacts **[CHECK: noise reduction / voice enhance controls on CapCut PC]**
- Roll off low-end rumble on a voice clip
- Level three clips recorded at different volumes
- Take a genuinely unfixable clip and demonstrate covering it instead

**4. The Trap**
- Maxing noise reduction and turning a voice into something underwater
- Trying to save audio that should be re-recorded or cut

**5. Drill**
- Clean the audio on your current edit: noise, levels, one EQ move
- A/B against the original before and after each step

## 🔴 LIVE EDIT 04 — Color and Audio Polish Pass
**Shot type:** Live Edit

**Format:** take a finished, unpolished cut and run the entire polish pass in real time.

**What to hit:**
- Play the unpolished version first so the improvement is measurable
- Correct every clip, then grade, then match — in that order, out loud
- Choose music on camera, including rejecting a track and saying why
- Build the mix: levels, ducking, fades
- Add no more than three sound effects
- Play the before and after twice — once muted, once with sound — so they see color and hear audio separately
