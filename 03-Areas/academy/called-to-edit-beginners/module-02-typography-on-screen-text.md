# Module 02 — Typography & On-Screen Text

## 12. Dynamic Typography Principles
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- Text is a design element with a job. Most people treat it as an afterthought and it shows immediately

**2. Principle**
- **Readability before style** — contrast, weight, size, and the fact that it's being read on a phone at arm's length
- Safe zones: platform UI eats the bottom and right of every frame
- **Hierarchy:** one message on screen at a time. Two competing lines means neither gets read
- **Timing:** text should appear when the idea lands, not before it and not after
- What makes text feel alive: purposeful motion, tight timing, restraint
- What makes it feel cluttered: too many fonts, too many words, movement on everything
- Word count per card — if they have to pause to read it, it's too long
- What your font choice signals, and why picking one type system and reusing it builds recognition

**3. Demo**
- Show the same line of text five ways: bad contrast, too small, too long, over-animated, and correct
- Overlay the platform UI safe zones on a real frame
- Show two competing text elements, then remove one

**4. The Trap**
- Text that repeats exactly what's being said out loud, word for word, with no emphasis — that's captions, not typography
- A different font every video

**5. Drill**
- Choose your type system now: one display font, one body font, one accent color
- Write them down. You'll use this for every video in the rest of the course

## 13. Captions That Actually Get Read
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Most of your audience is watching muted. Captions aren't accessibility garnish, they're the primary channel

**2. Principle**
- Why captions drive retention: sound-off viewing, comprehension, and the eye having something to lock onto
- **Auto-captions are a first draft, not a result** — the review pass is the whole job
- Word-by-word vs phrase-by-phrase: energy vs readability, and which content suits which
- Sync precision: a caption a beat late breaks the illusion instantly
- Styling for legibility over *any* footage — stroke, shadow, or a plate behind the text
- **Emphasis:** highlighting the one word that carries the point, not every third word
- Placement, safe zone, and never covering your own face
- Saving a caption style so every video you make matches

**3. Demo**
- Generate auto-captions on the Live Edit 01 project
- Do the correction pass on camera — show the errors, fix the sync **[CHECK: current auto-caption + edit workflow on CapCut PC]**
- Style from default to finished: font, size, weight, stroke, position
- Add emphasis to three words and explain why those three
- Save the style as a preset and apply it to a second clip

**4. The Trap**
- Shipping auto-captions unreviewed. It reads as careless and viewers notice
- Captions so stylized they're harder to read than plain text

**5. Drill**
- Caption your Lesson 06 cut end to end
- Review it once with the sound off and fix anything you can't read instantly

## 14. Animating Text
**Shot type:** Screen Record

**1. Open**
- Text that appears is fine. Text that arrives is better. Text that dances is usually worse

**2. Principle**
- Presets vs keyframed text — when a preset is genuinely fine and when it isn't
- The reliable combination: scale plus opacity, eased. Works in every editor, never looks cheap
- **Timing to speech:** the text hits on the word, not on the beat after it
- **Read time:** hold long enough for the slowest viewer, which is longer than feels comfortable to you
- Attaching text to motion in the shot so it belongs in the frame instead of floating on it
- Direction of entry as meaning — text that comes from the subject, from the edge, from nowhere
- Restraint: when the animation becomes louder than the message it's carrying

**3. Demo**
- Build a clean in/out with keyframes from scratch — scale + opacity + easing
- Time three text hits to speech, showing one placed late so they hear the wrongness
- Attach a text element to a moving subject
- Apply a heavy preset and compare it directly against the keyframed version

**4. The Trap**
- Animating every caption. Motion on everything means emphasis on nothing
- Text that leaves before it's been read

**5. Drill**
- Add three animated text hits to your current edit, keyframed by hand
- No presets for this one

## 🔴 LIVE EDIT 02 — Adding Text and Captions
**Shot type:** Live Edit

**Format:** open the Live Edit 01 project. Same footage, next layer. Real time.

**What to hit:**
- Play the cuts-only version first so the contribution of text is measurable
- Generate captions, then do the full correction and sync pass on camera
- Style them live using the type system from Lesson 12
- Add two or three intentional text hits — and say out loud why you're *not* adding more
- Play cut-only vs cut-plus-text back to back at the end
