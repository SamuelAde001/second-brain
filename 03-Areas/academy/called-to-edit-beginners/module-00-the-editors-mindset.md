# Module 00 — The Editor's Mindset

Source's own module description: *"Nothing technical. Four lessons. This module is why the rest of the course works, and it's what makes this different from a YouTube tutorial."*

Four lessons. No live edit session in this module.

Lesson spine (Open / Principle / Demo / Trap / Drill) and shot-type definitions are in [[teaching-rules]] — not repeated per lesson here beyond the content itself.

---

## 01. Introduction
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- This is a course about editing, not about a piece of software
- One line on what they'll be able to make by the end

**2. Principle**
- The tool changes, the craft doesn't — a good editor is good in any software
- CapCut is what you'll see on screen; the thinking is what you're buying
- Everything taught here maps directly onto Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut

**3. Demo**
- The course map: eight modules, what each one adds
- How the live edit sessions work and why they're placed where they are
- Show a 15-second clip of the capstone result — this is the destination

**4. The Trap**
- Don't binge it. Editing is a motor skill, not information
- Don't wait until you feel ready to start cutting — Module 01 starts in software

**5. Drill**
- Install CapCut, download the practice footage pack, open a project
- Come to Lesson 2 with one video of your own you wish was better

## 02. Editing Mindset Fundamentals
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- Editing is decision-making. The software is just how you record the decision

**2. Principle**
- What an edit actually is: choosing what the viewer sees, in what order, for how long
- Intention vs decoration — every cut, effect and title needs a reason that isn't "it looked cool"
- **Signal vs noise:** does this choice change whether the video connects, or is it just technically correct?
- The viewer doesn't know what you removed — they only feel the result
- Audience awareness: who is watching, on what, in what mood
- Niche conventions — a fitness edit, a vlog and a talking-head essay obey different rules and you have to know which room you're in

**3. Demo**
- Show the same 20 seconds cut two ways: decorated vs intentional
- Talk over both. Don't explain which is better — let them feel it, then name it

**4. The Trap**
- Copying an edit style you like without knowing what job it's doing
- Adding things to "make it look edited" — the most common beginner instinct and the most damaging

**5. Drill**
- Write one sentence for your next video: "This video exists to ______"
- Keep it visible while you edit it

## 03. Anatomy of a Banger — Hook, Tension, Payoff
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- Structure beats polish. A well-structured rough cut outperforms a beautiful mess every time

**2. Principle**
- The three-part shape: hook, tension, payoff — and what each one owes the viewer
- **The hook (first 1–2 seconds):** stop the scroll and open a question. Not "introduce yourself"
- Hook types an editor actually controls: cold open, visual, text, motion, audio, mid-sentence start
- **Tension:** the reason to still be there at second 5. Open loops, withheld information, escalation, pattern breaks
- Where viewers leave: the setup that goes too long, the point that arrives too late, the flat middle
- **Payoff:** delivering what the hook promised. A video that doesn't pay off teaches people to scroll past you next time
- Ending vs looping — when to close and when to send them back to frame one
- How the shape stretches: 15 seconds vs 60 vs 3 minutes

**3. Demo**
- Break down three real short-form videos on screen — pause and name the hook, the tension device, the payoff
- Pick one that fails and diagnose where it lost the viewer

**4. The Trap**
- Burying the hook behind an intro, a logo, or "so basically"
- Building tension you never resolve — the video that just stops

**5. Drill**
- Take your own last video and write down its hook, tension and payoff
- If any of the three is blank, that's the fix

## 04. Planning Your Edit Before You Start
**Shot type:** Presentation

**1. Open**
- The edit is mostly decided before you open the software. What's left is execution

**2. Principle**
- Watch everything before cutting anything — you can't structure footage you haven't seen
- Marking as you watch: keepers, hooks, moments that surprised you
- Finding the hook *inside the footage you already have*, not the one you planned
- Beats on paper, not a script: 5–8 lines that describe the shape
- Deciding the one thing the video is for, before the first cut
- Studying a reference edit properly — name what it's doing, don't copy what it looks like
- The swipe file habit: saving edits that worked and writing one line on *why*

**3. Demo**
- Watch a raw clip set on screen in real time, taking notes as you go
- Turn those notes into a 6-line beat sheet, live

**4. The Trap**
- Cutting from the first frame forward, chronologically, hoping structure appears
- Planning so hard you never start

**5. Drill**
- Take the practice footage, watch it all the way through, produce a 6-line beat sheet
- Don't edit yet
