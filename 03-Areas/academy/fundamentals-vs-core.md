# Fundamentals vs. core editing content

## What this is

From the "Balancing fundamentals with core editing content" chat (2026-08-07 to 2026-08-09) — an earlier-stage working session than the teaching-structure documents already in the vault ([[teaching-rules]], [[current-production]], [[called-to-edit-beginners/_index|Called to Edit: Beginners]]). Most of what came out of this chat — the 31-lesson, 8-module curriculum, the five-beat lesson spine, the "editing not CapCut" repositioning — is already recorded there in full detail and isn't repeated here. This note holds two things that aren't recorded elsewhere: the actual tension Samuel raised and how it got resolved, and one specific create-vs-edit boundary case this chat surfaced.

## The question he actually asked

His own words, opening the chat:

> "Do you think I should be teaching up subjects like Capcut tour, file organisation, and some others when I can just go to the point of the course which is the main editing itself as that's what people came to learn... I feel people will get bored of the course and feel this are things everyone knows and I am wasting their time teaching unnneccarry stuff when they came to actually learn the main video editing itself."

And later, stating plainly what he wants the course to actually be:

> "I am focused on the principles of editing, and the skills that make you an actual good editor for your content."

## The reframe that resolved it

Not "cut vs. keep" but **"standalone module vs. embedded just-in-time."** The pushback that got him there: "everyone already knows this" is the trap experts fall into teaching beginners — skipping orientation doesn't remove the confusion, it relocates it to a worse moment (mid-lesson, hunting for a button, while you're trying to teach pacing). That produced three buckets, applying his own signal-vs-noise filter to *sequencing*, not just content selection:

1. **Cut entirely** — content that contradicts the course's own positioning. The one clear case: a full "survey of every editing software" lesson, dropped because the course was CapCut-specific at that point.
2. **Compress into a fast cold-open, not a standalone lesson** — content that's genuinely necessary but shouldn't gate access to real editing. The software tour became a 60–90 second orientation folded into the first cutting lesson.
3. **Move later, don't cut** — content that's real but, by his own test, doesn't affect whether any single video connects with an audience: file organization, templates, the asset library. These moved to a late bonus module instead of gating the course open.

## The boundary case this chat surfaced — not recorded elsewhere

While merging lessons, one fold-in was flagged directly against the create/edit line already on file in [[courses]]:

> "Finding real-world visuals" folded into the B-roll lesson. **Flag: this one leans toward Create Academy — it's about what to film, not how to cut.**

This is a live example of the exact test [[courses]] states in the abstract — *"Create Academy teaches why and what to film or cut. The Edit courses teach how to execute in software"* — actually being applied to one real piece of content. **What happened to it is worth stating plainly: it didn't get moved to Create Academy, and it didn't get cut. It got folded into an Edit-course lesson anyway, in compressed form, flagged but kept**, because it's in direct service of executing a technique — you need visuals before you can build a B-roll sequence with them. This doesn't overturn the "hard line" in [[courses]], but it shows the line gets applied as a judgment call at individual-lesson level, not only at the course level, and that a little "what to film" content stays embedded inside an Edit lesson when the alternative is a real gap in the how-to. No other note in the vault records this specific case — confirmed by search.

## The bigger correction, in his own words

Two days later, Samuel corrected the whole frame the curriculum work had been built on:

> "Why did you do a teaching structure for capcut? ... the aim is to teach the main principles of editing regardless of the editing software, just that I am using capcut for this demostration... I don't care if it is PC or mobile anymore... also I don't care if it is a paid feature or free, as far as it's something important to teach the editing and get them better in the editing from beginning to end."

This is the correction that produced the "editing, not CapCut" positioning, the dropped platform/tier columns, and the five-beat lesson spine (Open → Principle → Demo → Trap → Drill) — all already recorded in full in [[teaching-rules]]. Noted here only because this transcript is where the correction actually happened, in his own words; the downstream document doesn't show that moment.

## The loose end this chat left open

Samuel then asked whether he should record in DaVinci Resolve instead — the tool he already knows well — versus CapCut, which the course was built around for beginner accessibility. Claude's recommendation was to stay in CapCut: DaVinci's multi-page interface (Cut, Edit, Fusion, Color, Fairlight, Deliver) raises the intimidation threshold for a true beginner, and switching would quietly break the free-tier, low-end-device accessibility promise the course is built on. **The transcript ends without Samuel's reply.** [[current-production]] — a later, more current source (2026-09-06) — confirms the course did stay CapCut-based, so this got resolved eventually; it just didn't get resolved in this conversation.

## Does this change the create/edit boundary in courses.md?

Not the stated rule — the "hard line" in [[courses]] stands as written: Create teaches why/what to film, Edit teaches how to execute. What this chat adds is the one place in the vault showing that line actually being drawn against a real piece of content, and the outcome wasn't a clean removal — it was a flagged, compressed fold-in kept inside the Edit course. Worth knowing if the same judgment call comes up again while the rest of the curriculum gets built out.
