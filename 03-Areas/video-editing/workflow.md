# The editing workflow — brief to payment

Samuel's own account, 2026-09-20. This is what actually happens, in order. Everything the editing agent is built to take over is a step in this list.

## 1. Get the script

A brief arrives. **First move is to get the script for it** — before anything else.

## 2. Download the video

The raw footage comes down from the client.

## 3. Cut out the fluff

First pass on the timeline: cut the video down, remove all the fluff.

## 4. Claude pass — transcript and what's still uncut

The cut goes into Claude to produce the transcript **and to answer "what have I not cut out yet?"** Claude does a lot of the cutting decisions here — finding what still needs to come out.

## 5. Transcription

Always transcribe, so there is a transcript to refer back to **throughout the edit**. Existing skill: `subtitle-transcript-formatter`.

## 6. Ideation — the visuals

Work out what visual sits on **every sentence** of the video: motion graphics, where a screen recording is needed, screenshots, real-life footage, B-roll. Claude supplies the visual ideas alongside the script.

## 7. Edit — add the visuals end to end

The real work. Visuals go in from the start of the video to the end.

> **"I make sure that the visuals take most of the time. So that's where you see me working for three to five days."**

## 8. Sound effects

After the visuals.

## 9. Render and deliver

Back to the client.

## 10. Invoice and payment

**Not paid per video on delivery.** At the end of the month he invoices for however many videos he did that month — four, or whatever the count was. Then:

- **70% paid at the end of that month.**
- **30% paid in the middle of the following month.**

So the money for any month's work is only fully in hand by the middle of the next month. See [[03-Areas/finances/invoicing|Invoicing]] — this is the timing the budget system's Receipt Ratio was built around.

## Pace

**One video in five days** is the normal expectation, depending on the video assigned.

Steps 3–7 are where the time goes, and they are exactly what [[agent-plan]] targets. The intro stays Samuel's — see that note.
