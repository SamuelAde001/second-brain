# Creative director breakdown 06: Matt #1 (How I'd Sell AI Digital Products, Matthias Bellmann)

The company's creative director (he does not say his name in this recording) screen-shares a 7.4-minute training on Matthias Bellmann's channel ("a different client, different editor"; the editor is never named). The main video is "How I'd Sell AI Digital Products (If I had to Start Over)" (13:20 long on the YouTube player); he reviews its hook and first ~5 minutes. From 05:20 he switches to JP Middleton's "Claude + Google Maps = $30,000/Mo" (the agency's reference list notes it as "simple, but very effective to TEACH viewers") and plays its hook and intro. He calls the Matthias style "a little bit more simple", "more heavy on text graphics and animations, sometimes b-rolls", with "very nice colour" and "pretty consistent good visuals". On the channel page he also points at other Matthias videos worth studying; the agency's reference list on screen names "Why Digital Products are Bullsh*t (I made $75M with them)" and "It's Boring, But Will Make Beginners Millionaires in 2026". Source: `Docs\Creative director breakdowns (Editor)\transcripts\06-matt-1.txt` plus frames every 5 s.

On screen, the Matthias look is magenta/hot pink on near-black (dark red-black gradient), white bold italic caps with pink keywords, glowing rounded cards with big numbers and flat icons. JP's video uses the same box language in purple.

## Points in order

- **00:00-00:24** [process & tools] Style intro: simpler, text graphics and animations first, some B-roll, "very nice colour", "consistent good visuals". Rule: a video can be mostly motion graphics if they are consistent and well coloured.
- **00:46-01:14** [process & tools] Why the breakdowns exist: understand "how we want the video to look", "why you're doing the edits", the techniques, "so that you can apply all these into your own videos": "the right frequency of visuals, the right style of visuals ... the right sound design". Rule: judge every edit on frequency, style, look and sound, and know why each edit is there.
- **01:20-01:35** [hook] Hook opens on "a slide" of his real first product (an Excel course page). "We highlight it, dark in the screen, we put the big text here for the viewer to focus": page darkened, pink border on the price card, big white/pink "MY FIRST DIGITAL PRODUCT". Rule: when showing a real page, darken the rest, highlight the one element, and put a big keyword title over it.
- **01:35-01:47** [charts & data] "Monthly earnings $17 / account balance" card over the talking head. "It pops up purple and then it shakes and it turns grey, because ... 17 is not a lot", with a sound effect. Rule: animate a number's meaning: pop in with colour, then shake and desaturate to grey when the number is bad. Always with an SFX.
- **01:47-01:58** [sound & music] "We had a sound effect in the background as well." Rule: every number pop/shake gets a sound.
- **01:52-02:04** [UI & screen recordings] Second product (his current business) shown as "a just slow, well presented" scroll of the site. Rule: website B-roll is a slow, clean scroll, not a jumpy one.
- **02:04-02:13** [charts & data] "When we're showing money we're not just showing a basic money counter": Passion.io logo + "$75,000,000" in a glowing pink card, "nice different colours", "ching sound effect". Rule: brand the money figure (logo of the business that made it), style it, add a cash SFX. Never a plain counter.
- **02:13-02:17** [charts & data] "Sometimes you can show [the] account", "there's different ways to do it, obviously depends on the video". Rule: pick the money visual that fits: branded card, account screenshot, counter.
- **02:21-02:31** [typography & text] "DIGITAL PRODUCTS ARE DEAD": "darkened background there, text, nice glow on the text, keywords in colour" (DEAD in red over blurred YouTube results). Rule: darken/blur the background, glow on white text, colour the keyword.

## The rules from this video

1. Tease, then reveal: show steps and chapters with their labels blurred, reveal each as he says it ("good for retention").
2. Never a plain money counter: brand the figure (business logo), style it in a glowing card, add a cash/ching SFX.
3. Encode judgement in colour and motion: bad number or rejected option pops, shakes and turns grey; the winner glows green. Each with a sound.
4. When showing a real page, darken it, highlight the one element he means, and put big keyword text over it.
5. Full-screen statements: darkened background, white glowing caps, one keyword in the accent or red.
6. Simple and super clear beats complex, especially for older viewers in teaching niches (logo + logo = result).
7. Section graphics: title on top, one diagram or card set below, the same palette all video.
8. Text big enough for mobile; chapter cards included ("make the text bigger").
9. No childish or emoji-style icons on premium cards; keep icons clean and on-style.
10. B-roll matches the exact action spoken; brands and people named get their logo or real profile on screen.
11. Add the small qualifier he says to a number ("generated in 2026") in a sub-bubble.
12. Screen-recording sections: tight cuts, presenter cam in a corner, spotlight the area being read, blur the rest.
13. End the intro with a "COMING UP..." tease of the screen recording, blurred.
14. Slow, clean scrolls for website B-roll; slight, smooth transitions between graphic states.
15. Reuse a device once it works (grey + shake, blur reveal) so the video feels consistent.

## What this means for Samuel's Alex videos

- Same mechanics, Alex's colours: blur-then-reveal steps, grey + shake for the rejected option, green for the winner, branded money cards with SFX all transfer straight to Alex's SaaS/B2B numbers.
- Clash with the Frontal look: Matthias uses rounded, glowing pink cards; the square, blue Frontal kit should keep its square corners and blue accent. Take the behaviour (darken, glow, focus, grey-out), not the pink rounded styling.
- Alex's tool and company names (Claude, Apollo, SaaS logos) should get the "logo + logo = result" treatment and grey out when he dismisses one.
- Check every chapter/step card for phone-size text, and drop any emoji-style icons.
- For Alex's screen-recording sections: cam in a corner, spotlight the line being read, blur the rest, and a "coming up" blurred tease at the end of the intro.
