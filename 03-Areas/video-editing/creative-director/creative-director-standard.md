# Creative director standard: the six breakdowns, distilled

What Route Rise's creative director asks of every edit, merged from his six recorded trainings (the "MOST UP TO DATE + IN DEPTH BREAKDOWN" list) and checked against Samuel's own rules. Where Samuel has ruled differently, his ruling is stated and wins. Each rule carries its source as `(note time)`, where the time is in the training recording: 01-jp, 02-jp, 03-andy, 04-nate, 05-alex, 06-matt. Lines marked *(Editor's inference)* are the Editor's reading. No source states them.

**This is the standard for every video, every client, from 2026-10-22.** Samuel, 2026-10-22: *"This study must be used as standard in every video we do from hence"*. Order of authority: Samuel's own rulings and markers first, then the client's brand kit for colours, fonts and shapes (for Alex: [[03-Areas/video-editing/clients/frontal-brand-v2|Frontal brand v2]] as Samuel ruled it on 2026-10-22), then this standard, then the older house notes.

## Who he is

- He is Route Rise's creative director, the agency's head of editing standard. He never says his name in the recordings. The sign-off in 04-nate (54:40) reads "message me on slack sean". [[03-Areas/video-editing/ways-of-working|Ways of working]] (2026-10-15) calls him **Shawn**, and Samuel counts his notes as Samuel's own corrections.
- He calls these videos "the exact way we expect our videos to look on all of our channels" (01-jp 00:01). The point of the trainings is that a first video does not come back with "300 comments" (01-jp) or "hundreds of comments from me" (04-nate 22:59).
- He judges every edit on four things: visual frequency, visual style, the look, and the sound design. He also asks *why* each edit was made (06-matt 00:46, 01-jp 00:01).

## The principles he repeats (in at least four of the six notes)

1. **Triple attack.** He says it, the text says it, and a visual shows it (01-jp 02:36, 04-nate 3:59, 05-alex 17:03). Before you design anything, understand what he says and why (01-jp 02:49, 04-nate 22:12, 05-alex 22:03).
2. **Show, don't tell.** Every sentence gets a visual of exactly that (02-jp 1:47, 03-andy 11:20, 05-alex 22:03). Plain text alone is the weakest option.
3. **Real proof first.** Use the creator's footage, real UI, real sites, articles and data. Only then use AI B-roll made to match the line (02-jp 9:03, 03-andy 01:35, 04-nate 31:18, 05-alex 04:44).
4. **Never static.** Put a slow push or pan on every shot, still and graphic. Movement is constant but moderate (02-jp 5:18, 03-andy 06:00, 04-nate 8:19, 05-alex 03:54).
5. **One focal point.** "Even a 75-year-old viewer" must get it, and the eyes never chase around the screen (01-jp 03:34, 02-jp 4:15, 03-andy 13:28, 04-nate 10:14).
6. **Darken behind everything** so the text and the graphic pop (01-jp 06:42, 02-jp 2:15, 03-andy 04:03, 04-nate 25:30, 05-alex 03:45).
7. **Steer the eye with light.** Zoom to the spot, darken the rest, then shine it or unblur it. On text, use the "double highlight" (01-jp 12:41, 02-jp 4:15, 04-nate 14:40, 05-alex 03:01).
8. **Tease with blur.** Blur the promised answer, the chapter names, the steps and a "COMING UP..." preview. Reveal each on its word (all six notes; e.g. 03-andy 05:38, 04-nate 6:45, 06-matt 03:14).
9. **A title on top** of every list, chart and diagram (01-jp 09:09, 03-andy 05:16, 04-nate 6:02, 05-alex 14:55, 06-matt 02:31).
10. **Colour carries judgement.** Red is negative, green is money, the winner or positive, and grey is dead, rejected or a competitor (02-jp 1:00, 04-nate 9:21, 05-alex 22:21, 06-matt 04:31).
11. **Mobile first.** Big, bold and centred, with one or two things on screen at a time (04-nate 5:21, 05-alex 01:43, 06-matt 03:09).
12. **Build a library and reuse it.** Once a style works for a client, reuse the same music, SFX and devices for the same ideas, with "copy paste with slight changes" (03-andy 02:01, 05-alex 20:24, 06-matt 04:46).

## Rules by category

### Hook
- He opens on the creator's face 75 % of the time, for 1-2 s with a slow zoom out. He opens on a visual when the script calls for it (01-jp 01:28-01:56, 04-nate 3:01).
- A dense, layered first second is fine: in Samuel's B2B hook, Alex sits over a "0% growth" pipeline graph with text and a shine (05-alex 02:18).
- **A new visual about every second** (01-jp 07:03, 04-nate 12:46, 05-alex 23:52).
- On the tension line, the card turns red with a screen shake and a hit (04-nate 3:44 and 42:43).
- When he dismisses the alternatives, show them first, then grey them (02-jp 0:05, 03-andy 02:45).
- Blur the prize and the promised answer (02-jp 1:17, 02-jp 10:33, 01-jp 09:51). Add a "?" when the viewer should guess (04-nate 31:31).
- On "In this video", show the chapters as numbered boxes (02-jp 11:36, 04-nate 43:58).
- Use more SFX in the hook than anywhere else (02-jp 10:33, 04-nate 8:52, 05-alex 01:59).
- End the intro with a blurred "COMING UP..." preview of the tutorial (01-jp 20:06, 02-jp 7:34, 05-alex 17:12, 06-matt 06:34). Use a tension riser into chapter one (01-jp 11:24, 03-andy 04:25).
- Not for Alex: the giant keyword behind the cut-out speaker (03-andy 01:53) breaks Samuel's "nothing behind his back" (2026-10-21).

### Pacing and retention
- **Visual frequency: every 1 s in the hook, every 2 s in the intro, every 5-10 s in the body, 15 s at most** (04-nate 12:46). On Alex's channel the body is "every like five to ten seconds" (05-alex 23:52).
- A visual people have to read stays "at least a few seconds". Only a one-glance visual (a red graph going down, 8 of 10 icons lit) may flash (05-alex 11:00, 05-alex 21:24).
- No more than about 30 s on a screen that doesn't change. After that, cut to the full-screen face or to B-roll (01-jp 15:18).
- Extend a graphic by building its next layer rather than cutting back to the creator (03-andy 06:23). Pop new things onto the shot instead of cutting to another screenshot (04-nate 49:14).
- Once a device works (grey + shake, blur reveal), reuse it all video (06-matt 04:46).
- Leave a small gap uncut now and then "to make it seem more natural" (04-nate 52:55).

### Typography
- The on-screen text matches the spoken words "97% of the time". Change a word or two only when it reads better (03-andy 08:36).
- Always darken behind text (02-jp 2:15, 04-nate 25:30). Over darkened B-roll, put a big centred caption with a glow and the keyword in colour (01-jp 06:42, 04-nate 5:12, 06-matt 02:21).
- Text is white. Keywords take the client's accent colour, negative words go red, and real logos keep their own colours (01-jp 10:04, 01-jp 21:43, 03-andy 04:19, 04-nate 33:13).
- The text lands first, then each supporting visual in its own box. A shine crosses the key phrase as he says it (01-jp 03:59-04:19).
- Vary the text. It should never look like "basic boring subtitle text": use a gradient, a second style for a number or year, italics sometimes, text in a box (04-nate 6:54, 13:24, 34:31, 40:00; 05-alex 08:45, 23:18).
- Write numbers as 1, 2, never 01, 02. Chapter numbers in white so they don't blend into the box (04-nate 43:30, 05-alex 05:20).
- Labels that appear together sit close to the centre, never in the far corners (03-andy 02:53, 04-nate 33:51).
- A quote gets quote marks, the person's picture and the attribution (02-jp 5:42).
- Text can go at the bottom over the creator or centred over darkened B-roll, depending on the line (03-andy 07:44). Punch in and add a bottom line on intimate lines (03-andy 14:24).

### Boxes and motion graphics
- **Boxes are opaque.** He calls see-through "hollow" panels cheap: "too many layers on screen" (03-andy 09:42, 04-nate 13:47).
- Run a shine across each item in turn as he names it, including the part of a diagram he points at (02-jp 1:27, 02-jp 6:03, 03-andy 05:46, 03-andy 10:59).
- Chapter cards get a number, a title and an icon (01-jp 09:30, 04-nate 11:17, 04-nate 40:38).
- Money is never a bare counter. Give it a box, a subline and the business logo, and add a ching SFX (02-jp 0:26, 06-matt 02:04). Add the small qualifier he says, such as "generated in 2026" (06-matt 05:57).
- A stat card is a big number in the accent colour, plus a detail line, a different text style and a logo (05-alex 06:01).
- Layer the aesthetic: shapes in different sizes and shades, concentric rings, glows. It reads "more premium" (04-nate 13:34, 04-nate 32:33).
- Icons are solid and custom. Emoji-style icons look "a little bit like childish" (04-nate 13:47, 06-matt 02:50).
- Simple and exact beats complex: logo + logo = result, or one icon grid split into groups (02-jp 6:38, 02-jp 11:03, 06-matt 05:35).
- Show a ratio as countable icons that fill (05-alex 03:18, 06-matt 04:56).
- Dim the item he is not talking about (05-alex 26:05, 05-alex 14:55). Tell groups apart by colour and logo (05-alex 12:42).
- Animate a process step by step on his words (03-andy 13:28, 05-alex 18:34). Make a metaphor plain with an arrow, a one-word label and a zoom (02-jp 4:15).
- Elements arrive and leave with smooth slides. Callouts leave the screen eased (02-jp 0:41, 03-andy 07:17).

### UI and screen recordings
- The screen fills the frame. The creator's camera sits small in a corner and moves if it hides something (01-jp 12:05, 06-matt 06:47).
- Use three tricks: **zoom**, **lighting** (darken all but the area) and **progressive unblur** as he reads each part (01-jp 12:41, 05-alex 19:16, 06-matt 06:57). Use the same tricks on normal graphics (01-jp 14:04).
- **Double highlight:** on an article or document, darken everything except the line, then highlight the exact words. He flagged twice that leaving the page bright is a mistake (03-andy 12:50, 04-nate 30:46, 04-nate 37:56). Done right: 04-nate 49:08, 05-alex 03:01. Mix highlight styles, for example an underline (04-nate 49:30).
- Highlight the exact detail the line names, for example the view counts when he says views (01-jp 08:36).
- Prefer the real interface, typed live (05-alex 07:13). Real Claude "looks more professional and official" than a mock (04-nate 16:24, 04-nate 27:05).
- Name a site or tool, then show it full screen with a slow zoom (01-jp 15:51, 03-andy 08:58). Scroll websites slowly and cleanly (01-jp 06:25, 06-matt 01:52).
- Act out the verb with a cursor and real icons (02-jp 2:08).
- No music under long screen-recording stretches (01-jp 15:07).

### B-roll
- Real first: the creator's own footage, real TikTok and YouTube clips, real pages. "It takes time to research these b-rolls but this is the quality we're looking for" (03-andy 09:04, 04-nate 31:18).
- AI B-roll is fine when nothing real exists. It must match the line exactly and never show a generator watermark (02-jp 4:43, 04-nate 53:43, 05-alex 04:58). AI stills must move (01-jp 17:25).
- Illustrate metaphors literally (05-alex 13:38, the fire) and emotions with emotion B-roll (02-jp 8:57, 03-andy 09:30).
- Show a named person as real footage or an animated still, and their real profile (02-jp 2:45, 06-matt 03:25).
- Stage a quoted client as a call with the thing he mentions on screen (05-alex 09:24).
- Grade B-roll to carry meaning: grey for dead or quiet, red corners for bad (05-alex 07:36, 05-alex 23:04).
- Fill 16:9 with vertical clips by tripling them (03-andy 06:55).
- Add fitting examples he didn't say when you understand the business (04-nate 36:31, 05-alex 22:47).

### Charts and data
- Every chart gets a title on top. Missing titles were his criticism of 01-jp (09:09).
- Abstract claims get one simple chart (01-jp 08:51). Animate the number he says (04-nate 3:33).
- A bad number pops, shakes and turns grey, with a sound (06-matt 01:35). Money lost is a falling red number in real-looking UI (03-andy 03:14).
- Show the wrong claim and the true one side by side: red X for the wrong one, the accent colour for the right one (05-alex 12:07). The winner glows green (06-matt 02:46).
- Back claims with a real chart, article or search result (03-andy 12:26). Benchmarks are glowing labelled bars (04-nate 39:03).

### Zooms
- Slow, constant and moderate: "not moving too fast... not moving super super slow boring" (04-nate 8:19). He criticised one talking-head stretch for having no slow zoom (05-alex 11:47).
- Punch in slowly on the emotional or personal beat (01-jp 18:53, 03-andy 14:24).

### Transitions
- Smooth, slow directional pushes and slides. "No crazy transitions" (01-jp 04:26, 03-andy 03:27, 04-nate 42:56, 06-matt 02:39).
- The slide direction follows the meaning: moving on goes to the right (03-andy 04:57). A new chapter gets a transition and a song change (03-andy 13:06).

### Colour
- Use a dark base with a gradient in the brand colour, with bright elements on top. Never bright on bright (05-alex 03:36). The beige background in Samuel's Apollo video was his "only bad thing" (05-alex 25:35).
- Rank items with tints of the one brand colour (05-alex 15:58). The shades may vary but the hue stays (05-alex 05:56).
- Grey the competitor and keep the light on the winner. Grey "is more chill" than red (04-nate 45:53).
- The look is a quality lever of its own: glow, contrast, layering and shades (04-nate 0:20, 04-nate 4:37).

### Sound and music
- Put Adobe Podcast (Enhance Speech) on the voice in every video (01-jp 02:11, 04-nate 1:55).
- The music never competes with the voice. When in doubt, take it down 3-5 % (01-jp 21:12, 04-nate 27:47).
- Change the song after the hook, after the intro and at each chapter, only in a gap in speech, fading out and in (01-jp 20:43, 03-andy 07:26, 04-nate 14:23). That is roughly 1-2 min per song (04-nate 20:03).
- Use a high-energy track in the hook and intro and a calmer one in the body (05-alex 10:28). Never use the same song twice in one video. Reusing one across videos is fine (05-alex 20:24).
- SFX are placed on purpose: a hit with a music dip on the punchline (01-jp 06:04), a boom on grey-out (06-matt 05:38), a ching on money (06-matt 02:04). Vary them and keep them low. Use fewer after the intro, but keep some (04-nate 8:52, 20:56, 44:16).
- Put a tension riser before a reveal (04-nate 30:12, 04-nate 45:05).

### CTA
- On "it's in the description", scroll the description on screen (04-nate 20:42). The community plug is clearer full screen (04-nate 29:06). The song changes for the outro (04-nate 28:53).

### Storytelling
- Every visual has a one-sentence reason (01-jp 00:01). Listen to the script and teach it "as if you were the teacher" (04-nate 37:30).
- When he says "this video", show it in context (02-jp 0:55). Tell a story beat by beat with images, each on a slow move (01-jp 17:41).
- Learn the client's niche so the right proof comes quickly (04-nate 21:07, 05-alex 06:35).

**Tensions, and how Samuel's ruling settles them**

1. **Orange and rounded vs Frontal.** He praised Alex's orange "SOP colour" and rounded glowing cards (05-alex 01:54, 06:14). Samuel ruled on 2026-10-22 that for Alex the fonts, the colours (Frontal) and the square boxes change, and nothing else. So his "client accent colour" now means Frontal Blue-300. Rounded becomes square. Glow, shine, glass and bevel stay ([[03-Areas/video-editing/clients/frontal-brand-v2|Frontal brand v2]] §11).
2. **Red words, glow, shadows and arrowheads.** The Frontal book bans these (red only as a small tag; no glow, shadow or arrowheads). Both Samuel (§11, marker 17) and the director use them. Samuel's ruling wins: red and green keywords, glow, shadows and arrowheads are allowed.
3. **Zoom until readable vs zoom out.** He zooms until a phone can read it (01-jp 13:19). Samuel said "Don't zoom too much... zoom out so we understand the context" (marker 4). Settled by Samuel's three points of focus: the first frame shows what the page is, then a zoom to the area (at most 1.25), then the words highlighted.
4. **"Every sentence a visual" vs Samuel's removals.** Samuel removed SY-01 and the SY-12 sales call (markers 14, 25). Those stay A-roll. The frequency targets count elements inside a visual as well as cuts *(Editor's inference)*.
5. **Text behind the speaker** (03-andy 01:53). Samuel: nothing behind Alex's back (2026-10-21). Not used.
6. **Source line on a stat card** (05-alex 06:01). Samuel removed it himself (2026-10-13). There is no source line. A logo and his own qualifier stay.
7. **Callouts leave eased** (02-jp 0:41). Samuel: hard cut from full-frame visuals into the A-roll, no fade-in (2026-10-10). Elements over the A-roll leave with his NeoAnim Out.
8. **Opaque boxes vs glass.** He calls see-through panels cheap (03-andy 09:42). Samuel wants glass. Glass must be blurred and tinted enough that nothing behind it reads as a second layer *(Editor's inference)*.
9. **SFX "only on punchlines"** (01-jp 06:04) vs Samuel's "every animated item gets its own sound", low and varied (2026-10-16). Samuel's rule wins. The director's other notes ask for SFX throughout too (05-alex 01:59).
10. **Music.** He wants a new song at each chapter and never one twice in a video. Samuel uses the last video's music in its order, one cue per major segment (2026-10-16). These fit together. Sound comes last, after Samuel approves the visuals.
11. **Not ruled yet; ask Samuel:** titles in all caps (04-nate 44:49), a slight side angle on cards (04-nate 6:50), and the tool's logo in the corner of tool B-roll (04-nate 40:09; Samuel bans text at the top of a capture). Until he says so: sentence case as planned, flat cards, and no corner logo *(Editor's inference)*.
