# Creative director breakdown 01: JP Middleton #1

The agency's creative director (his name is not said in the recording; he is the man in the webcam corner) walks through two JP Middleton videos from the agency's Notion page "TOP EDITS IN THE AGENCY, reference videos to study": **"$25M AI Expert Explains: Why Agentic Workflows are Bullsh\*t"** (about six months old at recording, 28:34 long) from 1:15 to 17:20, then **"If You Only Watch One AI Business Video, Make It This"** (18:27 long) from about 12:00 to the end. The editor of the videos is not named. He calls this "one of the most important trainings" in the course: it is "the exact way we expect our videos to look on all of our channels", and he wants editors to understand *why* each edit was made, so that a first video does not come back with "300 comments". Recording: `JP training #1.mkv`, 22.4 min. Hook/intro dissection 1:25-11:30; screen-recording method 11:30-17:00; AI-image storytelling, teasers, music changes and keyword colour 17:20-22:25. He stops mid-video and continues in training #2.

## Points in order

- **00:01-01:12** [process & tools] Watch it twice and take notes; the trainings exist so editors understand "the reference that we're looking for and the standard" before their first video. Do not copy edits blindly: "understand why the editor did it like this". Rule: for every visual you can explain the reason in one sentence.
- **01:28-01:50** [hook] Face or animation first? "75% of the time, we start with the creator's face", 25% start on a visual when the script calls for it. Here: face "for at least the first one or two seconds". On screen: JP mid-shot, warm-lit wall. Rule: default to opening on the creator's face for 1-2 s.
- **01:56** [zooms & camera] Opening shot has "a very slow zoom out". Rule: the first face shot is never static; a slow push-out.
- **02:02** [sound & music] Listen to "the volume of the background music, the song choice, amazing". Rule: music choice and level are judged from second one.
- **02:11-02:25** [sound & music] "On every client, basically, we use Adobe Podcast for his audio", so every video sounds the same, "very, very crisp". Repeats: "slow zoom out, good background music, good volume, Adobe Podcast on his audio". Rule: run the creator's voice through Adobe Podcast (Enhance Speech) on every video.
- **02:36-03:25** [storytelling & show-don't-tell] **Triple attack strategy**: "he says something, there's text, and then there's visuals that show exactly what he's saying". Level 2 = the text ("agentic workflows"); then real examples of people talking about agentic workflows and the AI tools' logos. On screen: "AGENTIC WORKFLOWS" title centred on purple, ringed by boxed screenshots of other creators, tool logos, a clock, a $49 receipt. Rule: every key line gets voice + on-screen words + a literal visual.
- **02:49** [storytelling & show-don't-tell] "Before you make a visual, the first thing you have to understand is what he says and why he's saying it." Rule: read the line's intent before designing anything.
- **03:34-03:55** [boxes & motion graphics] Two levels: the visual **idea**, then the **execution**. Execution = aesthetic, "but most importantly, it's clear for the viewer", "the viewer's eyes doesn't have to run away all around the screen", clear enough that "even a 75-year-old viewer can understand". Rule: one focal point; clarity beats decoration.
- **03:59-04:13** [typography & text] "We popped the text first. It has a nice glow on it. Nice colors." Then "all of the other visuals have their own little box or square or size, and they all pop on screen very, very smooth" ("very unique"). Rule: text lands first, then each supporting visual in its own framed card, staggered in.
- **04:15-04:19** [typography & text] A shine sweeps across the text exactly when he says "agentic workflows": "we add a shine on the text to reinforce it so the viewer focuses there and reads it once again". Rule: shine/sweep on the key phrase synced to the spoken word.
- **04:26-04:50** [transitions] As the shine finishes, a "smooth transition to the right". "All of our videos are always very, very smooth. Slow transitions. It's always moving, but it's very, very smooth movement." On screen 4:25-4:45: directional motion-blur push. Rule: constant but slow movement; directional push transitions, no hard jolts.
- **05:00-05:19** [UI & screen recordings] Line: write a couple of lines and you get an automation. "We show a visual example of typing a prompt into Claude", "nice glow", then the AI-built automation (a purple "AI Automation Dashboard"). On screen: a Claude-style prompt box typing "Build me an Agentic workflow because I'm lazy." Rule: rebuild the action he describes as a clean, glowing UI mock, typed live.
- **05:25-05:57** [colour & look] "That you never have to touch again" → the dashboard **turns gray** "to show that that's not the case. This is not real." "Even little things like that, the viewer's... subconscious brain picks up." Rule: use colour state (desaturate/grey) to carry the creator's judgement.
- **05:31-05:32** [zooms & camera] "Notice how smooth. Slow zoom in and then it goes down." Rule: slow zoom plus a gentle drift on every UI card.
- **05:34-05:39** [sound & music] "Pay attention to the sound effects... the volume of them and which sound effects we use and why we use them." Rule: every SFX is chosen and levelled on purpose.
- **06:04-06:13** [sound & music] On "That's complete bullshit": a sound effect "right there", and "we even cut the music a little bit". "We're not spamming sound effects, but when we use them, it's very, very well placed." Rule: punchline = one SFX plus a music dip; otherwise restraint.
- **06:25-06:36** [B-roll & stock] "I invested in an agentic AI company": the creator sent the website, so it goes on screen as B-roll with a smooth scroll ("notice how smooth the scroll is"). On screen: "Agentic AI for gyms and fitness studios" site. Rule: use client-supplied links as scrolling website B-roll.
- **06:42-06:54** [typography & text] "A trick we like to do a lot": darken the B-roll in the background and "layer text in the middle, very, very clear, nice glow", and "always put the colors on the keywords". On screen: dimmed site, white "I still have to tell it **what to do**" with the last words in purple. Rule: dim B-roll + centred glowing caption + keyword in the accent colour.
- **07:03-07:14** [pacing & retention] "For the hook, we have visuals every second." "We're showing everything he says visually... and smoothly", eyes never chase across the screen. Rule: in the hook, a new visual about every second.
- **07:24-07:46** [B-roll & stock] Triple attack on "whenever a new wave of AI videos from AI experts comes out": a stack of real AI YouTube videos, with a fake title "This is the best AI tool for business owners" on the mock player, because it fits the line. Rule: mock up YouTube UI with a title written for the line.
- **07:46-08:16** [storytelling & show-don't-tell] On "they exaggerate how good the tech really is": a frame from another creator's video with the on-screen words "ONE OF THE MOST POWERFUL AI TOOLS". "This visual perfectly matches what our creator is saying." "If you can't find this, you can just put fake text." Rule: find the real proof clip; if none exists, fake the caption.
- **08:23** [sound & music] "Pay attention to the background music. Again, the volume of it." (repeat of 02:02).
- **08:36-08:45** [storytelling & show-don't-tell] **Criticism**: "a little mistake". He says views are how they make money, so "we should have highlighted the view count of all of the videos or zoomed in to one of the view counts". On screen: the 12-thumbnail grid with nothing highlighted. Rule: when the line names a detail (views), highlight or zoom on that detail in the visual.
- **08:51-09:06** [charts & data] "Very simple graphic": a POWER line chart 2024-2029 with the tool logos in a bubble at the start, "still early compared to the future". Rule: abstract claims get one simple chart, nothing more.
- **09:09-09:26** [charts & data] **Criticism**: "usually we like to have a title on top... so the viewer reads the title and then they know exactly what the visual is"; here there was none, "we could have put a title like growth of AI tools". Rule: every chart/diagram gets a title at the top.
- **09:30-09:45** [boxes & motion graphics] Chapters preview: "always, you have custom graphics, the chapter title, and then you have the number". On screen: two glowing purple cards, "1 Why agentic workflows are bullshit?" and "2 What you should focus on instead?", each with an icon. "Look at how everything pops up smooth and clear." Rule: chapter previews = numbered custom cards with title and icon.
- **09:51-10:02** [pacing & retention] Suggestion: for "what you should focus on instead" you "could honestly put the answer down below blurred out" because "this is going to create retention", the viewer wants to find out later. Rule: when the hook promises an answer, show it blurred.
- **10:04-10:20** [typography & text] Claude Code, Antigravity, Clawdbot appear as real logos in their real colours: "you don't always have to spam the same color of the SOP. Sometimes it makes more sense to put the actual logo color" with the real word; that is triple attack too. On screen: red "CLAUDE CODE", red Clawdbot, blue Antigravity tags around card 1. Rule: brand names use their own logo and colour, not the house accent.
- **10:39-11:05** [pacing & retention] Rewatch of the full intro: "see how smooth everything is, how we're showing everything visually, try to understand the why behind the visuals".
- **11:24-11:31** [sound & music] "You can put a tension riser before we start the first chapter... and then boom, you transition into the actual main video." Rule: optional riser into "Let's get into it", hit on the cut to chapter one.
- **12:05-12:37** [UI & screen recordings] Screen-recording videos: the background (creator's recording, or slides made by the creator, the agency or you) is **full screen** "so that the viewer can actually see everything"; the creator's camera sits in a corner, "shouldn't be too big", and "don't be afraid to move the camera around" if it hides something. On screen: "# The AI Website" slide full frame, JP cam top-right. Rule: screen full frame, small movable cam.
- **12:41-13:16** [UI & screen recordings] Three tricks: **zooms** (push in on what he talks about), **lighting** ("darken the entire screen and you only keep light on this area"), and **progressive unblurring**: "you don't want the viewer to have access to the entire screen because that's bad for retention", keep it blurred and "unblur things as he reveals it". Rule: zoom, spotlight, and unblur line by line as he speaks.
- **13:19-13:43** [zooms & camera] In practice: zoomed in "so the viewer, even on mobile can read", the rest dark, unread lines blurred "to add retention". On screen 13:40-14:10: "The Problem" bullets unblur one by one (53% ... 3 seconds; 60%+ ...). Rule: zoom until text is phone-readable.
- **14:04-14:35** [zooms & camera] These zoom, lighting and blur tricks belong in normally edited videos too: "zoom into one animation or zoom into one graphic or darken the screen or use blurring tricks". Rule: apply spotlight/blur/zoom to your own graphics, not only screen recordings.
- **15:07-15:17** [sound & music] "There's no music" in long screen-recording sections, "only the guy talking. That's important." Rule: long screen-recording stretches run without music.
- **15:18-15:36** [pacing & retention] If he talks "longer than like 30 seconds and there's nothing changing in the screen, just go full screen" on the creator, "so at least the guy's moving around", and "sometimes add basic visuals, add a basic B-roll". Rule: max ~30 s on a static screen; then cut to full-screen face or B-roll.
- **15:51-16:10** [B-roll & stock] He mentions Google PageSpeed Insights, so "we literally show just very basic B-roll" of the PageSpeed report. Rule: name a tool = show the tool.
- **16:14-16:23** [UI & screen recordings] "We're using the lighting tricks to show the viewer what to look at. You don't want the viewer... to be confused." Rule: always direct the eye to the exact element he names.
- **16:23-16:50** [pacing & retention] When "the actual screen recording is useless" while he talks, let him talk full screen; then B-roll of a site's offer and form with lighting on the form; then back to the recording, "zooming in, lighting". "This is how we get maximum retention." On screen 16:40: gym site form "START YOUR TRANSFORMATION" spotlit.
- **17:25-17:35** [B-roll & stock] AI B-roll: "you don't always have to make video B-roll", sometimes a picture "but making it animated is better". Rule: AI stills are fine if they move.
- **17:41-18:30** [storytelling & show-don't-tell] The Jack and Jill story is told with AI images of each beat ("we see the picture of him going first", bucket pouring water), each with a "slow zoom", "still has a smooth movement". On screen 18:00, 18:30: boy and girl with buckets on a hill; leaking bucket close-up. Rule: illustrate a spoken story beat by beat with AI images, each on a slow move.
- **18:53-18:56** [zooms & camera] "Slow zoom" into the creator on the story's turn ("friend only patched one hole"). Rule: slow punch-in on the face for the emotional beat.
- **19:58-20:02** [pacing & retention] "Two things you should focus on, blurred out, to tease the viewer. We're going to reveal the answers later." On screen 19:55: two numbered purple cards with blurred labels. Rule: the promised list is shown, blurred.
- **20:06-20:14** [pacing & retention] "We're showing the screen recordings that are coming up, blurred out, coming up in the corner." On screen 20:10: "COMING UP..." with blurred slides. Rule: preview later content, blurred, in the intro.
- **20:19-20:20** [zooms & camera] "Look at all the zooms in this video. Look at the smoothness of everything."
- **20:28-20:39** [B-roll & stock] On "you won't watch another build tutorial": AI B-roll of a man watching a build video, "or you could just put the build video full screen. That's up to you." Then "darken the thing, put the text, make it more powerful". On screen 20:35: dark B-roll, "THAT ULTIMATELY LEAVES YOU **MAKING**" in red. Rule: AI B-roll or the real video both work; darken and add text for weight.
- **20:43-21:07** [sound & music] "After the hook, the song changes. After the intro, the song changes. And then for chapters, song changes." The change is "a smooth song change transition", "never while the guy's talking", "in between sentences and in between chapters". Rule: new song at hook end, intro end and every chapter, crossfaded in a gap in speech.
- **21:12-21:16** [sound & music] **Criticism**: "the music is actually a little bit too loud here", maybe "three to five percent less loud so we can hear the guy more clear". Rule: music sits clearly under the voice; trim 3-5% when in doubt.
- **21:22-21:25** [B-roll & stock] "A year ago, N8N": shows people (YouTube results) "our spelling" [Whisper-garbled; likely the n8n search results]. On screen 21:25: YouTube search page of Claude Code videos. Rule: show the trend with real search results.
- **21:39** [pacing & retention] "Visuals every few seconds." (after the hook: every few seconds, not every second).
- **21:43-21:47** [typography & text] "These are in red because it's like negative words. It's procrastination." On screen 21:40: subtitle "It's just the newest form of *procrastination*, where you get that *little dopamine hit*" with the negative words in red italics. Rule: negative keywords in red; the accent colour is for neutral/positive keywords.
- **22:05-22:14** [B-roll & stock] "B-roll that matches what he's saying plus text, triple attack": leaking roof with "ROOFS WILL **ALWAYS LEAK**" (red), clogged gutter with "GUTTERS WILL...". "The visual in the back matches what he's saying and we're adding text just to copy what he's saying." Rule: literal B-roll + centred caption of the same words.

## The rules from this video

1. **Triple attack every key line:** voice + the same words on screen + a literal visual of exactly what he says. Understand what he says and why before you build it.
2. **One focal point, always clear:** the viewer's eyes never run around the screen; "a 75-year-old" must get it.
3. **Hook density: a new visual about every second;** after the hook, every few seconds.
4. **Smooth, slow, always moving:** slow zoom in/out on every shot and card, slow directional push transitions, smooth scrolls; no jarring moves.
5. **Darken the B-roll, centre a glowing caption, colour the keyword** (accent for neutral/positive, red for negative words; real brand colours for real logos).
6. **Text pops first, then supporting visuals in their own framed boxes;** a shine sweep on the key phrase synced to the word.
7. **Screen recordings: full frame, small movable cam, then zoom + spotlight (darken the rest) + progressive unblur** as he reads each point; zoom until phone-readable. Use the same tricks on normal graphics.
8. **No more than ~30 s on an unchanging screen:** cut to full-screen face or basic B-roll; no music under long screen-recording stretches.
9. **Tease answers blurred:** promised lists, "what to focus on instead" and "coming up" previews appear blurred and are revealed later.
10. **Sound:** Adobe Podcast on the voice every video; SFX only on punchlines, with a music dip; music clearly under the voice (trim 3-5% if in doubt).
11. **Music changes after the hook, after the intro and at each chapter,** crossfaded only in a gap between sentences, never while he talks; optional tension riser into chapter one.
12. **Open on the creator's face (75% of videos) for 1-2 s with a slow zoom out;** start on a visual only when the script demands it.
13. **Every chart or diagram gets a title on top;** keep data graphics simple.
14. **Highlight the exact detail the line names** (e.g. view counts when he says views make money); if a proof clip doesn't exist, fake the text.
15. **Use colour state to carry meaning** (grey a dashboard when he says it's not real); chapter previews are numbered custom cards with title and icon.

## What this means for Samuel's Alex videos

- Triple attack, one-visual-per-second hooks, smooth slow motion and darkened-B-roll captions are channel-agnostic: apply them to Alex Vacca's videos as the baseline, whatever the brand look.
- JP's look is rounded, glowing purple cards on near-black. Alex's Frontal kit is square and blue: keep the *mechanics* (framed boxes, text-first pop, shine on the key word, numbered chapter cards, blurred teasers) and build them in Frontal's square blue language; don't import the rounded purple glow.
- Keyword colour rule maps directly: Frontal blue for neutral/positive keywords, red for negative words, real logo colours for named tools (the director says not to force the house colour onto logos).
- Screen-recording and spreadsheet/data segments in Alex's videos should get the zoom + spotlight + progressive-unblur treatment, phone-readable, with no music under long stretches and a cut away after ~30 s of a static screen.
- Audio checklist per delivery: Adobe Podcast on Alex's voice, music changed at hook/intro/chapters in speech gaps, SFX only on punchlines with a music dip, music level checked against the voice.
