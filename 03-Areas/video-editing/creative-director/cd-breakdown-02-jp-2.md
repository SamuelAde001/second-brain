# Creative director breakdown 02: JP Middleton training #2

The company's creative director (his name is not said in this recording) screen-shares three finished videos from the **JP Middleton** YouTube channel and talks over them for 12.5 min: "If You Only Watch One AI Business Video, Make It This" (0:00-6:15), "Claude + Google Maps = $30,000/Mo" (6:15-10:15) and "Another AI Goldrush Just Started (and nobody even realises)" (10:15-12:00, hook only, picked because "this video did really good"). He calls them "our video"; the editor is not named. It is in the list because he calls this "the type of editing we're looking for": every line shown, negative ideas made dark and red, light and zoom to steer the eye, nothing static. Source: `Routerise\2. I Gave Claude 10,000 SaaS Companies...\Docs\Creative director breakdowns (Editor)\transcripts\02-jp-2.txt` and frames. Whisper errors fixed from context ("an 8-N" = n8n, "clawed" = Claude, "right stottle" = Aristotle, "AIB role" = AI B-roll).

## The rules from this video

1. Every line gets the visual of exactly what is said; listen to the creator first, then build the visual.
2. Nothing is static: slow zoom on A-roll, B-roll and every held graphic ("our video is never static").
3. Negative statements: dim and desaturate the frame, gray the dismissed logo, shake, key words in red.
4. Text always sits on a darkened area or gradient so it pops; one key phrase in an accent colour.
5. Steer the eye with light: glow on the object, shine sweeps across named items, dim everything else (graphics and screen recordings alike).
6. Arrow + one-word label + zoom to make a metaphor obvious "even [to] a 75 year old viewer".
7. Real over generic: real logos, the creator's real business, real client photos, real calls, Google results with the line highlighted.
8. Tease with blur: blur the promised answer, system labels, the prize, the "coming up" preview; reveal later.
9. Money and number callouts: a box, a subline (period/source), the company logo; never a bare counter.
10. Callouts leave with a smooth eased transition off screen.
11. AI B-roll is fine, but never show the generator's watermark; it must look normal.
12. Quotes: quote marks, the person's picture, attribution.
13. Simple and exact beats complex: an icon grid split into groups, text on fitting B-roll.
14. Hooks: show the alternatives and gray them, list the chapters as boxes, more SFX, change the music when the body starts.
15. Named product or person: pop the real logo; for a person, real footage or an animated still with a treated background.

## What this means for Samuel's Alex videos

- Agrees with the Frontal kit: boxes, numbered pills, logo cards and callouts are the same building blocks; keep them square and blue instead of JP's rounded purple glow, but add the polish he praises (shine sweep, eased exit, slow push).
- Clash to watch: JP's look leans on heavy darkening and coloured glow. In the blue Frontal look, use blue for positive accents and keep red only for negatives, so "red = bad" still reads.
- Add a slow zoom to every held Frontal graphic and screen recording; nothing static.
- On Alex's screen recordings, dim the frame and light the field being used, the way JP's tutorials do.
- Where Alex names a tool, company or person, use the real logo or photo and real proof (sites, results) before reaching for generic B-roll; strip AI watermarks.
