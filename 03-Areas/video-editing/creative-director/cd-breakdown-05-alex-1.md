# Creative director breakdown 05: Alex Vacca #1 (The Most Valuable AI B2B Marketing Training)

The company's creative director (he does not say his name in this recording) screen-shares a 27-minute training on Alex Vacca's channel, Samuel's own client. He breaks down "The Most Valuable AI B2B Marketing Training" (the Frame.io file reads "FINAL 4K (2026-10-13)") from the start to about 16:10, then plays the hooks and intros of three more Alex videos by the same team: "I Tried 100+ AI Tools. These 4 Are Best for Businesses", "Taking a step back from Claude (and why you should too)" and "I cancelled Apollo.io and built this Claude skill instead". He also names "$7M Founder: How I Use Claude for Cold Outreach (B2B sales)" (the "one about LinkedIn") as worth studying. The editor is never named ("this guy", "this editor"). It is in the list because he calls it one of "our best videos" and says the edit has been "perfect" so far. ## The rules from this video

1. Show, don't tell: hear and understand Alex's point, then put the thing itself on screen (b-roll, real UI, a staged call), never text alone.
2. Real proof first (his website, campaign data, reports); when none exists, generate the b-roll with AI.
3. One big, clear element at a time, sized for a phone; dark (brand-gradient) background, bright graphic on top, darken behind every graphic.
4. Never static: slow zoom or slow pan on every shot, talking head included.
5. On documents and screen recordings: highlight the spoken line, darken the rest; spotlight, zoom in, progressively unblur.
6. Blur what pays off later (chapter cards, the mistake, the tutorial "COMING UP...") to hold retention.
7. Grade b-roll to carry meaning: grey for dead/quiet, red corners plus SFX plus zoom for bad/messy, red = negative, green = positive.
8. Pace: about one edit per second in hook and intro; every 5-10 s in the body, same style.
9. A visual stays a few seconds unless it reads in one glance (red graph down, 8 of 10 icons).
10. Music: high-energy track for hook/intro, calmer body, a new song at each chapter; never the same song twice in one video (reusing across videos is fine).
11. Stat card: big number in the accent colour + detail line + source line + logo; ratios as icons that fill.
12. Chapter cards: number without a leading zero, white, so it doesn't blend into the orange.
13. Every diagram gets a short title; dim the parts he isn't talking about.
14. Use the client's SOP colour (orange here) and tints of it; never bright on bright.
15. SFX throughout, heaviest in the hook and intro.

## What this means for Samuel's Alex videos

- This is the bar for Alex's channel itself: b-roll heavy, real proof, AI-made b-roll where proof is missing, constant slow movement, blur teases, one song per section.
- Colour clash: here the creative director says the SOP colour for this client "is orange" and praises orange-on-dark-gradient everywhere. A square, blue Frontal brand look contradicts that; check which colour the current Alex SOP names before building more blue graphics.
- Shape: the praised cards here are rounded with glow and shine; nothing in this recording asks for square boxes. Treat square as unconfirmed for Alex until the SOP or Samuel says so.
- Agrees with a brand-kit approach: he says to find the style once and "copy paste with slight changes" across videos, and the hook song may repeat across videos.
- Check every chapter-card set for leading zeros and orange-on-orange numbers, and every graphic for bright-on-bright backgrounds: those are his only two criticisms of this edit, plus one missing slow zoom.
