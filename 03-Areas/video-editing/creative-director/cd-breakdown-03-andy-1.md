# Creative director breakdown 03: Andy Stauring training #1

The company's creative director (his name is not said in this recording) reviews a top-performing video on the **Andy Stauring** channel (dropshipping / make-money-online niche, 180K subscribers on screen): **"Fastest Way to Make $20k/month After Doing it 97+ Times"** (14:01). He says it is by "one of our best editors" (no editor named), a "different style", "different niche" and "different editor" from the earlier trainings. He also opens the channel's videos page and points at the agency's other videos there ("$387/Day", "Start with $0", "$1,000,000 in 30 days", "Digital products middleman", "The new way", a vlog) as worth watching, and opens **"My Dropshipping Strategy is Lazy, But Makes $5337/Day"** as the second video, but stops at 14:47 and says he will review it in the next training. He covers about the first 3:05 of the Andy video. Its style: simpler, built on real b-roll, real screenshots and the creator's own footage, plus teal-glow custom graphics. ## The rules from this video

1. Every sentence gets a visual that says the same thing; animate processes step by step on the words, simple enough for "a 75 year old viewer".
2. Prefer real footage and real proof (the creator's b-roll, real TikTok/YouTube clips, real sites, charts, articles) over generic stock or invented visuals; research the exact clip.
3. On-screen text matches the spoken words about 97% of the time; change a word or two only when it reads better.
4. Every explainer graphic has a title at the top; viewers read it first and then understand the visual.
5. Constant slow, light movement: slow pan/zoom on b-roll and graphics, subtle motion in backgrounds, slow smooth animations, no fast or "crazy" transitions or SFX.
6. Extend a graphic by building it further (next layer, next step, swap one element) instead of cutting straight back to the creator.
7. Tease what comes later by blurring/pixelating it; reveal it on the line that names it.
8. Darken the background under text; negative words in red, keywords in the client's SOP accent colour, the rest white.
9. Shine (or bounce) each element in turn as he mentions it, and the parts of a diagram he points at.
10. Boxes are opaque; no see-through "hollow" panels.
11. Keep labels that appear together close to the centre so the eye catches them all.
12. Music: the same tracks and SFX library every video; riser into the end of the hook, song change at hook end, intro end and each new chapter.
13. Vertical phone footage is tripled across the frame so 16:9 is full.
14. Grey out (and add an SFX to) what he rejects; slide direction follows the meaning.
15. On articles: darken all but the headline, then highlight the exact words he says. Punch in with a lower-third on intimate lines.

## What this means for Samuel's Alex videos

- The bar is literal: each sentence of Alex's script needs a matching visual, and real proof (real sites, real data, real articles, real clips) beats invented graphics. Budget research time for exact clips.
- On-screen text should quote Alex's words; check every text card against the transcript.
- Agrees with a square, blue Frontal look: dark teal/blue glow panels, title-on-top, icon-line-icon diagrams, client accent colour on keywords, red for negatives all fit. Keep Frontal boxes **opaque** (no see-through glass panels) and use the Frontal blue as the keyword accent, the way Andy's videos use the client's SOP colour.
- Clash to watch: Andy's cards have rounded glowing borders; Frontal is square. The rules (title, slow push, shine per item, blur-tease, extend the graphic) carry over; the corner shape does not need to.
- Build the reusable library he asks for: one hook track, one SFX set, Fusion macros for shine, blur-tease, tripled phone b-roll and article darken-and-highlight, reused on every Alex video.
